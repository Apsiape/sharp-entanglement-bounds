\section{Directional invariance}\label{sec:direction}
%======================================================================

A one-way rate has a direction, and a game could in principle demand usable entanglement in one direction only. This
section shows that, for agreement games, it cannot: one Alice-local channel turns every near-winning strategy into a
strategy of at most three times the loss whose two directional rates are equal and no larger than either original rate.
The channel is a Petz recovery map~\cite{Petz86} in pretty-good form, its output is the reflected state of Dutta and
Faulkner~\cite{DF19}, and the
correlation maps below are KMS-adjoint correlation maps, related to the weighted correlation-operator framework of quantum
maximal correlation~\cite{Bei13}; the theorems of this section are compositions of these known objects, and a bounded search
found no preemption of them.

\subsection{A local symmetric output that keeps Bob}\label{sec:local-symmetry}

\begin{theorem}[Local symmetry with Bob kept]\label{thm:local-symmetry}
Let $\rho_{AB}$ be a normal state and let Alice apply the channel of \cref{thm:recovery}, the isometry followed by the
discard of $\bar E$. Its output $\tau_{\bar BB}$ satisfies
\[
\tau_B=\rho_B,\qquad\mathrm{Swap}\,\tau\,\mathrm{Swap}=\bar\tau .
\]
The two directional rates of $\tau$ are equal, and this common rate is at most \emph{each} directional rate of $\rho_{AB}$.
Bob's entire operator tuple and his marginal are unchanged, so every specified Bob word or relator error, state
commutator, complex phase and visibility is unchanged, including for soft tuples that satisfy no relation exactly. An
actual Alice mirror of error $e\le\sqrt2$ of a Bob generator becomes the exact transpose with error at most $\phi(e)$, and
at most $\sqrt2e$ always. Consequently, for any class of sources closed under this local output, the infimum of either
directional rate at mirror error $e$ dominates the infimum of the other at $\phi(e)$, all Bob budgets fixed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With $R=\rho_{BE}=\sum_k\lambda_k|u_k\rangle\langle u_k|$, the lifted vector is $|\sqrt R\rangle=\sum_k\sqrt{\lambda_k}|\bar u_k\rangle|u_k\rangle$,
and exchanging the copy $\bar B\bar E$ with $BE$ sends it to $\sum_k\sqrt{\lambda_k}|u_k\rangle|\bar u_k\rangle$, its complex
conjugate. Tracing out $E$ and $\bar E$ gives $\mathrm{Swap}\,\tau\,\mathrm{Swap}=\bar\tau$. Conjugating all Kraus operators of
a one-way protocol gives a protocol with the same direction and the same real Bell target, so $\ED^{A\to B}(\bar\tau)=
\ED^{A\to B}(\tau)$; exchanging the parties reverses the direction. Hence $\ED^{A\to B}(\tau)=\ED^{A\to B}(\mathrm{Swap}\,\bar\tau\,
\mathrm{Swap})=\ED^{B\to A}(\tau)$. The channel is Alice-local, so each directional rate of $\tau$ is at most the
corresponding rate of $\rho$. The mirror statement is \cref{thm:recovery}(b), and Bob is untouched.
\end{proof}

The output is exactly the reflected state of $\rho_{BE}$ in the sense of Dutta and Faulkner~\cite{DF19}: in the notation of
the proof, $\tau=\Tr_{E\bar E}|\sqrt R\rangle\langle\sqrt R|$; equivalently, if $\mathcal N$ is the channel with
$\rho_{AB}=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathcal N)(\psi_{\rho_A})$ for the real symmetric purification $\psi_{\rho_A}=\sum_i\sqrt{\lambda_i}|ii\rangle$,
then $\tau=(\bar{\mathcal N}\otimes\mathcal N)(\psi_{\rho_A})$, which makes the symmetry evident. That Alice's recovery map acts,
up to complex conjugation, as $\mathcal N$ itself is, up to conventions for transposes and the order of the factors, the
finite-dimensional case of the correspondence between the Petz recovery map and the swap transpose of quantum
couplings~\cite[Theorem~1]{BPTV25}; Parzygnat and Fullwood obtain the same identification as a quantum Bayes'
rule~\cite[(14) and (25)]{PF23}. What is used here is that this known state is reached by an Alice-local isometry, from the
original state, keeping the original Bob and the original environment; the lift is the Petz recovery map~\cite{Petz86}, in the
pretty-good form of~\cite{BK02,BCW14,IRS17,Ren17}. The output does not preserve a fixed global rank or
spectrum, a bound on Alice's dimension smaller than Bob's, or the efficiency of a decoder.

\subsection{A universal factor three for agreement games}\label{sec:factor-three}

A bipartite relation $\mathcal R$ between Alice's and Bob's answers is \emph{difunctional} if
$(a,b),\allowbreak(a',b),\allowbreak(a',b')\in\mathcal R$ implies $(a,b')\in\mathcal R$; equivalently its bipartite graph is a disjoint union of complete bipartite blocks and isolated
vertices, that is, there are label maps $l_A,l_B$ into a common set, with private labels for isolated answers, such that
acceptance is exactly $l_A(a)=l_B(b)$. This is standard relational vocabulary~\cite{Rig48,HT15}. A game is an
\emph{agreement game} if its acceptance relation on every positively weighted question pair is difunctional; the label maps
may depend on the pair. Projection games, partial projection games,
constraint-variable games and constraint-constraint overlap-agreement games (the synchronous BCS games of Kim, Paulsen and
Schafhauser~\cite[Def.~4.2]{KPS18}) are agreement games; synchronous and graph colouring predicates are not, merely by
name.

For a finite-dimensional state $\rho_{AB}$ define, on Hermitian operators on the supports of the marginals,
\[
S(X)=\rho_B^{-1/2}\Tr_A[(X\otimes I)\rho_{AB}]\rho_B^{-1/2},\qquad T(Y)=\rho_A^{-1/2}\Tr_B[(I\otimes Y)\rho_{AB}]\rho_A^{-1/2},
\]
and the real inner products $\langle X,Y\rangle_\rho=\Tr[\sqrt\rho X\sqrt\rho Y]$. We use these KMS-adjoint correlation maps, related to
the weighted correlation-operator framework of quantum maximal correlation~\cite{Bei13}, where correlations are measured with the
weighted second moments $\Tr(\rho XX^*)$ rather than with this KMS form; we do not identify the two definitions. The positivity,
adjointness and contraction needed here are proved below.

\begin{theorem}[Factor-three normal form]\label{thm:factor-three}
Let $G$ be a finite agreement game, and take an arbitrary finite-dimensional mixed strategy with legal POVMs $\{P_x^a\}$
for Alice and $\{Q_y^b\}$ for Bob and loss $\eps$. Apply the channel of \cref{thm:local-symmetry}, let Alice use the
whole-answer POVM $M_x^a=S(P_x^a)^T$ on $\bar B$, and let Bob keep his original tuple. All answers remain legal, every
original referee weight is kept, and the new loss satisfies
\[
\eps_{\mathrm{new}}\le3\eps,\qquad\eps_{\mathrm{new}}\le g(\eps):=1-(1-\eps)^3\quad(0\le\eps\le\tfrac12).
\]
There is no dependence on arity, alphabet, game size or smallest question probability. The output has the two equal
directional rates of \cref{thm:local-symmetry}, below either original rate. Consequently
\[
d_G^\to(\eps)\ge d_G^\leftarrow(3\eps),\qquad d_G^\leftarrow(\eps)\ge d_G^\to(3\eps),
\]
and the same with $g(\eps)$ in its range. This holds also at every fixed number of input copies and fixed Bell-target size,
and for one-way secret-key demand when the discarded environments go to the purifying adversary. For an ordinary parallel
product of agreement games, applying the channel once to the whole correlated strategy gives the factor three, not
$3^m$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Correlation maps.} $S$ and $T$ are positive and unital on the supports, and
$\langle S(X),Y\rangle_{\rho_B}=\Tr\rho_{AB}(X\otimes Y)=\langle X,T(Y)\rangle_{\rho_A}$, so they are adjoint in the displayed inner
products, which are positive definite on the supports. Hence $K=TS$ is self-adjoint and positive semidefinite; it is also
positive and unital, so it contracts the operator norm and its real eigenvalues have modulus at most one. Thus the
spectrum of $K$ lies in $[0,1]$ and $S$, $T$ are contractions. On a direct sum of outcome-indexed copies,
$H=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&T\\S&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ is a self-adjoint contraction. For any POVM $\{E_b\}$,
$\sum_b\|E_b\|_\rho^2\le\sum_b\Tr\rho E_b^2\le\sum_b\Tr\rho E_b=1$.

\emph{The new success.} For the channel $C$ of \cref{thm:local-symmetry}, $C^*(R^T)=T(R)$ for Hermitian $R$ on $B$: with the
Choi matrix $J_C=(\rho_A^{-T/2}\otimes I)\overline{\rho_{AB}}(\rho_A^{-T/2}\otimes I)$,
$\Tr[C(X)R^T]=\Tr[(X^T\otimes R^T)J_C]=\Tr[\rho_{AB}(\rho_A^{-1/2}X\rho_A^{-1/2}\otimes R)]=\Tr[XT(R)]$. Fix a tested pair $(x,y)$ and
coarse-grain both answers into the labels of its difunctional relation: $P_\ell=\sum_{l_A(a)=\ell}P_x^a$ and
$Q_\ell=\sum_{l_B(b)=\ell}Q_y^b$, both lists complete, private labels contributing zero on the other side. With
$p=(P_\ell)_\ell$, $q=(Q_\ell)_\ell$ and $w=(p,q)/\sqrt2$, $\|w\|\le1$, the original success is
$s=\sum_\ell\Tr\rho(P_\ell\otimes Q_\ell)=\langle w,Hw\rangle$. Since $S$ is linear, the coarse-grained new effect is exactly
$S(P_\ell)^T$, and the new success is
\[
s'=\sum_\ell\Tr\bigl[\tau(S(P_\ell)^T\otimes Q_\ell)\bigr]=\sum_\ell\langle TS(P_\ell),T(Q_\ell)\rangle_{\rho_A}=\langle w,H^3w\rangle .
\]
For $h\in[-1,1]$, $h^3-3h+2=(h-1)^2(h+2)\ge0$, so $s'\ge3s-2\|w\|^2\ge3s-2$, that is, the loss at $(x,y)$ is at most three
times the original loss. Summing with the original weights gives $\eps_{\mathrm{new}}\le3\eps$. For the refinement, form the
weighted direct sum $w=\bigoplus\sqrt{\mu_{xy}}w_{xy}$, so that the total original and new successes are $\langle w,Hw\rangle$ and
$\langle w,H^3w\rangle$; for $s\ge\frac12$, $(h-s)^2(h+2s)\ge0$ on $[-1,1]$ gives $\langle w,H^3w\rangle\ge3s^2\langle w,Hw\rangle-2s^3\|w\|^2\ge
s^3$. The new effects $S(P^a_x)^T$ are positive and sum to the identity on the support; unused support is assigned to one
legal answer.

\emph{Resources.} The resource statements follow from \cref{thm:local-symmetry}: a near-winning strategy of loss $\eps$
yields a strategy of loss at most $3\eps$ whose two directional rates are equal and below either original rate. The same
precomposition argument applies to a fixed-block one-shot Bell-extraction fidelity and to one-way secret key, since each
is monotone under deterministic local preprocessing, invariant under full conjugation of protocols, and reversed by the
swap. A conjunction of block-agreement predicates is block agreement with tuple labels, which gives the statement for
parallel products.
\end{proof}

The inequality $s'\ge s^3$ is the three-link analogue of the Barnum--Knill bound $P_{\mathrm{PGM}}\ge P_{\mathrm{opt}}^2$ for the
pretty-good measurement~\cite{BK02}. That bound is the classical-fidelity case of their main theorem, whose proof for ensembles of
more than one state was corrected in 2026 (arXiv version~2 of~\cite{BK02}); the proof of $s'\ge s^3$ above is self-contained and does
not use it. Squaring a strategy through such correlation maps has ancestry in the analysis of projection games by Dinur, Steurer
and Vidick~\cite[Lemma~6]{DSV15}. The output is generally mixed and is not a maximally
entangled or tracial strategy; this is a resource normal form, not a strategy rounding of the kind studied by Paddock and
Culf~\cite{Pad22,Cul26}. The theorem supersedes, in its guarantee, an earlier sequential decoder with a game-dependent loss
factor; on noisy mixed magic-square strategies that decoder in fact did better (loss ratio about two, against about three for
the construction here), so the factor three is a uniform guarantee, not the best decoder on every instance. There is also a dual, Bob-local
construction, with Bob effects $T(Q^b_y)^T$, which produces the identical full correlation table and can keep the smaller
local dimension; it changes Bob's tuple, so it does not inherit the literal preservation of Bob's source budgets.

\subsection{Sharpness and the exact predicate boundary}\label{sec:factor-three-sharp}

\begin{theorem}[Sharpness of the factor three]\label{thm:factor-three-sharp}
The factor three is sharp for deterministic Alice-local channels producing $\mathrm{Swap}\,\sigma\,\mathrm{Swap}=\bar\sigma$ on
$\bar B:B$, complex conjugation being taken in Bob's computational basis, with the original Bob and his measurements fixed, even allowing an alternative symmetrizing channel and
optimal legal decoding. For $N\ge2$ and $0<p<1$ take
$\rho=N^{-1}\sum_i|i\rangle\langle i|\otimes\beta_i$ with $\beta_i=p|0\rangle\langle0|+(1-p)|i\rangle\langle i|$; Alice's answers are the $N$
one-hot words, Bob is asked a uniform $j$ and tests $|j\rangle\langle j|$. The original loss is $p/N$, and the best new loss under
this contract is
\[
\frac pN\Bigl[1+2(1-p)\Bigl(1-\frac1N\Bigr)\Bigr],
\]
whose ratio to $p/N$ tends to three. At $N=3$, $p=\frac18$ the loss goes from $\frac1{24}$ to $\frac{13}{144}$, ratio $\frac{13}6$.
Difunctionality is the exact boundary for this channel-and-whole-answer guarantee: a nondifunctional relation has a
classical source with three accepted corners, of original loss zero, for which the new rejected probability is $\frac1{12}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Alice returns her label $i$; Bob sees $0$ with probability $p$, in which case the two disagree exactly at $j=i$, so the
original loss is $p/N$. Since $\rho_A=I/N$, the channel sends $|i\rangle\langle i|$ to $\beta_i$ and
$\tau=N^{-1}\sum_i\beta_i\otimes\beta_i$, which is classical. On it any POVM of Alice reduces, for this score, to a classical
decision on her label. If she sees $i\ge1$, answering $e_i$ is optimal and loses $p/N$ on that branch. If she sees $0$
(probability $p$), all one-hot answers tie and an answer $e_k$ disagrees with Bob at $j=k$ if he sees $0$, and at two
queries if he sees $i\ne k$; the conditional loss is $[p+2(1-p)(1-1/N)]/N$. Adding the branches gives the display.

Now let $\Lambda$ be any deterministic Alice-local channel with output of Bob's dimension and $\sigma=(\Lambda\otimes\mathrm{id})\rho$
satisfying the symmetry, and write $\alpha_i=\Lambda(|i\rangle\langle i|)$, so $\sigma=N^{-1}\sum_i\alpha_i\otimes\beta_i$. Bob's side is
diagonal, so the symmetry makes $\sigma$ diagonal on Alice's side too, and each $\alpha_j$, the Bob block $j\ge1$ divided by
$(1-p)/N$, is diagonal. Equality of Alice's marginal with Bob's conjugate marginal gives $\sum_j\alpha_j(i)=1-p$ for $i\ge1$,
and the symmetry of the entries $(0,i)$ and $(i,0)$ gives $(1-p)\alpha_i(0)=p\sum_j\alpha_j(i)$, so $\alpha_i(0)=p$. Hence
$\alpha_i(j)=(1-p)W_{ji}$ for a symmetric doubly stochastic $W$, and $\sigma=(\Lambda_W\otimes\mathrm{id})(\tau)$, where $\Lambda_W$ fixes
$0$ and applies $W$ to the other labels: every permitted output is a local degradation of $\tau$, and no legal decoder on
it beats the optimal decoder on $\tau$.

For the boundary, take answers $a,a'$ and $b,b'$ with $(a,b),(a',b),(a',b')$ accepted and $(a,b')$ rejected, and the
classical state with joint probabilities $P(a,b)=P(a',b)=P(a',b')=\frac13$, $P(a,b')=0$. The canonical output has table
$\tau(b,b)=\frac12$, $\tau(b,b')=\tau(b',b)=\tau(b',b')=\frac16$, and Alice's new effect for $a$ is $\mathrm{diag}(\frac12,0)$, so
the rejected pair has probability $\frac12\cdot\frac16=\frac1{12}$.
\end{proof}

The sharpness does not depend on the choice of conjugation: if $J=UK$ is any antiunitary on Bob's space with $U\bar U=\pm I$,
applied to both factors, and one requires $\mathrm{Swap}\,\sigma\,\mathrm{Swap}=(U\otimes U)\bar\sigma(U\otimes U)^*$, then every
$\alpha_i$ is diagonal in the rotated basis $\{U|m\rangle\}$ and rotating Alice's output by $U^*$ reduces to the case above; an
independent review proved this and checked it by semidefinite programming. The example is separable and its game has a
perfect classical strategy; its force is a sharp local normal form, not an entanglement bound. Sharpness is not claimed for an unrestricted rescaling of demand profiles, for operations that change
Bob, or for two-party preprocessing. With an illegal all-zero answer allowed, the ratio in the example would drop to
$2(1-p)$; the original legal alphabet is essential.

\subsection{Synchronous games, and what the directions share}\label{sec:direction-sync}

Synchronous predicates need not be difunctional off the diagonal, and the factor three need not apply to them. The
tested diagonals still give a fixed-game comparison.

\begin{proposition}[Synchronous repair]\label{prop:sync-repair}
Let $G$ be a game in class~(S). There is $C_G$ such that, for every finite-dimensional strategy of loss $\eps$, the
construction of \cref{thm:factor-three} gives a strategy of loss at most $\eps+C_G\sqrt\eps$ with equal directional rates
below either original rate. Hence $d_G^\leftarrow(\eps+C_G\sqrt\eps)\le d_G^\to(\eps)$ and symmetrically.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a diagonal question $x$ with success $s_x$, put $d_{xa}=\langle P_{xa},(I-TS)P_{xa}\rangle_{\rho_A}$ and $d_x=\sum_ad_{xa}$. By
Cauchy--Schwarz, $\sum_a\|SP_{xa}\|^2\ge(\sum_a\langle SP_{xa},Q_{xa}\rangle)^2/\sum_a\|Q_{xa}\|^2\ge s_x^2$, so
$d_x\le1-s_x^2\le2(1-s_x)\le2\eps/\mu(x,x)$. The new probability of $(a,b)$ at $(x,y)$ differs from the old by
$\langle(TS-I)P_{xa},T(Q_{yb})\rangle$, which, since $(I-TS)^2\le I-TS$, is at most $\sqrt{d_{xa}}\,\|T(Q_{yb})\|$ in modulus. Summing
over the finitely many weighted effects with Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the total change of loss by $C_G\sqrt\eps$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Directional agreement of the spectra]\label{cor:direction-spectra}
For the agreement games of \cref{thm:rational-ED,thm:lamps-ED,thm:point-window}, the reverse infima have the same
asymptotic exponents and leading logarithmic laws as the forward ones, by \cref{thm:factor-three} with a universal
rescaling of the loss. For the exact sources of \cref{sec:exact}, \cref{thm:recovery,thm:local-symmetry} and the separate
pure upper constructions give the stated orders in both physical directions. For an arbitrary game in class~(S),
\cref{prop:sync-repair} transfers a logarithmic floor between the directions but does not supply the sharp constant.
None of this asserts that the two rates of a given original state are equal.
\end{corollary}
