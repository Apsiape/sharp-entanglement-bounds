\section{Transfer and return with the state kept}\label{sec:transfer}
%======================================================================

Slofstra and Vidick showed that near-optimal strategies for a linear system game give approximate representations of
its solution group, and that the entanglement of the game is controlled by the hyperlinear profile of the central
element $J$~\cite{SV18}. For a general state they reach a representation that is accurate to order $\eps^{1/4}$: winning
with probability $1-\eps$ controls the relations, and the commutators of Bob's observables with $\rho^{1/2}$, to order
$\sqrt\eps$ in the state~\cite[Proposition~5.4]{SV18}, and the passage to a spectral projection of $\rho$ halves the
exponent again~\cite[Theorem~5.1 and Lemmas~5.5, 5.6]{SV18}. Our companion paper keeps the state in both directions for
linear system games~\cite[Theorem~D]{DM-game}: a strategy gives an assignment satisfying three conditions measured in Bob's
reduced state at precision $\sqrt\eps$, and conversely such an assignment gives a strategy of loss $O(\eps)$ on a purification
of the same state. This section states that correspondence in the form used here and proves its extension to Boolean
constraint games (\cref{thm:boolean-correspondence}), which the universal compiler of \cref{sec:compile-universal} needs, and
the comparison of whole spectra (\cref{cor:spectrum}).

\begin{definition}[Linear system games]\label{def:lsg}
Let $A\in\F_2^{m\times n}$ and $b\in\F_2^m$, let $V_i=\{j:A_{ij}=1\}$ be nonempty, and $s=\max_i|V_i|$. The \emph{solution
group} $\Gamma(A,b)$ is generated by $x_1,\dots,x_n,J$ with the relators
\begin{gather*}
x_j^2,\quad J^2,\quad[x_j,J]\ \ (1\le j\le n),\qquad[x_j,x_k]\ \ (j,k\in V_i),\\
J^{b_i}x_{j_1}x_{j_2}\cdots x_{j_r}\ \ (V_i=\{j_1<\dots<j_r\})
\end{gather*}
\cite{CM14,CLS17}. In the game $G(A,b)$ a referee draws a pair $(i,j)$ from a distribution $\pi$ on
$\{1,\dots,m\}\times\{1,\dots,n\}$. Alice receives $i$ and answers an assignment to the variables of $V_i$ that satisfies
equation $i$; Bob receives $j$ and answers one bit; they win if $j\notin V_i$ or they give $x_j$ the same value. Two
distributions are in use: $j$ uniform in $V_i$ after a uniform $i$~\cite{DM-game}, and $i$ and $j$ independent and
uniform~\cite{SV18}. Put $\pi_{\min}=\min\{\pi(i,j):j\in V_i\}$; it is at least $1/(ms)$ for the first and equals
$1/(mn)$ for the second.
\end{definition}

A strategy consists of a unit vector $\psi\in H_A\otimes H_B$, POVMs $\{P^i_a\}_a$ on $H_A$ indexed by the satisfying
assignments of equation $i$, and POVMs $\{Q^j_c\}_{c=\pm1}$ on $H_B$; we write answers as signs. Its observables are
$A_{ij}=\sum_aa_jP^i_a$ for $j\in V_i$ and $B_j=Q^j_{+1}-Q^j_{-1}$, Hermitian contractions, and the pair $(i,j)$ with
$j\in V_i$ is won with probability $\frac12(1+\langle\psi,(A_{ij}\otimes B_j)\psi\rangle)$.

\begin{definition}[State-weighted profile]\label{def:hlp-rho}
Let $\langle S\mid R\rangle$ be a finite presentation, $w\in F(S)$ and $e>0$. The \emph{state-weighted profile}
$\hlp^\rho(w;e)$ is the least $d$ for which there are a homomorphism $\varphi:F(S)\to\mathrm U(d)$ and a density matrix
$\rho$ on $\C^d$, with $\xi=\rho^{1/2}$, such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(S\arabic*),itemsep=1pt]
\item\label{S1} $\hs{(\varphi(r)-I)\xi}\le e$ for every $r\in R$;
\item\label{S2} $\hs{[\varphi(s),\xi]}\le e$ for every $s\in S$;
\item\label{S3} $\hs{(\varphi(w)+I)\xi}\le e$.
\end{enumerate}
We write $\hlp^\rho_R$ when the presentation matters.
\end{definition}

The hyperlinear profile $\hlp(w;\delta,\eps)$ of~\cite[Definition~2.3]{SV18} is the least dimension of an
$\eps$-representation, that is $\fn{\varphi(r)-I}\le\eps$ for $r\in R$, with $\fn{\varphi(w)-I}\ge\delta$. For $\delta=2$
this forces $\varphi(w)=-I$, and the flat state $\rho=I/d$ then satisfies \ref{S1}--\ref{S3} with $e=\eps$. So
$\hlp^\rho(w;e)\le\hlp(w;2,e)$: a lower bound on $\hlp^\rho$ is a stronger statement than the same lower bound on
$\hlp(\,\cdot\,;2,\cdot)$.

\begin{theorem}[Transfer; {\cite[Theorem~5.1]{DM-game}}]\label{thm:transfer}
Let $G=G(A,b)$ be a binary linear system game with question distribution $\pi$, and let a strategy win $G$ with
probability at least $1-\eps$. Let $r$ be the Schmidt rank of its state and put $\theta=2\sqrt{\eps/\pi_{\min}}$. There
are a homomorphism $\varphi:F(x_1,\dots,x_n,J)\to\mathrm U(r)$ and a density matrix $\rho$ of full rank on $\C^r$ such
that $\varphi(J)=-I$, every $\varphi(x_j)$ is an involution, and, with $\xi=\rho^{1/2}$,
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),itemsep=1pt]
\item $\hs{(\varphi(R)-I)\xi}\le\max(s,4)\,\theta$ for every relator $R$ of $\Gamma(A,b)$;
\item $\hs{[\varphi(x_j),\xi]}\le\sqrt2\,\theta$ for every $j$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular $r\ge\hlp^\rho_\Gamma(J;\max(s,4)\theta)$, and, if $\omega_q(G)=1$,
\[
\Ent(G,\eps)\ \ge\ \hlp^\rho_\Gamma\bigl(J;\ 2\max(s,4)\sqrt{\eps/\pi_{\min}}\bigr).
\]
The density matrix $\rho$ is Bob's reduced density matrix restricted to its support.
\end{theorem}

The proof~\cite[Section~5.1]{DM-game} compresses Bob's measurements to the support of his reduced state, replaces his
observables by involutions without lowering the winning probability and dilates Alice's measurements (Step~1 there); a tested
pair lost with probability $\ell_{ij}$ has $\lVert(A_{ij}\otimes I-I\otimes B_j)\psi\rVert=2\sqrt{\ell_{ij}}$ (Step~2); the
relations follow by transferring products of Bob's observables to Alice's commuting row observables (Step~3); and
\cref{lem:standard-form,lem:consistency} give the state commutators (Step~4).

\begin{remark}\label{rem:transfer}
For the flat state, (a) is~\cite[Proposition~4.4(b)]{SV18} up to constants. For a general state,~\cite[Proposition~5.4]{SV18}
proves (a) and (b) with unspecified constants, and then passes to a spectral projection of $\rho$, which gives
$\hlp(J;2,C\eps^{1/4})\le\Ent(G,\eps)$~\cite[Corollary~5.2]{SV18}; \cref{thm:transfer} stops before that step. For boolean
constraint system games,~\cite[Proposition~3.13]{FKMPRSZ25} and \cite[Lemma~2.9]{FKMPRSZ25} bound by $O(\sqrt\eps)$ the defect,
in the state of an $\eps$-perfect commuting-operator strategy, which need not be tracial, of every relation with a decomposition
of bounded size. A square root is also lost when near-optimal strategies for boolean constraint system games are rounded to
strategies on a maximally entangled state~\cite[Corollary~1.3]{Pad22}.
\end{remark}
