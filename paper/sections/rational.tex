%======================================================================
\section{Games with a dense set of coefficients}\label{sec:rational}
%======================================================================

The lamp sources and their sums realize every positive rational exponent at the source level, and more generally every number
$\sum_i\log_{m_i}q_i$ with $q_i\ge2$ a power of two dividing $m_i$. Compiled into fixed games, they give the rational family, with
minimum pure Schmidt rank $\Theta(\eps^{-\alpha})$, the whole finite-R\'enyi spectrum and pure one-shot concentration
(\cref{sec:rational-boolean,sec:rational-linear,sec:renyi}). For every coefficient $\alpha=\frac12\sum_i\log_{m_i}q_i$, rational or not, they
give games in which the one-way distillable entanglement of every mixed near-winner is at least
$\alpha\log(1/\eps)-O(\log\log(1/\eps))$, and the infimum demand has the sharp leading coefficient $\alpha$ (\cref{sec:rational-ED}). These coefficients form a dense set that contains transcendental numbers (\cref{sec:coefficient-set}), and
\cref{sec:arithmetic} bounds the arithmetic complexity of the coefficient of every fixed game.

\subsection{The Boolean constraint family}\label{sec:rational-boolean}

\begin{theorem}[Every positive rational exponent]\label{thm:rational-games}
For every positive rational $\alpha=a/b$ there is a fixed finite Boolean constraint-variable game $G_\alpha$ of quantum value
one, with every incidence positively tested and no finite-dimensional perfect strategy, such that the minimum Schmidt rank of
a pure strategy of loss at most $\eps$ is $\Theta_\alpha(\eps^{-\alpha})$, and the minimum entanglement entropy of a pure
strategy and the minimum entanglement of formation of a mixed strategy are both $\alpha\log(1/\eps)+O_\alpha(1)$. The game is
fixed before $\eps$ is chosen.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take $k=2a$ copies of the binary lamp source $L_{2,2^b}$, so that \cref{thm:additive} applies with $s=k/b=2\alpha$, and let
$G_\alpha$ be the game of the universal compiler (\cref{thm:universal}) for this presentation, with contexts of at most three
variables. A strategy of loss $\eps$ gives, by \cref{thm:boolean-correspondence}(a), a model of the constraint system at precision
$C\sqrt\eps$ on Bob's Schmidt support with Bob's reduced state, and the lower map of \cref{thm:universal} turns it into a model
of the source with the \emph{same} state at precision $C'\sqrt\eps$. By \cref{thm:additive} its rank is at least
$c\eps^{-s/2}=c\eps^{-\alpha}$ and its entropy at least $\alpha\log(1/\eps)-C$. Conversely, the flat tensor-product models at
precision $e$, lifted by the upper map with the state $I_{12}/12\otimes\rho$ and returned by
\cref{thm:boolean-correspondence}(b), give pure strategies of loss $K_3C^2e^2$, Schmidt rank $O(e^{-s})$ and entropy
$s\log(1/e)+\log12+O(1)$. Taking $e$ of order $\sqrt\eps$ proves the pure statements. For the entanglement of formation,
the pure lower bound $f(\ell)=\alpha\log(1/\ell)-C$ is convex and decreasing; it holds for every pure strategy of loss $\ell>0$
after enlarging $C$, and no pure strategy has $\ell=0$, since the source has no finite-dimensional exact model with the
marked involution $-I$. For a decomposition $\sigma=\sum_jp_j|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|$ with the same measurements the losses
average to at most $\eps$, and Jensen's inequality gives $\sum_jp_jS((\psi_j)_B)\ge f(\eps)$. The upper bound is attained by
the pure strategies above.
\end{proof}

This is a statement about a Boolean constraint game and needs no linear compiler. The mixed-state $\ED$ statement is
\cref{thm:rational-ED}; it does not follow from the bound on $\EF$.

\subsection{Linear system games}\label{sec:rational-linear}

\begin{theorem}[The one-copy linear family]\label{thm:rational-linear}
For every $q=2^r$ and every $m$ divisible by $q$ there is a fixed finite linear system game of quantum value one whose minimum
pure Schmidt rank at loss $\eps$ is $\Theta(\eps^{-\alpha})$, $\alpha=\frac12\log_mq$, and whose minimum pure entanglement
entropy and minimum mixed entanglement of formation are $\alpha\log(1/\eps)+O(1)$. In particular every positive rational
$\alpha\le\frac12$ occurs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The lamp source $L_{q,m}$ is an extended homogeneous-linear-plus-conjugacy presentation in the sense of Slofstra and
Vidick~\cite[\S6, (a)--(d)]{SV18}: its involutions are $z_0,\dots,z_{q-1},w$, the single set $V=\{z_0,\dots,z_{q-1},w\}$ carries
the commuting row $z_0\cdots z_{q-1}w=e$, the relations $yz_jy^{-1}=z_{j+1}$ and $xwx^{-1}=w$ have form~(d)(ii), and
$xyx^{-1}=y^m$ has form~(d)(iii) with $y<x$. Its marked involution $w$ is nontrivial in approximate representations, by the
Fourier-lamp models. \Cref{prop:extended}, with the $l=2$ non-involutive generators $y<x$ and $N_G=16$, therefore applies to the solution
group $\Gamma$ of the balanced construction: every strategy of loss $\eps$ has Bob's nonzero spectrum in $\Lambda_{L_{q,m}}(C_1\sqrt\eps)$,
so by \cref{thm:lamp} its rank is at least $c\eps^{-\alpha}$ and its entropy at least $\alpha\log(1/\eps)-C$; and flat models of $L_{q,m}$ of
dimension $O(e^{-2\alpha})$ give strategies of Schmidt rank $16\cdot O(e^{-2\alpha})$ and loss $O(e^2)$. The formation statement follows as in
\cref{thm:rational-games}. For $\alpha=a/b\le\frac12$ take $r=2a$ and $m=2^b$.
\end{proof}

We also use a restricted linear compiler of our own, which eliminates the non-involutive generators in order (a generator $Y$ becomes the product $fv$ of two new involutions, and a power relation $UYU^{-1}=Y^m$ the
conjugacy $UvU^{-1}=p_m$ with the involutive palindrome $p_m=v(fv)^{m-1}$), adds a parity cover with first-negative corner
selectors, which is the multi-corner map of \cref{prop:corner}, and finishes with Slofstra's gadgets. For one copy it gives
amplification $16$ and was used for the explicit exports below. The balanced construction behind \cref{prop:extended} has the same
architecture, with Slofstra's conversions, and the same amplification $2^{l+2}$ for its own games; it does not certify the
specifications below.

\begin{theorem}[The restricted compiler for several copies; conditional]\label{thm:rational-linear-k}
The restricted compiler applies to the input grammar of homogeneous commuting-involution rows, involutive conjugacies,
noninvolutive conjugation of an involution, and strictly later-on-earlier positive-power actions (including power one), with a
shared central negative involution; it does not compile arbitrary signed rows or arbitrary presentations. For the $k$-copy
sources of \cref{thm:additive}, with $2k$ ordered eliminations, the recorded maximum arity is
$\max\{3,\max_i(q_i+1),2^{2k-1}+1\}$, the amplification $2^{2k+2}$, and the entropy overhead $2k+2$; the precision constants are
computable from the finite maps, and no uniform constant for the whole family is asserted. \emph{Conditional on a
self-contained proof of the family compiler for general $k$}, the compiler itself converts these sources into fixed linear
system games with these specifications and the profiles of \cref{thm:rational-games} for every positive rational $\alpha$.
\end{theorem}

Linear system games with these profiles exist unconditionally, by the route of \cref{cor:linear-existence} below, through
\cref{prop:extended}; that route does not certify the arity, the amplification, the algorithm or the exports of the restricted
compiler in \cref{thm:rational-linear-k}, which keep their condition.

The return step must use arity-dependent constants: for a context of $k$ variables, \cref{thm:return} with $s=k$, or the
boolean return with $K_kD_k^2$, where $D_k=\max\{1,[1+k(k-1)(3k+1)/4]/2\}$ converts parity errors into forbidden-product
errors (\cref{rem:parity-forbidden}). At arity nine $K_9=138496$ and $K_9D_9^2=8829985600$; the ternary constant $111$ must
never be used there.

\begin{remark}[Exported objects; checked]\label{rem:exports}
Three finite objects were exported and checked exactly. K4 has $5206$ game variables and $4325$ parity rows, of arity three,
and L4M8 ($q=4$, $m=8$) has $6798$ game variables and $5653$ rows, of maximum arity five; both have amplification $16$, their
exponents are $\frac14$ and $\frac13$, and their solution-group presentations have one more generator, the central $J$. A
two-copy object has $199129$ game variables, again without $J$, $164608$ rows, maximum arity nine, amplification $64$ and
exponent one. The script \texttt{verify/check\_linear\_games.py} rebuilds K4 and L4M8 from their sources and checks exactly, in
the negative source algebra, every square, parity row and row commutator and the restriction of each conjugated source word to
its generator on its block. The exported L4M8 and two-copy games are archived in \texttt{verify/artifacts}: the archived L4M8
file is checked exactly against its source presentation, every square, row and row commutator, with the normal-form engine of
\texttt{check\_linear\_games.py}, and the two-copy file by its own exact checker; complete Fourier-lamp strategies were played on every
context of L4M8 at four sizes, with observed rank exponents $0.318$, $0.331$, $0.333$, and on every context and raw outcome of the
two-copy game at two sizes.
This is evidence for these finite objects, not a derivation of every $k$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Several copies are an extended presentation]\label{rem:SV-route-k}
The source $L_{\mathbf q,\mathbf m}$ of \cref{thm:additive} is an extended homogeneous-linear-plus-conjugacy
presentation~\cite[\S6, (a)--(d)]{SV18}, after a Tietze transformation, with the noninvolutive generators ordered
$y_1,\dots,y_k,x_1,\dots,x_k$. Its involutions are the lamps of all copies and the shared $w$; the rows $z^{(i)}_0\cdots
z^{(i)}_{q_i-1}w=e$, one set of the collection in~(c) for each copy, carry the within-copy commutations; and within a copy the remaining
relations have forms (d)(ii) and (d)(iii) as in the proof of \cref{thm:rational-linear}, $x_iy_ix_i^{-1}=y_i^{m_i}$ being a
later-on-earlier positive power. The cross-copy commutators are of three kinds. Two involutions $u,v$ commute exactly when $uvu=v$,
an allowed involutive conjugacy (d)(i); a two-element row $uv=e$ of form~(c) would instead impose $u=v$. A noninvolutive generator
$g$ commutes with an involution $u$ through the allowed action $gug^{-1}=u$ of form (d)(ii). Two noninvolutive generators commute
through the later-on-earlier power-one relation $g_ig_jg_i^{-1}=g_j^1$, $j<i$, of form (d)(iii). The shared central $w$ stays one
involution, and tensor products of the negative Fourier-lamp models respect that identification. \Cref{prop:extended}, with $l=2k$
non-involutive generators and its multi-corner lower map, and \cref{thm:additive} therefore give an unconditional linear realization
of every positive rational exponent, with flat amplification $N_G=2^{2k+2}$ and fixed computable presentation-dependent precision
constants. Nothing here uses that $m_i$ is a power of two, which is how \cref{thm:rational-ED} obtains linear games for every
coefficient of \cref{cor:coefficient-set}.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Linear games with every positive rational exponent]\label{cor:linear-existence}
For every positive rational $\alpha=a/b$ there is a fixed finite linear system game $\widetilde G_\alpha$ of quantum value one, with every
incidence positively tested and no finite-dimensional perfect strategy, such that the minimum Schmidt rank of a pure strategy of
loss at most $\eps$ is $\Theta_\alpha(\eps^{-\alpha})$, the minimum entanglement entropy of a pure strategy and the minimum entanglement of
formation of a mixed strategy are $\alpha\log(1/\eps)+O_\alpha(1)$, the pure-state statements of \cref{thm:renyi} hold for
$\widetilde G_\alpha$, and there is $\eps_\alpha>0$ such that, separately in both directions, for $0<\eps<\eps_\alpha$,
\[
d^\to_{\widetilde G_\alpha}(\eps)=\alpha\log(1/\eps)+O_\alpha\bigl(\log\log(1/\eps)\bigr),\qquad
d^\leftarrow_{\widetilde G_\alpha}(\eps)=\alpha\log(1/\eps)+O_\alpha\bigl(\log\log(1/\eps)\bigr).
\]
This is not an equality of the two functions at each loss. The corollary concerns existence only: it does not certify the
specifications of \cref{thm:rational-linear-k}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $k=2a$ binary copies, $q_i=2$ and $m_i=2^b$, so that $s=k/b=2\alpha$ in \cref{thm:additive}, and let $L=L_{\mathbf q,\mathbf m}$ with
marked involution $w$. By \cref{rem:SV-route-k}, $L$ is an extended homogeneous-linear-plus-conjugacy presentation after a
Tietze transformation, which changes precisions by constant factors and keeps the state (\cref{prop:algebraic-map}). The
involution $w$ is central, and it is nontrivial in approximate representations, since tensor products of the Fourier-lamp
models of \cref{ex:fourier-lamps} have $W=-I$ at every precision. So the balanced construction before \cref{prop:extended}, with
$l=2k=4a$ non-involutive generators, gives a binary linear system $(A,b)$, and \cref{prop:extended} applies to $\widetilde G_\alpha=G(A,b)$
with either question distribution of \cref{def:lsg} and $N_G=2^{4a+2}$; both test every incidence.

\emph{Pure strategies.} By \cref{prop:extended}(a) Bob's nonzero spectrum lies in $\Lambda_L(C_1\sqrt\eps)$, so \cref{thm:additive} gives
Schmidt rank at least $c\eps^{-\alpha}$ and entropy at least $\alpha\log(1/\eps)-C$. The flat tensor models of \cref{thm:additive}, of
dimension $O(e^{-s})$ with $W=-I$, give $\hlp_L(w;2,e)=O(e^{-s})$, and \cref{prop:extended}(b) turns them into strategies on maximally
entangled states of Schmidt rank $O(\eps^{-\alpha})$ and entropy $\alpha\log(1/\eps)+O(1)$. No finite-dimensional strategy is perfect,
since \cref{thm:additive} excludes exact finite models with $W=-I$; the formation statement follows as in \cref{thm:rational-games}.
The proof of \cref{thm:renyi} uses only the energy functional of the source on Bob's original state, which the transfer of
\cref{prop:extended}(a) supplies at precision $O(\sqrt\eps)$, these upper strategies, and a product strategy of positive loss; it
applies verbatim.

\emph{Mixed strategies.} The lower map of \cref{prop:extended} is the corner map of \cref{prop:corner} with $2^{4a-1}$ corners, a
unital $*$-homomorphism given by fixed polynomials in the variables, the letter $J$ evaluating to $-1$. So \cref{prop:interface}, in the
form noted after it for linear system games and corner maps, supplies the actual source interface on the original mixed state,
and the usable-entanglement law in both directions is the case $k=2a$, $q_i=2$, $m_i=2^b$ of \cref{thm:rational-ED} below, for the
game $\widetilde G_\alpha$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The R\'enyi spectrum and pure concentration}\label{sec:renyi}

For a density matrix $\rho$ and $1<\beta<\infty$ write $H_\beta(\rho)=\frac1{1-\beta}\log\Tr\rho^\beta$, $H_\infty(\rho)=-\log\opn\rho$, and
$H_{\min}^\delta(\rho)=\sup\{-\log\opn\sigma:P(\rho,\sigma)\le\delta\}$ with the purified distance $P=\sqrt{1-F^2}$ between normalized
states.

\begin{theorem}[R\'enyi spectrum]\label{thm:renyi}
Fix a rational $\alpha>0$ and the game $G_\alpha$ of \cref{thm:rational-games}. Over pure strategies of loss at most $\eps$, with
$\rho=\rho_B$,
\begin{gather*}
\inf H_\beta(\rho)=\alpha\log(1/\eps)+O(1)\qquad(0\le\beta\le1),\\
\inf H_\beta(\rho)=\min\Bigl\{\alpha,\frac\beta{\beta-1}\Bigr\}\log(1/\eps)+O_\beta(1)\qquad(1<\beta<\infty),
\end{gather*}
the finite critical order included; the constants are not uniform as $\beta\to\infty$. For $H_\infty$ the coefficient is
$\min(\alpha,1)$ with $O(1)$ remainder when $\alpha\ne1$; at $\alpha=1$,
\[
\log(1/\eps)-\log(1+\log(1/\eps))-O(1)\ \le\ \inf H_\infty(\rho)\ \le\ \log(1/\eps)+O(1).
\]
For fixed $0<\delta<1$, $\inf H_{\min}^\delta(\rho)=\alpha\log(1/\eps)+O_\delta(1)$. For every fixed $0<\eta<1$, every pure strategy of
loss at most $\eps$ admits a state-dependent heralded LOCC protocol that succeeds with probability at least $1-\eta$ and then
outputs an exact $\Phi_M$ with $\log M\ge\alpha\log(1/\eps)-O_\eta(1)$; this uses the Schmidt basis of the state and is not the
protocol of \cref{thm:protocol}. The same holds for the linear games of \cref{thm:rational-linear} and of
\cref{cor:linear-existence}.
\end{theorem}

The proof turns the rank profile into a functional inequality. Fix the source unitaries of an arbitrary assignment and define,
for positive $A$,
\[
E(A)^2=\sum_R\hs{(R-I)A}^2+\sum_V\hs{[V,A]}^2+\hs{(W+I)A}^2,
\]
summing over the relators $R$, the generators $V$ and the marked involution $W$. A model on $\xi=\rho^{1/2}$ at precision $e$ has
$E(\xi)\le Ce$, and the game transfer keeps $\rho$ and gives $E(\xi)\le C\sqrt\eps$.

\begin{lemma}[Faber--Krahn and layers]\label{lem:faber-krahn}
Let $s=2\alpha$. There is $c>0$ with $E(A)^2\ge c\,\rank(A)^{-2/s}\hs A^2$ for every positive $A$ and every assignment. With
$f_l(t)=\min((t-2^l)_+,2^l)$ for $l\in\Z$, $\sum_lE(f_l(A))^2\le E(A)^2$, and $E((A-tI)_+)\le E(A)$ for $t\ge0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:additive} to the density $A^2/\hs A^2$ with the same unitaries, at precision $E(A)/\hs A$; lowering $c$
covers large precisions, and $E(A)=0$ is impossible for $A\ne0$ since the rank bound would then hold at every precision. For
the layers, $\sum_lf_l(t)^2\le t^2$ and $\sum_l|f_l(t)-f_l(u)|^2\le(t-u)^2$, the $f_l(t)$ being the lengths of the disjoint
intervals $[2^l,2^{l+1}]\cap[0,t]$. So $\sum_l\hs{Lf_l(A)}^2=\Tr(L^*L\sum_lf_l(A)^2)\le\hs{LA}^2$, and in an eigenbasis of $A$,
$\sum_l\hs{[V,f_l(A)]}^2=\sum_{i,j}|V_{ij}|^2\sum_l|f_l(a_j)-f_l(a_i)|^2\le\hs{[V,A]}^2$. The cutoff $(t-u)_+$ is $1$-Lipschitz and
vanishes at zero, which gives the last claim in the same way.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:renyi}]
\emph{Schatten bounds.} First let $s>2$, $\vartheta=2/s$ and $p=2s/(s-2)$. With $r_l=\rank f_l(A)$ and $b_l=2^{pl}r_l$, the lemma gives
$E(f_l(A))^2\ge c2^{2l}r_{l+1}r_l^{-\vartheta}=c2^{-p}b_{l+1}b_l^{-\vartheta}$, since $2-p+p\vartheta=0$; with $T=\sum_lb_l$ and $b_l\le T$,
$\sum_lb_{l+1}b_l^{-\vartheta}\ge T^{1-\vartheta}$, and dyadic layer cake gives $T\asymp\Tr A^p$. Hence $\|A\|_p\le C\,E(A)$, the strong
endpoint, without a logarithm. For any $s>0$ and $p>2$ with $t=2p/(p-2)>s$, add the mass term $\lambda\hs B^2$ to the energy;
since $c\,\rank^{-2/s}+\lambda\ge c^a\lambda^{1-a}\rank^{-2/t}$ with $a=s/t$, the endpoint with parameter $t$ and the choice
$\lambda=E(A)^2/\hs A^2$ give $\|A\|_p\le C\,E(A)^a\hs A^{1-a}$. Above the endpoint use monotonicity of Schatten norms. With
$A=\xi$, $\hs A=1$ and $E(A)\le e$, this gives $\Tr\rho^\beta\le Ce^{\min(s(\beta-1),2\beta)}$, that is
$H_\beta(\rho)\ge\min(s,2\beta/(\beta-1))\log(1/e)-C$, and with $e\asymp\sqrt\eps$ the lower bounds for $1<\beta<\infty$. For
$0\le\beta\le1$, $H_\beta\ge H_1$, and \cref{thm:rational-games} gives the coefficient $\alpha$.

\emph{Min-entropy.} If $s>2$, $\opn A\le\|A\|_p\le Ce$, so $\opn\rho\le Ce^2$. If $s<2$, normalize $\hs A=1$ and let
$a_1\ge a_2\ge\cdots$ be the eigenvalues; $B=(A-a_{2r})_+$ has rank below $2r$, $\hs B\ge\sqrt r(a_r-a_{2r})$ and $E(B)\le e$, so
$a_r-a_{2r}\le Cer^{1/s-1/2}$; telescoping over dyadic $r$ up to $R\asymp e^{-s}$ and $a_R\le R^{-1/2}$ give $a_1\le Ce^{s/2}$, that
is $\opn\rho\le Ce^s$. If $s=2$, normalize $\hs A=1$, let $M=\opn A$, and suppose $M\ge E(A)$; otherwise $M^2<E(A)^2$ and there is
nothing to prove. Put $K_0=\lceil\log(4/M^2)\rceil\ge2$, $\Delta=M/(4K_0)$, $t_j=M/2+j\Delta$, $h_j=\rank(A>t_j)$ and
$F_j=\min\{(A-t_j)_+,\Delta\}$ for $0\le j<K_0$. Then $h_0\le\hs A^2/(M/2)^2=4/M^2$ and $h_{K_0}\ge1$, since $t_{K_0}=3M/4<M$. The bands
$[t_j,t_j+\Delta]$ are disjoint, so, as for the layers of \cref{lem:faber-krahn}, $\sum_jE(F_j)^2\le E(A)^2$; and $F_j=\Delta$ on the
eigenvalues above $t_{j+1}$, so $\hs{F_j}^2\ge\Delta^2h_{j+1}$, while $\rank F_j=h_j$. The $s=2$ inequality
$E(F)^2\ge c\,\rank(F)^{-1}\hs F^2$ and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality give
\[
E(A)^2\ge c\Delta^2\sum_{j<K_0}\frac{h_{j+1}}{h_j}\ge c\Delta^2K_0\Bigl(\frac{h_{K_0}}{h_0}\Bigr)^{1/K_0}\ge\frac{cM^2}{16K_0}\Bigl(\frac{M^2}4\Bigr)^{1/K_0}
\ge\frac{cM^2}{32K_0},
\]
and $K_0=O(1+\log(1/E(A)))$ because $M\ge E(A)$. So $\opn\rho=M^2\le CE(A)^2[1+\log(1/E(A))]$, that is $\opn\rho\le Ce^2(1+\log(1/e))$.
With $e\asymp\sqrt\eps$ these are the lower bounds for $H_\infty$; at $s=2$, substituting $e=C_G\sqrt\eps$ gives
$H_\infty(\rho)\ge\log(1/\eps)-\log(1+\log(1/\eps))-O(1)$, a single logarithmic-logarithmic loss. The $O(1)$ endpoint remainder is open,
and the constants are not uniform in the R\'enyi order.

\emph{Upper bounds.} Take a pure strategy of loss at most $\eps/2$ and Schmidt rank $D\le C\eps^{-\alpha}$, and a deterministic
product strategy of loss $\ell_{\mathrm{bad}}>0$; such a positive loss exists because there is no perfect finite strategy. With
$\delta=\eps/(2\ell_{\mathrm{bad}})$ and orthogonal local flags on both sides, $\sqrt{1-\delta}|0,0\rangle|\psi_{\mathrm{good}}\rangle+\sqrt\delta|1,1\rangle|a\rangle|b\rangle$
is a pure strategy of loss at most $\eps$ whose squared Schmidt coefficients are $(1-\delta)\lambda_j$ and $\delta$. So
$\Tr\rho^\beta\ge(1-\delta)^\beta D^{1-\beta}+\delta^\beta\ge c(\eps^{\alpha(\beta-1)}+\eps^\beta)$ and $\opn\rho\ge\max((1-\delta)/D,\delta)$, which
give the upper bounds.

\emph{Smoothing and concentration.} Choose $\beta>1$ with $\alpha\le\beta/(\beta-1)$, so $\Tr\rho^\beta\le C\eps^{\alpha(\beta-1)}$. For
$\eta\in(0,1)$ let $t=(C/\eta)^{1/(\beta-1)}\eps^\alpha$ and $Q$ the spectral projection of $\rho$ onto eigenvalues at most $t$. Then
$\mu=\Tr(I-Q)\rho\le t^{1-\beta}\Tr\rho^\beta\le\eta$, and $\sigma=Q\rho Q/(1-\mu)$ has $F(\rho,\sigma)=\sqrt{1-\mu}$ and
$\opn\sigma\le t/(1-\eta)$; with $\eta=\delta^2$ this is the lower bound for $H^\delta_{\min}$. For the upper bound, a strategy of rank
$D$ and any $\sigma$ with $P(\rho,\sigma)\le\delta$ have $\Tr(\Pi\sigma)\ge1-\delta^2$ on the support $\Pi$, so $\opn\sigma\ge(1-\delta^2)/D$. For
concentration, project locally onto $Q$ in the Schmidt basis, which succeeds with probability $1-\mu\ge1-\eta$, and convert the
resulting pure state, whose largest Schmidt weight is at most $1/M$ with $M=\lfloor(1-\eta)/t\rfloor$, to $\Phi_M$ deterministically by
Nielsen's theorem~\cite{Nie99}. Schmidt rank cannot increase in a pure branch, so $M\le D$ for the upper scale.
\end{proof}

The weight in the largest $r$ Schmidt coefficients is controlled as well: by H\"older, $L_r\le C(r\eps^\alpha)^{(\beta-1)/\beta}$,
so for every $r=o(\eps^{-\alpha})$ it tends to zero uniformly over near-winning pure strategies. The functional-inequality
framework is that of Bakry, Coulhon, Ledoux and Saloff-Coste and of Rumin~\cite{BCLS95,Rum10}; one-shot concentration is
due to Vidal, Lo--Popescu and Buscemi--Datta~\cite{Vid99,LP01,BD10}. A large rank alone is not a large entanglement:
it could sit in a negligible tail, and the theorem excludes that at the certified scale. For the source of
\cref{thm:shared}, $s=1$ and the same argument gives $\opn\rho\le Ce$, so every R\'enyi entropy, $H_\infty$ included, has
coefficient one there.

\subsection{Usable entanglement with the sharp coefficient}\label{sec:rational-ED}

The coefficients of the lamp sums need not be rational. For $k\ge1$ and $i=1,\dots,k$ fix integers $r_i\ge1$ and $m_i\ge2$ with
$2^{r_i}\mid m_i$, put $q_i=2^{r_i}$, let $L_{\mathbf q,\mathbf m}$ be the source of \cref{thm:additive}, and put
\[
\alpha=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^k\log_{m_i}q_i=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^k\frac{r_i}{\log m_i}.
\]
Let $G_{\mathbf q,\mathbf m}$ be the Boolean constraint game of \cref{thm:universal} for $L_{\mathbf q,\mathbf m}$, and $\widetilde G_{\mathbf q,\mathbf m}$
the linear system game that \cref{rem:SV-route-k} and \cref{prop:extended} give for it, with either question distribution of
\cref{def:lsg}; the remark uses only the form of the relators, not the values of $q_i$ and $m_i$. For $\alpha=a/b$, the choice of $k=2a$ copies of $L_{2,2^b}$ gives the games $G_\alpha$ and
$\widetilde G_\alpha$ of \cref{thm:rational-games,cor:linear-existence}, and one copy gives the games of \cref{thm:rational-linear}.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp coefficients on the original state]\label{thm:rational-ED}
Let $G$ be $G_{\mathbf q,\mathbf m}$ or $\widetilde G_{\mathbf q,\mathbf m}$. Then $G$ has quantum value one, every incidence positively tested
and no finite-dimensional perfect strategy, and there are constants $\eps_G,C_G>0$ such that, for $0<\eps<\eps_G$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),itemsep=1pt]
\item the minimum Schmidt rank of a pure strategy of loss at most $\eps$ is $\Theta_G(\eps^{-\alpha})$, and the minimum entanglement
entropy of a pure strategy and the minimum entanglement of formation of a mixed strategy are $\alpha\log(1/\eps)+O_G(1)$;
\item every finite-dimensional mixed strategy of loss at most $\eps$ satisfies, separately in both directions,
\[
\min\bigl\{\ED^{A\to B}(\rho_{AB}),\ED^{B\to A}(\rho_{AB})\bigr\}\ \ge\ \alpha\log(1/\eps)-O_G\bigl(\log\log(1/\eps)\bigr),
\]
and there are pure maximally entangled strategies of loss at most $\eps$ whose two directional rates are $\alpha\log(1/\eps)+O_G(1)$;
so $d^\to_G(\eps)$ and $d^\leftarrow_G(\eps)$ are both $\alpha\log(1/\eps)+O_G(\log\log(1/\eps))$, and an $O(1)$ lower remainder is not
proved;
\item explicitly, with $e=C_G\sqrt\eps$, $x=\log(1/e)$ and
\[
L_i=\Bigl\lfloor\frac{x-32\log(2+x)}{\log m_i}\Bigr\rfloor,\qquad D=\prod_iq_i^{L_i},
\]
one has $L_i\ge1$, and every finite-dimensional mixed strategy of loss at most $\eps$ admits strategy-dependent product local
isometries extracting $\Phi_D$ with vector error at most $C'_G(2+x)^{-3}$, the environment included in the junk.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The restriction $\eps<\eps_G$ ensures that the depths $L_i$ are positive, that the error bounds of the proof are in range, and that
$\log\log(1/\eps)$ is defined. For rational $\alpha$ the theorem contains the usable-entanglement laws of the games $G_\alpha$ and
$\widetilde G_\alpha$ of \cref{thm:rational-games,cor:linear-existence}, and it adds that law to the one-copy games of
\cref{thm:rational-linear}.

\begin{proof}
\emph{The game and the pure and formation laws.} These are proved as in \cref{thm:rational-games,cor:linear-existence}, with
\cref{thm:additive} for $s=2\alpha$ in place of the rational choice. For $G_{\mathbf q,\mathbf m}$, \cref{thm:boolean-correspondence}(a) and the
lower map of \cref{thm:universal} give a model of $L_{\mathbf q,\mathbf m}$ with Bob's reduced state at precision $C\sqrt\eps$; for
$\widetilde G_{\mathbf q,\mathbf m}$, \cref{prop:extended}(a) puts Bob's nonzero spectrum in $\Lambda_{L_{\mathbf q,\mathbf m}}(C\sqrt\eps)$. In both cases
\cref{thm:additive} gives Schmidt rank at least $c\eps^{-\alpha}$ and entropy at least $\alpha\log(1/\eps)-C$. The flat tensor-product
Fourier-lamp models at precision $c\sqrt\eps$, lifted by the upper map of \cref{thm:universal} with amplification $12$ and returned by
\cref{thm:boolean-correspondence}(b), or turned into strategies by \cref{prop:extended}(b) with amplification $N_G=2^{2k+2}$, give strategies of loss
at most $\eps$ on maximally entangled states of Schmidt rank $O(\eps^{-\alpha})$ and entropy $\alpha\log(1/\eps)+O(1)$, whose two directional
rates equal their entropy~\cite{BBPS96}. They show that $G$ has quantum value one, and no finite-dimensional strategy is perfect, since
\cref{thm:additive} excludes exact finite models with $W=-I$. The formation statement follows by Jensen's inequality as in
\cref{thm:rational-games}. Both games test every incidence.

\emph{The source on the actual state.} Fix a strategy of loss at most $\eps$, dilate it locally, and let $\Psi$ purify the dilated state.
\Cref{prop:interface}, applied to the lower map of \cref{thm:universal}, or, for $\widetilde G_{\mathbf q,\mathbf m}$, to the corner map in the form
noted after it, gives for every copy $i$ Bob unitaries $X_i,Y_i$ and Alice unitaries $X_i^A,Y_i^A$ such that
$X_iY_iX_i^*=Y_i^{m_i}$ on Bob and $X_i^{A*}Y_i^AX_i^A=(Y_i^A)^{m_i}$ on Alice hold with residual at most $e=C_G\sqrt\eps$, all cross-copy
generator commutators have residual at most $e$, and all mirrors, inverse mirrors and square-root-state commutators are at most $e$; this
holds on the whole original state. For the rotations apply the paragraph on selector polynomials after \cref{prop:interface} to the selector
$T_i$ of \cref{lem:rotation-selector} for copy $i$: in the negative quotient $T_i^*T_i=T_iT_i^*=1$ and $T_iy_i=\zeta_iy_iT_i$,
$\zeta_i=e^{2\pi i/q_i}$, and $T_i$ commutes with every generator of another copy, being a polynomial in the lamps of copy $i$. This gives
Bob unitaries $U_i$ and Alice unitaries $U_i^A$, with mirrors, inverse mirrors and square-root-state commutators $O(e)$,
\[
\|(U_iY_iU_i^*-\zeta_iY_i)\Psi\|=O(e),\qquad\|(U_i^AY_i^AU_i^{A*}-\zeta_i^{-1}Y_i^A)\Psi\|=O(e),
\]
the inverse phase on Alice because she realizes the opposite algebra, and commutators $O(e)$ of $U_i$ and of $U_i^A$ with the generators
of the other copies on the same party. No sector is selected, and the $U_i$ need not have finite order. We enlarge $C_G$ to absorb the
constants. Choose $\eps_G$ so small that $L_i\ge1$ and that the error bounds $\eta$, $\delta$ and $\gamma$ below lie in the ranges in
which they are used, in particular $\eta\le1$ and $\gamma^2<\frac12$ in~\eqref{eq:singlet-hashing}; this is possible because each of
them tends to zero with $\eps$.

\emph{Powers without an exponential square.} For opposite-party unitaries, $\|(R_A^n-S_B^n)\Psi\|\le n\|(R_A-S_B)\Psi\|$
(\cref{lem:transfer-parties}(c)); this is not asserted for two operators of one party. On each party and for each copy, with $S=X_i$ on
Bob or $S=X_i^{A*}$ on Alice and $Y$ the $Y_i$ of that party, put $F_d=S^dYS^{-d}-Y^{m^d}$, $m=m_i$. Then
\[
F_{d+1}=SF_dS^{-1}+\bigl((SYS^{-1})^{m^d}-(Y^m)^{m^d}\bigr).
\]
Moving the one-letter suffix $S^{-1}$ of the first term to its actual mirror costs $2e$. For the second term compare $SYS^{-1}$ with the
opposite-party mirror of $Y^m$, at cost $(m+1)e$, raise this opposite-party comparison to the power $m^d$, and compare the mirror's power
$m^{d+1}$ back with $Y^{m^{d+1}}$ on the same party, at cost $m^{d+1}e$. So $\|F_{d+1}\Psi\|\le\|F_d\Psi\|+2e+(2m+1)m^de$, and
$\|F_d\Psi\|\le C_mm^de$; no term $m^{2d}e$ appears. In the same way, raising the rotation relation across the parties,
$\|(U_iY_i^nU_i^*-\zeta_i^nY_i^n)\Psi\|\le C_Gne$ for $n\ge1$, with $\zeta_i^{-n}$ on Alice.

\emph{Common roots and controls.} For copy $i$ put $N_i=L_i-1$ and, on Bob,
\[
Y_{i,j}=X_i^{-j}Y_iX_i^j,\qquad V_{i,j}=X_i^{-j}U_iX_i^j,\qquad R_{i,j}=Y_{i,N_i}^{m_i^{N_i-j}}\qquad(0\le j\le N_i),
\]
with the reversed words on Alice, for example $Y^A_{i,j}=(X_i^A)^jY_i^A(X_i^A)^{-j}$; put $N=\max_iN_i$ and $M=\sum_iL_i$. All $R_{i,j}$ of
one party and copy are powers of one unitary and commute exactly. Conjugating $F_d$ by a word of length $O(N+1)$,
$\|(Y_{i,j}-Y_{i,l}^{m_i^{l-j}})\Psi\|\le C_G(m_i^{l-j}+N+1)e$ for $j\le l$, and in particular
\[
\|(R_{i,j}-Y_{i,j})\Psi\|\le C_G(m_i^{N_i-j}+N+1)e
\]
on each party, likewise for adjoints. With the mirror of $Y_{i,j}$, which costs $O((N+1)e)$ by \cref{lem:transfer-parties}(a), this gives
$\|(R^A_{i,j}-R^B_{i,j})\Psi\|\le C_G(m_i^{N_i-j}+N+1)e$, and \cref{lem:transfer-parties}(d) gives the square-root-state commutators. For
$j\le l$, compare $R_{i,j}$ with $Y_{i,l}^{n}$, $n=m_i^{l-j}$, and conjugate the rotation estimate for $Y_i^n$ by the native word
$X_i^{-l}$, moving its suffix through actual mirrors:
\[
\|(V_{i,l}R_{i,j}V_{i,l}^*-\zeta_i^{n}R_{i,j})\Psi\|\le C_G(m_i^{L_i}+N+1)e .
\]
Since $q_i$ divides $m_i$, $\zeta_i^n=1$ for $j<l$, and $\zeta_i^n=\zeta_i$ for $j=l$: the control of stage $(i,l)$ fixes the earlier roots
of its copy and rotates its own by $\frac1{q_i}$ of a turn, by $-\frac1{q_i}$ on Alice. The same holds for the powers $V_{i,l}^h$,
$|h|\le Q=\max_iq_i$, at cost $C_Q$ times larger. Words of different copies have length $O(N+1)$ and commute after $O((N+1)^2)$ swaps of
generators or controls, each with a suffix of length $O(N+1)$, so their commutators on $\Psi$ are $O((N+1)^3e)$. Replacing a native
$Y_{i,j}$ by $R_{i,j}$ costs the estimate above; when the replaced factor stands to the left of an operator of the other copy, that
operator is first moved to the other party through its actual mirror, so the two large powers are never multiplied. Hence every mirror,
inverse mirror, square-root-state commutator, triangular rotation and cross-copy commutator among the $R$, the $V$ and their powers
$|h|\le Q$ has residual at most
\[
\eta=C_G\Bigl(e\max_im_i^{L_i}+e(N+1)^3\Bigr),
\]
on both parties. No relation between different controls is used, and no term $e\prod_im_i^{L_i}$ appears.

\emph{Spectral cuts.} Order the stages $(i,j)$ copy by copy and with increasing $j$ within a copy, $M$ in all. For each stage choose an
offset $\vartheta_{i,j}$ uniform modulo $1/q_i$, independent over the stages and the same on both parties, and let $P^P_{i,j,a}$,
$a\in\Z_{q_i}$, be the spectral projections of $R^P_{i,j}$ onto the arcs of \cref{lem:random-cuts}. Since multiplication by $\zeta_i$ moves
each arc to the next, the control $V^B_{i,j}$ approximately sends the label $a$ to $a-1$ and $V^A_{i,j}$ sends $a$ to $a+1$, which is the
orientation of \cref{lem:cyclic-triangular} on Alice. Within a copy the partitions commute exactly. For the mirrors, let $\nu$ be the
joint spectral measure of the commuting unitaries $R^A,R^B$ of two parties in $\Psi$; an offset uniform modulo $1/q$ separates two points at
distance $d\le\frac12$, in turns, with probability at most $qd$, and $d\le\frac14|z-w|$, so, as in \cref{lem:cut-parties},
\[
\mathbb E\sum_a\|(P_a(R^A)-P_a(R^B))\Psi\|^2\le2q\int d\,d\nu\le\frac q2\|(R^A-R^B)\Psi\| .
\]
On Alice, with $\xi=\rho_A^{1/2}$ and vector norms on $\Psi$ equal to Hilbert--Schmidt norms (\cref{lem:standard-form}),
\cref{lem:random-cuts} bounds the squared expectations of the covariance defects and of the commutators of controls with earlier
partitions by $O(\eta)$, the state commutators entering through \cref{lem:transfer-parties}(d). Its covariance estimate is stated for
the rotation $\zeta$; for a power $V^h$ the same bound follows from its first estimate with $R=V^hZV^{-h}$ and $S=\zeta^hZ$, since
multiplication by $\zeta^h$ shifts the arcs by $h$, converted into the covariance defect at the cost of the state commutators, as in its
proof. For two partitions of different copies, the third estimate
of \cref{lem:random-cuts} bounds the first moment of the commutator aggregate by $O(\eta^{1/4})$. As $\eta\le1$, every residual of
\cref{lem:cyclic-triangular} has first moment at most $C_G\eta^{1/4}$. There are at most $C_QM^2$ of them, counting every bounded power,
both mirrors and every earlier stage; summing their first moments before fixing the offsets, some deterministic choice of offsets has
every residual at most
\[
\delta\le C_GM^2\eta^{1/4}.
\]
For a single copy no two partitions of different copies occur, every residual has squared expectation $O(\eta)$, and summing the squares
instead gives $\delta\le C_GM\eta^{1/2}$.

\emph{Extraction.} \Cref{lem:cyclic-triangular}, with these partitions and the controls $V^P_{i,j}$ in the order of the stages, gives
product local isometries with vector error $\gamma\le C_QM^3\delta\le C_GM^5\eta^{1/4}$ from $\Phi_D\otimes\chi$; for a single copy,
$\gamma\le C_GM^4\eta^{1/2}$. With $L_i$ as displayed, $m_i^{L_i}\le2^x(2+x)^{-32}$, so $em_i^{L_i}\le(2+x)^{-32}$; and $N+1\le x+1$,
$M\le kx$ and $e(N+1)^3\le2^{-x}(x+1)^3\le C(2+x)^{-32}$. So $\eta\le C_G(2+x)^{-32}$ and $\gamma\le C_Gx^5(2+x)^{-8}\le C'_G(2+x)^{-3}$,
which is~(c).

\emph{Hashing.} Discard the junk. The output vector $v$ is a unit vector within $\gamma$ of $\Phi_D\otimes\chi$, so the projection onto
$\Phi_D$ times the junk misses at most $\gamma^2$ of it, and the $D$-dimensional output has singlet fraction at least $1-\gamma^2$.
By~\eqref{eq:singlet-hashing}, in either direction,
\[
\ED(\rho_{AB})\ \ge\ (1-2\gamma^2)\log D-h(\gamma^2)\ =\ \log D-o(1),
\]
since $\gamma^2\log D\le C(2+x)^{-6}x\to0$; all preprocessing was local on $AB$ and nothing acted on $E$, so this bounds each directional
rate of the original state. Finally $\log D=\sum_ir_iL_i\ge2\alpha x-64\alpha\log(2+x)-\sum_ir_i$ and $x=\frac12\log(1/\eps)-\log C_G$, which
gives~(b); the upper bound is given by the pure strategies above.
\end{proof}

The entropy and formation bounds of~(a) are not substitutes for this proof, which never uses a decomposition of the state, a canonical
purification, or an exact joint spectral law for different copies. The cuts use only the first-moment estimate of \cref{lem:random-cuts}
for two partitions, which costs the margin $32$ in $L_i$.

\begin{remark}[The numbers without the cyclic lemma]\label{rem:binary-route}
For the coefficients alone, \cref{lem:cyclic-triangular} is not needed. A copy of $L_{2^r,m}$ can be replaced by $r$ commuting copies of
$L_{2,m}$, which keeps the source exponent $r/\log m$ but changes the source and the game; with $q_i=2$ throughout, the lamp $z_0$ of each
copy, which satisfies $z_0yz_0=wy$, is a reflection control with $TYT\approx-Y$, and \cref{thm:triangular} suffices. So every coefficient of
\cref{cor:coefficient-set} below is realized by binary copies $L_{2,m}$ with even $m$. The cyclic lemma is needed for the original
$q$-lamp games with $q\ge4$, in particular for the one-copy games of \cref{thm:rational-linear} with $r\ge2$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The set of coefficients}\label{sec:coefficient-set}

Let $\mathcal S$ be the set of the coefficients $\alpha$ of \cref{thm:rational-ED}, over all finite families $(q_i,m_i)$ with $q_i=2^{r_i}$,
$r_i\ge1$ and $q_i\mid m_i$. Logarithms are to base two.

\begin{corollary}[A dense set of coefficients]\label{cor:coefficient-set}
\[
\mathcal S=\Bigl\{\frac12\sum_{m\ \mathrm{even}}\frac{n_m}{\log m}:\ n_m\in\Z_{\ge0},\ \text{finitely many }n_m\ne0,\ \text{not all }n_m=0\Bigr\}.
\]
This set is countable, closed under addition and under multiplication by positive rationals, and consists of computable reals. It contains
every positive rational number and the set $\{a/(2b\log6):a,b\ge1\}$, which is dense in $(0,\infty)$ and consists of transcendental
numbers; for example $\frac12\log_62=1/(2\log6)$. For every $\alpha\in\mathcal S$ there are a fixed Boolean constraint game and a fixed linear
system game with the laws of \cref{thm:rational-ED} for $\alpha$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A generator $\frac12\log_mq$ with $q=2^r$ dividing $m$ has $m$ even and equals $r\cdot\frac1{2\log m}$, a sum of $r$ binary generators;
conversely every $\frac1{2\log m}$ with $m$ even is a generator with $q=2$. This proves the description, and closure under addition is
clear. For a positive rational $a/b$, $\frac ab\cdot\frac1{2\log m}=\frac a{2\log(m^b)}$ with $m^b$ even, which gives closure under positive
rational multiples; with $m=2$ it gives every positive rational. Integer logarithms are uniformly computable, so every element of $\mathcal S$
is computable, and $\mathcal S$ is countable. The numbers $a/(2b\log6)$ are realized by $a$ binary copies with $m=6^b$, and they are dense
since $a/b$ ranges over the positive rationals. Finally $\log6$ is irrational, since $2^u=6^v$ has no solution in positive integers; if it
were algebraic, the Gelfond--Schneider theorem~\cite{Gel34,Sch35} would make $2^{\log6}=6$ transcendental. So $\log6$ is transcendental, and
so is every nonzero rational multiple of its reciprocal. The last assertion is \cref{thm:rational-ED}.
\end{proof}

One copy already gives a dense family. For $m=3\cdot2^b$ and $q=2^r$ with $1\le r\le b$, the one-copy coefficient is
$\alpha_{b,r}=r/(2(b+\log3))$, which is transcendental by the same argument applied to $\log3$. For $0<t<\frac12$ and
$r_b=\lfloor2t(b+\log3)\rfloor$, one has $1\le r_b\le b$ for all large $b$ and $0\le t-\alpha_{b,r_b}<1/(2(b+\log3))$. So the one-copy linear
games of \cref{thm:rational-linear}, with \cref{thm:rational-ED}, have sharp usable-entanglement coefficients dense in $(0,\frac12)$.

We make no transcendence claim for general elements of $\mathcal S$, and the description gives no test of membership for a number
presented in another form. Sums are realized by commuting copies of sources, not by an additivity theorem for one-way distillable
entanglement; products and limits of elements of $\mathcal S$ are not realized, and a sequence of different games is not one fixed game.
The transcendence step is classical, and irrational dimension exponents of this kind are already in \cref{thm:rational-linear}; what is
added here is the sharp original-state usable-entanglement law for them.

\subsection{Which coefficients can occur}\label{sec:arithmetic}

In this subsection a game is a finite game with finite question and answer sets, a winning predicate and a question distribution. Let
$D_G(\eps)$ be the least $d$ such that some strategy on $\C^d\otimes\C^d$ has loss at most $\eps$. Let $\mathcal A_{\log}$ be the set of finite
$c\ge0$ for which some game of quantum value one has $\lim_{\eps\downarrow0}d^\to_G(\eps)/\log(1/\eps)=c$, and $\mathcal A_{\dim}$ the same with
$\log D_G(\eps)$ in place of $d^\to_G(\eps)$. A real is \emph{computable relative to} an oracle $X$ if it has a Cauchy name computable from
$X$, and \emph{left-c.e.}\ (\emph{right-c.e.}) relative to $X$ if the rationals below (above) it form a set that is computably enumerable
relative to $X$. We write $0^{(n)}$ for the $n$-th Turing jump, and call the reals computable relative to $0^{(n-1)}$ the $\Delta^0_n$ reals.

\emph{Input convention.} A game is \emph{effectively algebraic} if each question probability $\pi(x)$ is a real algebraic number and is
given by a polynomial $p_x$ with integer coefficients together with a rational isolating interval $(a_x,b_x)$, in which $\pi(x)$ is the only
real root of $p_x$; any equivalent effective way of selecting a real root would serve as well. The algorithms of
\cref{prop:arithmetic-dim,thm:arithmetic} take a game in this form.

\begin{lemma}[Reweighting]\label{lem:reweighting}
Two games with the same questions, answers and predicate, whose question distributions have the same support, have the same logarithmic
coefficients, dimension exponents and power-law exponents, whenever these exist.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The losses are $\ell_G=\sum_x\pi_G(x)\ell_x$ and $\ell_H=\sum_x\pi_H(x)\ell_x$ with the same conditional losses $\ell_x\ge0$, so $a\ell_H\le\ell_G\le
b\ell_H$ with $a=\min_x\pi_G(x)/\pi_H(x)>0$ and $b=\max_x\pi_G(x)/\pi_H(x)$ over the common support. Hence
$d_H^\to(\eps/a)\le d_G^\to(\eps)\le d_H^\to(\eps/b)$, and likewise for $D$. A fixed rescaling of $\eps$ changes $\log(1/\eps)$ by a constant
and leaves every limit of the three kinds unchanged.
\end{proof}

The same reweighting comparison holds for the pure original-state demand $p_G$: $p_H(\eps/a)\le p_G(\eps)\le p_H(\eps/b)$. The conditional losses are unchanged on restricting to pure strategies, so the logarithmic coefficient of $p_G$ is unchanged as well. This is the pure-state use of \cref{lem:reweighting} below.

Replacing positive real weights by the uniform distribution on their support, the sets $\mathcal A_{\log}$ and $\mathcal A_{\dim}$ are countable.
By \cref{thm:rational-ED}, and with zero from a game that has a perfect product strategy, $\{0\}\cup\mathcal S$ lies in both.

\begin{proposition}[Dimension exponents]\label{prop:arithmetic-dim}
For an effectively algebraic game of quantum value one, given in the input convention above, $D_G(\delta)$ is computable from $G$ and every
rational $\delta>0$, and it equals the least Schmidt rank of a pure strategy of loss at most $\delta$. Consequently every element of
$\mathcal A_{\dim}$ is the limit of a computable sequence of rationals, hence, by Shoenfield's limit lemma~\cite{Sho59}, computable relative to
$0'$: a $\Delta^0_2$ real.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $d$, the existence of a unit vector in $\C^d\otimes\C^d$ and of POVMs on $\C^d$ for the finitely many questions with loss at most
$\delta$ is an existential sentence over the real closed field of real algebraic numbers, after splitting complex coordinates into real
and imaginary parts and expressing positivity by principal minors. Each question probability enters as a variable $\pi_x$ constrained by
$p_x(\pi_x)=0$ and $a_x<\pi_x<b_x$, which by the input convention determines it, so the sentence has integer coefficients, and quantifier
elimination decides it~\cite{Sei54,CM12}. A mixed state does not do better: for fixed measurements the winning probability is linear in the
state, so an eigenvector of the game operator does at least as well, and a pure strategy compresses to its Schmidt supports. Searching
$d=1,2,\dots$ terminates because the value is one. Approximate $\log D_G(2^{-n})/n$ by a rational $r_n$ to error at most $2^{-n}$; if the
exponent exists, the computable sequence $(r_n)$ converges to it. Shoenfield's limit lemma~\cite{Sho59} says, relative to any oracle $X$,
that a function is computable from $X'$ exactly when it is the limit of a function computable from $X$; we use it with $X$ computable, in
the form for reals: the limit of a computable sequence of rationals is computable relative to $0'$. That direction is also immediate here,
since $0'$ decides, for given $k$ and $n$, the $\Pi^0_1$ statement that $|r_m-r_{m'}|\le2^{-k}$ for all $m,m'\ge n$, and so finds a Cauchy
name. By \cref{lem:reweighting} every element of $\mathcal A_{\dim}$ is the exponent of an effectively algebraic game.
\end{proof}

No computable rate of convergence is asserted, and the value-one promise is assumed, not decided. Nothing in this proof computes a
distillable entanglement, which needs a separate argument.

\begin{theorem}[One-way coefficients]\label{thm:arithmetic}
Let $G$ be an effectively algebraic game of quantum value one, given in the input convention above. For rational $\delta>0$,
$d^\to_G(\delta)$ is right-c.e.\ relative to $0'$,
uniformly in $\delta$. If $c=\lim_{\eps\downarrow0}d^\to_G(\eps)/\log(1/\eps)$ exists and is finite, then $c$ is left-c.e.\ relative to $0''$,
that is, the rationals below $c$ form a $\Sigma^0_3$ set; in particular $c$ is computable relative to $0'''$, a $\Delta^0_4$ real. The same holds
for $d^\leftarrow_G$, and for the exponent $\beta$ of a power law $d^\to_G(\eps)=\eps^{-\beta+o(1)}$. By \cref{lem:reweighting}, every element of
$\mathcal A_{\log}$ is left-c.e.\ relative to $0''$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{The formula.} By the theorem of Devetak and Winter~\cite[Theorem~13 of the arXiv version]{DW05},
$\ED^{A\to B}(\rho)=\lim_n\frac1nD^{(1)}(\rho^{\otimes n})$, where, for a state $\sigma$ on $\C^{d_A}\otimes\C^{d_B}$, $D^{(1)}(\sigma)$ is the
maximum over instruments of Alice of the average coherent information $\sum_\ell p_\ell I_c(A\rangle B)_{\sigma_\ell}$, and the instruments may
be taken with at most $d_A^2$ outcomes and one Kraus operator each. With unnormalized branches
$\sigma_\ell=(A_\ell\otimes I)\sigma(A_\ell\otimes I)^*$ and $H(C)=-\Tr C\log C$ for positive $C$,
\[
D^{(1)}(\sigma)=\max\Bigl\{\sum_\ell\bigl[H(\Tr_A\sigma_\ell)-H(\sigma_\ell)\bigr]:\ \sum_\ell A_\ell^*A_\ell=I,\ \ell\le d_A^2\Bigr\},
\]
the terms $p_\ell\log p_\ell$ of the two entropies cancelling. Product instruments make $D^{(1)}$ superadditive under tensor products, so
the limit is a supremum, and $D^{(1)}\ge0$, since a rank-one measurement on Alice leaves product branches. So
$\ED^{A\to B}=\sup_Nf_N$ with $f_N(\rho)=\max_{n\le N}\frac1nD^{(1)}(\rho^{\otimes n})$, an increasing sequence of continuous functions.

\emph{Computability of $f_N$.} The objective is uniformly continuous on its compact domain with a computable modulus: the eigenvalues of a
Hermitian matrix are $1$-Lipschitz in the operator norm, and $t\mapsto-t\log t$ is uniformly continuous on $[0,1]$ with an explicit modulus.
The domain of the instruments, and the set $K_d$ below, are compact sets defined by polynomial equations and inequalities; with the question
probabilities entered as constrained variables, as in the proof of \cref{prop:arithmetic-dim}, the defining formulas have integer
coefficients. Quantifier elimination decides for every rational box whether it meets such a set, so nested boxes give, for
every point of a rational grid that lies near the set, a computable point of the set near it; evaluating a function with a computable
modulus at these finitely many points gives its maximum or minimum over the set to any prescribed precision. So $f_N$ is computable,
uniformly in $N$ and in the dimensions.

\emph{Fixed dimension.} Let $K_d$ be the set of strategies with a state on $\C^d\otimes\C^d$, POVMs on $\C^d$ and loss at most $\delta$.
Whether it is empty is decidable, and padding shows that it is nonempty for all $d\ge d_0$, where $d_0$ is the least $d$ with
$K_d\ne\emptyset$, which exists because the value is one and is found by search. Put $a_{d,N}=\min_{K_d}f_N$, computable uniformly in $d,N$, and
$b_d=\min_{K_d}\ED^{A\to B}$, a minimum of a lower semicontinuous function on a compact set. Then $b_d=\sup_Na_{d,N}$: clearly
$a_{d,N}\le b_d$, and if $t<b_d$, the compact sets $\{z\in K_d:f_N(z)\le t\}$ decrease in $N$ and have empty intersection, so one of them is
empty and $a_{d,N}>t$. So $b_d$ is left-c.e., uniformly in $d$, and as $0\le b_d\le\log d$ the oracle $0'$, which decides whether $b_d>r$
for each rational $r$, computes it.

\emph{The demand.} Every finite-dimensional strategy fits, after padding, in some $K_d$ with $d\ge d_0$, so
$d^\to_G(\delta)=\inf_{d\ge d_0}b_d$. Enumerating rational upper bounds for all $b_d$ with $0'$ shows that it is right-c.e.\ relative to $0'$,
uniformly in $\delta$.

\emph{The coefficient.} Put $c_m=\inf_{n\ge m}d^\to_G(2^{-n})/n$; these are right-c.e.\ relative to $0'$, uniformly in $m$. If $c$ exists, it is
the limit along $\eps=2^{-n}$, and then $c=\liminf_n d^\to_G(2^{-n})/n=\sup_mc_m$. A rational $r$ is below $c$ exactly when there are $m$ and a
rational $r'>r$ with $c_m\ge r'$; the condition $c_m\ge r'$ is the complement of a set c.e.\ relative to $0'$, so it is $\Pi^0_2$, and the set
of such $r$ is $\Sigma^0_3$, that is, c.e.\ relative to $0''$. The oracle $0'''$ decides membership in it and so computes $c$. For a power law,
$\beta=\lim_n\log(1+d^\to_G(2^{-n}))/n$, and since $t\mapsto\log(1+t)/n$ is increasing and computable the same argument applies. For
$d^\leftarrow_G$ exchange the parties.
\end{proof}

Thus $\{0\}\cup\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal A_{\log}$, and the matched laws of~\cite[Theorem~16.6 and Corollary~16.7]{DM-U3} give the stronger inclusion
$\Delta^0_2\cap(0,\infty)\subseteq\mathcal A_{\log}$. Every element of $\mathcal A_{\log}$ is left-c.e.\ relative to $0''$.
These two bounds do not classify the full usable spectrum. Dimension and one-way demand differ here, through the regularization, the minimization over strategies and the unbounded
local dimension. The promise itself has a known place in the arithmetical hierarchy: Mousavi, Nezhadi and Yuen show that deciding
whether a game has quantum value one is $\Pi^0_2$-complete~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{MNY22}. Here value one is assumed, not decided, and the
objects we place in the hierarchy are the dimension exponents and one-way coefficients of games that have it. A zero coefficient
for a game of Theorem~A without a finite-dimensional perfect strategy would be a counterexample to the floor of \cref{prob:floor}.
