\section{Obstructions and refutations}\label{sec:obstructions}
%======================================================================

This section records what does \emph{not} hold, or what a proof cannot do, with proofs. Several entries refute statements we
once expected; they are kept because they mark the boundary of the method.

\subsection{Positivity under partial transposition is not distillability}\label{sec:ppt}

\begin{theorem}[A PPT gap]\label{thm:ppt-gap}
Let a finite binary linear system with nonempty rows be classically inconsistent, let every incidence be tested with
probability at least $p_{\min}>0$, and choose a parity-contradiction certificate, a set of rows in which every variable occurs an
even number of times and whose signs multiply to $-1$, with total number $N$ of variable occurrences. Every finite-dimensional
strategy on a state with positive partial transpose has loss at least $4p_{\min}/(N^3+3N^2+N)^4$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Relabelling illegal answers and local Naimark dilations with pure ancillas preserve PPT and do not increase the loss. Let
$A_{ij}$ be Alice's commuting row reflections, $\prod_{j\in V_i}A_{ij}=b_iI$, and $B_j$ Bob's, so $\|(A_{ij}-B_j)\sqrt\rho\|_2\le d=2\sqrt{\eps/p_{\min}}$,
with $\eps$ the loss and the norm taken with the joint density. Choose one row reflection $A_j$ per variable. If
$\sigma=\rho^{T_B}\ge0$, then $B_j^T$ mirrors $A_j$ in $\sigma$ with the same error, since $\Tr\sigma(A_j\otimes I-I\otimes B_j^T)^2=
2-2\Tr\rho(A_j\otimes B_j)$. For $W=A_jA_k\otimes B_jB_k$, $|\Tr\rho W-1|\le2d$; $\Tr\rho W=\Tr\sigma(A_jA_k\otimes B_k^TB_j^T)$, and transferring
the two Bob factors to Alice in $\sigma$ costs $2d$ and gives $A_jA_kA_jA_k$, whose expectation is the same in $\rho$ and $\sigma$. So
$\Re\Tr\rho A_jA_kA_jA_k\ge1-4d$ and $\|[A_j,A_k]\sqrt\rho\|_2^2\le8d$. For a certificate row $i$ with representative word $R_i$,
replacing its letters by the row reflections costs at most $r_i(r_i+1)d$, so $\|(R_i-b_i)\sqrt\rho\|_2\le r_i(r_i+1)d$; telescoping
against scalars, the ordered product $P$ of the certificate words has $\|(P+I)\sqrt\rho\|_2\le(N^2+N)d$. Every letter of $P$ occurs
an even number of times; sorting with at most $N^2/2$ adjacent interchanges, each costing at most $\sqrt{8d}+2Nd$ with its suffix
moved to Bob, gives $\|(P-I)\sqrt\rho\|_2\le\sqrt2N^2\sqrt d+N^3d$. Hence $2\le(N^3+N^2+N)d+\sqrt2N^2\sqrt d$, which for $d\le1$ is
at most $(N^3+3N^2+N)\sqrt d$, and the bound follows; if $d>1$ the loss exceeds $p_{\min}/4$.
\end{proof}

Sufficiently good near-winners of such a game are therefore entangled with negative partial transpose. This alone proves
neither distillability nor any growing rate, and it does not bypass the NPT distillability problem; PPT relaxations of
device-independent entanglement constraints are due to Moroder et al.~\cite{MBLHG13}. The operational theorems of
\cref{sec:extract,sec:law} are separate proofs.

\subsection{No exact output from full-rank mixed states}\label{sec:no-exact}

\begin{remark}[No exact entangled output; standard]\label{thm:no-exact}
If $\rho_{AB}$ is a full-rank finite-dimensional state, no nonzero stochastic separable operation on finitely many copies, and
hence no branch of an LOCC protocol, outputs an exact entangled pure state with nonzero probability. Every near-winning strategy
can be made full rank by an arbitrarily small depolarization at an arbitrarily small increase of loss.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
If $\rho\ge\lambda I$ with $\lambda>0$ and $\Lambda(\rho)=p|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|$ for a separable completely positive $\Lambda$, then
$0\le\lambda\Lambda(I)\le p|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|$; a positive separable operator supported on an entangled line is zero, so $\Lambda(I)=0$
and $\Lambda=0$. The same applies to $\rho^{\otimes n}$. For the second claim mix a strategy of loss $\eps/2$ with weight $t\le\eps/2$ of
the maximally mixed state.
\end{proof}

So the exact-output statements for pure strategies (\cref{thm:renyi}) and exactly synchronous strategies (\cref{thm:exact-sync})
cannot be extended to arbitrary mixed near-winners; approximate extraction is the theorem. This is standard finite-copy logic,
not a new no-distillation result.

\subsection{Restricted universal linear compilers}\label{sec:no-linear}

\begin{proposition}[Two exclusions]\label{prop:no-linear}
\emph{(a)} There is no split finite linear envelope ($\sigma F=I_N\otimes\mathrm{id}$, with $\sigma$ into $M_N$ of the negative quotient)
in which every source generator is recovered as a fixed scalar times \emph{one} group word in the involutive constraint
generators. \emph{(b)} A finite group $K$ with central involution $J$ whose negative group algebra is a single matrix algebra $M_d$ has
negative character values in $\{0,\pm d\}$, so it cannot contain a coordinate transposition of four sectors, of trace two, as a
group-word anchor.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) Take the source $\Z\times C_2$ with the negative central involution and its characters $u\mapsto z$, $z\in\Tor$. Composing $\sigma$
with a character gives an $N$-dimensional representation of the game algebra in which every involutive generator has determinant
$\pm1$, so a fixed scalar $\lambda$ times a fixed word has determinant $\pm\lambda^N$; but $\sigma F(u)=I_N\otimes u$ has determinant $z^N$.
So $z^N\in\{\pm\lambda^N\}$ for every $z$, which is impossible. (b) The tensor cube of the unique negative irreducible representation
is again negative, so $\chi^3=d^2\chi$ pointwise.
\end{proof}

A tested polynomial \emph{minority-frame} decoder, which recovers each source generator in the corner selecting the minority
coordinate of four commuting involutions with product $-1$, also fails: tensoring three lifted models of the source
$\langle u,v,q,w\mid uvq^{-1},w^2,[w,\cdot]\rangle$ gives an exact $128$-dimensional model of every frame relation in which the decoded
relator $uvq^{-1}$ equals $-I_{32}$ on one sector. This refutes that candidate at zero model error, not arbitrary polynomial
decoders. \Cref{thm:universal} produces Boolean constraint games, and the restricted grammar of \cref{thm:rational-linear-k} survives; neither establishes
nor rules out an unrestricted linear compiler (\cref{sec:open}).

\subsection{Raw pair bounds below the exponent three halves}\label{sec:raw-pairs}

\begin{proposition}[Raw pairs]\label{prop:raw-pairs}
For every $M\ge\lceil384\pi\rceil$ there is a native model $\mathcal N_M$ of \cref{sec:point-native}, namely the model
of~\cite[Proposition~6.5]{DM-H3} with the spanning trees chosen in the proof, such that on its flat maximally entangled
purification, with all actual word mirrors exact and source squared error at most $14.26M^{-3}$, the raw lamp commutator satisfies
$\|[x,a^{4M}za^{-4M}]-I\|_2^2\ge1/(128\pi)$ in the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm. Consequently no estimate
$\|([x,a^nza^{-n}]-I)\Psi\|\le Cn^ue$ with $u<\frac32$ holds uniformly over models and distances; flag dilution, weight $p$ on such a
block and $1-p$ on a more accurate one, keeps this failure at an arbitrarily small critical budget $N^3e^2\le C'p$, with ratio to
$N^{2u}e^2$ growing like $M^{3-2u}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{The model.} We use the data of~\cite[Section~6]{DM-H3} in its conventions: $\ell=2M+2$, the reduced colour coordinates
$1,\dots,L$ with $L=4M+4$, clocks $(i,j)\in(\Z/M\Z)^2$, the reverse cycles $c_r=(1\ r\ r{-}1\ \cdots\ 2)$, the base $a$-links
$A_{i,j}=c_{\ell-i-j}$, the base $b$-links, and the two exact moves $\eta_1$, on the $a$-link at $(M-1,M-1)$, and $\eta_2$, on a $b$-link.
The plaquette $(i,j)$ has bottom and top edges the $a$-links at $(i,j)$ and $(i,j+1)$, and left and right edges the $b$-links at
$(i,j)$ and $(i+1,j)$. The regions are those of~\cite[proof of Proposition~6.5]{DM-H3}: for $5\le t\le M+3$ the bulk region $R_t$, with
source $(M-1,M-1)$, consists of its source, the column plaquettes $(M-1,j)$ with $1\le j\le M-2$, and the staircase plaquettes $(i,j)$
with $0\le i\le M-3$, $0\le j\le M-2$, $i+j\le t+M-8$; the region $T$, with source $(0,M-2)$, consists of its source and the plaquettes
with $i+j\le M-3$; signs and labels are as given there. These regions satisfy (R1)--(R4) of~\cite[Lemma~6.4]{DM-H3}, every label at the
initial vertex of an interior edge has both coordinates at least $5$~\cite[proof of Proposition~6.5]{DM-H3}, and
$|R_t|\ge M(M-1)/2$, $|T|=1+(M-1)(M-2)/2$. Lemma~6.4 of~\cite{DM-H3} solves $\mathrm{curl}\,\varphi_R=F_R-s_R\pi\delta_{q_0}$, for the
constant fields $F_{R,q}=s_R\pi/|R|$, along an \emph{arbitrary} spanning tree of the plaquettes of $R$ joined through interior edges,
rooted at the source, with zero potential off the tree. The resulting H4 holonomies depend only on the fields, so the bound
$\|\mathrm{H4}-I\|_2^2\le14.26M^{-3}$ of~\cite[Proposition~6.5]{DM-H3} holds for every tree; and the rotations act in the label planes,
which avoid the coordinates $1,\dots,4$, so the other Houghton relators~\cite[Lemma~6.4]{DM-H3} and the lamp relators
(\cref{sec:point-native}) stay exact whatever tree is used. On the flat maximally entangled purification with Alice's transposes
every mirror is exact and every state commutator vanishes, so the model has precision $e_M\le\sqrt{14.26}\,M^{-3/2}$.

\emph{Row-spine trees.} For $R_t$: on each row $1\le j\le M-2$ join the staircase plaquettes $(0,j),(1,j),\dots$ consecutively
through their common $b$-links, and join $(0,j)$ to the column plaquette $(M-1,j)$ through the $b$-link at $(0,j)$, across the seam;
join the column plaquettes consecutively through the $a$-links at $(M-1,j)$, $2\le j\le M-2$, and $(M-1,M-2)$ to the source through
its bottom edge. Row~$0$ cannot cross the seam, since $(M-1,0)\notin R_t$: join its plaquettes to $(0,0)$ through $b$-links, and $(0,0)$
to $(0,1)$ through the $a$-link at $(0,1)$. For $T$: join each row to its plaquette $(0,j)$ through $b$-links, and the plaquettes
$(0,0),\dots,(0,M-3)$ and the source $(0,M-2)$ through the $a$-links at $(0,j)$, $1\le j\le M-2$. These edges are interior, the trees
are spanning and acyclic, and each source is a leaf attached by its bottom edge, as (R3) requires.

\emph{Angles.} If a tree edge $e$ separates a subtree $S$ not containing the source from the rest, summing the curl equation over
$S$, in which every edge inside $S$ cancels and every other edge but $e$ carries zero potential, gives $|\varphi_R(e)|=\pi|S|/|R|$.
Below the spine edge at $(M-1,j)$, $j\ge2$, lie the column plaquettes $(M-1,1),\dots,(M-1,j-1)$ and the staircase rows $0,\dots,j-1$, at
most $j(M-1)$ plaquettes, so $|\varphi_{R_t}|\le2\pi j/M$; below the $T$-spine edge at $(0,j)$ lie at most $j(M-2)$ plaquettes, so
$|\varphi_T|\le2\pi j/(M-1)$; the edge at $(0,1)$ of $R_t$ carries an angle at most $2\pi/M$. On a row $j\ge2$ the only $a$-links with
nonzero potentials are thus those at $(M-1,j)$, carrying the rotations of the bulk regions, and at $(0,j)$, carrying that of $T$. The
bulk rotations act in disjoint planes, so the first link factor is within $\max_t|\varphi_{R_t}|\le2\pi j/M$ of $I$ in operator norm,
not within the sum, and the second within $2\pi j/(M-1)$. Neither $\eta$-move lies on an $a$-link of a row $2\le j\le M-2$.

\emph{The unperturbed circuit.} In the representation of~\cite[Section~6.2]{DM-H3} the generator $a$ is the adjoint of the
right-action operator, so $a$ moves the clock by $-e_1$ and applies the inverse of the link at the new clock to the one-particle
space $\C^L$. From the clock $(0,j)$ the $M$-step circuit along row $j$ therefore applies, in chronological order, the forward cycles
$d_r=c_r^{-1}=(1\ 2\ \cdots\ r)$ of lengths $r=d+1,d+2,\dots,R$, where $d=M+2-j$ and $R=M+d$. Following the points,
\[
P(v)=\begin{cases}v+M,&1\le v\le d,\\ R-v+1,&d<v\le R,\\ v,&v>R:\end{cases}
\]
for $v\le d$ every cycle increments $v$; for $d<v\le R$ the cycles shorter than $v$ fix it, the cycle of length $v$ sends it to $1$, and
the remaining $R-v$ cycles increment it. For $j\ge3$, $d<M$, so $P$ exchanges the low block $[1,d]$ with the high block $[M+1,R]$, $P^2$
reflects each of them, and $P$ reflects the middle block $[d+1,M]$; hence $P^4=I$. A circuit from another clock of the row applies a
cyclic rotation of the same factors, which is conjugate to $P$ and has fourth power $I$. The orientation matters: the same cycles
applied in the opposite chronological order, or their inverses in the same order, do not in general give an element of order
four. The order used here is that of the single unitary $a$; its inverse gives the inverse circuits, with the same property.

\emph{The perturbed circuit.} The one-particle holonomy $W_{i,j}(a^{4M})$ of $a^{4M}$ from a clock $(i,j)$ of row $j$ uses every link of the
row four times, and the rotated links differ from the base links only in the columns $M-1$ and $0$. Telescoping against the
unperturbed product, which is $I$, gives
\[
\opn{W_{i,j}(a^{4M})-I}\le4\Bigl[\frac{2\pi j}M+\frac{2\pi j}{M-1}\Bigr]\le\frac{24\pi j}M ,
\]
uniformly in $i$. For $3\le j\le\lfloor M/(64\pi)\rfloor$ this is at most $\frac38$, so $|W_{i,j}(a^{4M})_{1,1}|\ge\frac12$; for
$M\ge\lceil384\pi\rceil$ these rows number at least $M/(128\pi)$, so they carry a fraction at least $1/(128\pi)$ of the clocks. This is a
one-particle estimate; it is not claimed that fermionic quantization keeps operator norms small on the whole space.

\emph{A Gaussian identity.} The lamps are $x=i\gamma_{1,1}\gamma_{1,2}$ and $z=i\gamma_{1,2}\gamma_{1,3}$, where $\gamma_{1,1},\gamma_{1,2}$ are the
Majorana operators of the first flavour at the coordinate $1$ and $\gamma_{1,3}$ one of the second flavour there. At each clock
$a^nza^{-n}$ is the conjugate of $z$ by the fermionic quantization of the one-particle holonomy $W$ of $a^n$ there, or of its inverse,
which has the same diagonal moduli. This conjugation sends $\gamma_{1,2}$ to a unit real combination $A$ of first-flavour Majorana
operators and $\gamma_{1,3}$ to a unit real combination $B$ of second-flavour ones. Write $A=A_\parallel+A_\perp$ with $A_\parallel$ in the
span of $\gamma_{1,1},\gamma_{1,2}$; its squared coefficient norm is $|W_{1,1}|^2$, so $A_\parallel^2=|W_{1,1}|^2I$. Conjugation by $x$ reverses
$A_\parallel$ and fixes $A_\perp$ and $B$, so $x(iAB)-(iAB)x=-2iA_\parallel Bx$ and, since $B^2=I$,
\[
\|x(iAB)-(iAB)x\|_2^2=4|W_{1,1}|^2 .
\]
The normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm is unitarily invariant, so averaging over clocks gives the exact identity
$\|[x,a^nza^{-n}]-I\|_2^2=4\,\mathbb E_c|W_c(a^n)_{1,1}|^2$, and at $n=4M$ the good rows give at least $4\cdot\frac14\cdot\frac1{128\pi}$.

\emph{Consequences.} With $n=4M$, $n^ue_M\le4^u\sqrt{14.26}\,M^{u-3/2}\to0$ for $u<\frac32$, while $\|([x,a^nza^{-n}]-I)\Psi\|$, the
normalized norm on the flat purification, stays at least $(128\pi)^{-1/2}$. For the dilution take the direct sum, with weights $p$ and
$1-p$, of this model and a native model $\mathcal N_{M'}$ with $(1-p)e_{M'}^2\le pe_M^2$, each on its maximally entangled purification:
the operators are block diagonal, the mirrors stay exact, the state commutators vanish, $e^2\le2pe_M^2\le29pM^{-3}$, and the squared raw
residual is at least $p/(128\pi)$. With $N=4M$, $N^3e^2\le64\cdot29\,p$, while the squared raw residual divided by $N^{2u}e^2$ is at least
$M^{3-2u}/(128\pi\cdot29\cdot16^u)$.
\end{proof}

This refutes a uniform raw estimate, not the endpoint $u=\frac32$, a decoder adapted to another architecture, or a suitably weighted
aggregate theorem; it is not legitimate to call the endpoint settled. The proposition is stated for the row-spine trees; we do not
need, and do not claim here, that the return amplitudes are the same for every legal tree.

\subsection{The finite-table gate and what refutes its cheap forms}\label{sec:table}

\begin{proposition}[A finite-table gate]\label{prop:table}
Let $G=\F_2^N\times\F_2^N$, $\omega(p,q)=(-1)^{t_p\cdot s_q}$ for $p=(s_p,t_p)$, let $U_p$ be arbitrary Bob unitaries and $M_p$ arbitrary
Alice unitaries, and put
\[
\mu^2=\mathbb E_q\|(M_q-U_q)\Psi\|^2,\qquad\Delta_N=\mathbb E_{p,q}\|(U_pU_q-\omega(p,q)U_{p+q})\Psi\|^2 .
\]
There are product local isometries with Bell overlap at least $1-2\mu^2-2\Delta_N$, and vector distance at most
$\sqrt{2\mu^2+2\Delta_N}$ from a Bell state of dimension $2^N$ times a residual purification. If $p_0=2\mu^2+2\Delta_N<\frac12$, then
$\ED^{A\to B}\ge(1-2p_0)N-h(p_0)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Bob's isometry $V_B|v\rangle=2^{-N}\sum_pU_p|v\rangle\otimes|p\rangle$ is always valid. On the fresh label register
$R_q|r\rangle=\omega(r+q,q)|r+q\rangle$ is an exact projective regular representation, $R_qR_r=\omega(q,r)R_{q+r}$, so it splits as $N$ standard
qubits times multiplicity. The coefficient of $R_qV_B|v\rangle$ at label $p$ is $\omega(p,q)U_{p+q}|v\rangle/2^N$, so
$\|(V_BU_q-R_qV_B)\Psi\|^2=\mathbb E_p\|(U_pU_q-\omega(p,q)U_{p+q})\Psi\|^2$, and $\mathbb E_q\|(M_q-R_q)V_B\Psi\|^2\le2\mu^2+2\Delta_N$. The coherent Alice-label
construction of \cref{thm:exact-point}, applied to the exact $R_q$ and the individual $M_q$, uses only this average.
\end{proof}

For the raw sorted point-source table, words of length at most $4N$ give $\mu^2\le16N^2e^2$, so the gate reduces to controlling the
same-state $\Delta_N$; it does not assume that perfect mirrors imply Pauli multiplicativity. In an exact negative representation
$\Delta_N=0$ and \cref{thm:exact-point} is recovered.

\begin{proposition}[What the collective witness refutes]\label{prop:collective-refutes}
On the collective models of \cref{prop:collective}, with $e_M=\Theta(M^{-2})$, pure cost $(4+o(1))M$ and all word mirrors exact, the
sorted table satisfies $\Delta_N\ge1-D_M/2^N$. Consequently no bound $\Delta_N\le CN^\alpha e^2$ with $\alpha<4$, and no modulus tending to zero
with $N^3e^2$, holds on all actual finite point-source tuples; no Bell-error-density law $b_N\le\omega(N^3e^2)$ with $\omega(t)\to0$ and
the hashing guarantee holds; and the raw aggregate estimate $E_B\le CN^4e^2$, inserted in \cref{thm:aggregate}, is false. The
quartic \emph{table} estimate $\Delta_N\le CN^4e^2$ is not refuted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With exact mirrors the cross term vanishes and the Bell failure of \cref{prop:table} is at most $\Delta_N$, while a local channel
applied to a pure state of Schmidt rank $D_M$ has overlap at most $D_M/2^N$ with $\Phi_{2^N}$. At $N=16M$ the lower bound tends to one,
while $N^\alpha e_M^2=O(M^{\alpha-4})\to0$. A density law would give a rate $N(1-o(1))$ at $N=16M$, exceeding the original $(4+o(1))M$ ebits;
the raw aggregate estimate would give $b_N=O(1/N)$ since $M_2=0$, with the same contradiction.
\end{proof}

These refutations do not exclude the matching quarter lower bound for the game.

\subsection{Corrections that keep the resource must be accurate}\label{sec:corrections}

\begin{proposition}[Correction costs]\label{prop:correction}
Apply a Bob-local isometry, or an explicitly reversible local lift, to the original collective state of \cref{prop:collective},
keeping Alice's actual mirrors, and suppose the target signed generators compare on that state within weighted error $\eta$. If
the target is an exact negative representation, then $\eta=\Omega(M^{-1})=\Omega(\sqrt{e_M})$, with infinite target dimension
allowed. If the target lies in the enlarged native class of \cref{thm:native-enlarged}, then $\eta=\Omega(M^{-3/2})=\Omega(e_M^{3/4})$,
conditional on that theorem. For the finite soft configuration models, of pure cost $O(e^{-2}\log(1/e))$ with exact mirrors, a
correction to an exact negative representation needs $\eta=\Omega(1/\log(1/e))$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The lifted state has the same directional rates, and since the old mirrors are exact, fixed word comparisons cost at most their
length times $\eta$: the lift is a model of the target at precision $O(e_M+\eta)$ with mirror errors $\eta$. For an exact target,
\cref{thm:exact-point} forces $S_M+1\ge c/(e_M+\eta)$ with $S_M=\Theta(M)$; for the native target \cref{thm:native-enlarged} forces
$S_M+1\ge c(e_M+\eta)^{-2/3}$. For the configuration models \cref{thm:exact-config} forces $S_e\ge\frac342^{\lfloor1/(24\eta)\rfloor}$, so
$\eta\ge1/[24(1+\log(4S_e/3))]$.
\end{proof}

These obstruct only the stated correction contract, not replacing the state, buying more entanglement, or qualitative
comparisons with vanishing error.

\subsection{Restricted phase schemes}\label{sec:phase}

\begin{proposition}[Scalar Weyl phases]\label{prop:phase}
Suppose actual unitaries on an original purification satisfy $\|(U_A-U_B)\Psi\|\le\delta$, $\|(V_A-V_B)\Psi\|\le\delta$ and
$\|(U_A^*V_AU_A-\lambda V_A)\Psi\|\le\delta$ for a \emph{fixed scalar} $\lambda\in\Tor$, and a game supplies $\delta\le C_G\sqrt\eps$. For a reduced
$p/q$ with $\zeta=e^{2\pi ip/q}$, $\Delta=|\lambda-\zeta|$, $2\le N\le q$, $m=\lfloor q/N\rfloor$, $P=mN/q$ and
$\eta=\sqrt{2q\delta}+N^{3/2}\delta+\sqrt{3qN\delta+qN\Delta}$, there is a one-way protocol succeeding with probability exactly $P$ whose output
has singlet fraction at least $1-\eta^2/P$. Consequently: for every irrational phase, $\ED^{A\to B}\ge c\log(1/\eps_n)$ along a sequence
$\eps_n\to0$; under polynomial growth of the continued-fraction denominators, for every small $\eps$; and so for every algebraic
$\lambda$ that is not a root of unity. A finite commuting-scalar rational semialgebraic gadget whose admitted phases avoid all
roots of unity admits only finitely many phases, all algebraic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
All operators are finite-dimensional, opposite-party operators commute, and commuting unitaries of the two parties have a
positive joint spectral measure on $\Psi$. Angles are in turns.

\emph{Random cuts across the parties.} For $t$ uniform in $[0,1/q)$ let $I_k(t)=[t+k/q,t+(k+1)/q)$, $k\in\Z/q\Z$, and for a unitary
$S$ let $E_k(S)$ be its spectral projection on $I_k(t)$. If $S_A$ and $T_B$ are unitaries of the two parties with
$\|(S_A-T_B)\Psi\|\le h$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-cut}
\mathbb E_t\sum_k\|(E_k(S_A)-E_k(T_B))\Psi\|^2\le2qh .
\end{equation}
Indeed, for the joint spectral measure $\nu$ of $(S_A,T_B)$ on $\Psi$ the sum is twice the $\nu$-probability that the two
spectral values $z,w$ lie in different arcs; two points at angular distance $d$ are separated with probability
$\min(1,qd)\le q|z-w|/4$; and $\int|z-w|\,d\nu\le h$ by Cauchy--Schwarz. Relabel the arcs by $j$ through $k=pj\bmod q$, a bijection, and let $P^A_j$, $P^B_j$ be the relabelled spectral
projections of $V_A$, $V_B$. By~\eqref{eq:phase-cut}, $\mathbb E_t\sum_j\|(P^A_j-P^B_j)\Psi\|^2\le2q\delta$.

\emph{Conjugation powers.} Put $C_a=U_A^{-a}V_AU_A^a$ and $b_a=\|(C_a-\lambda^aV_A)\Psi\|$. For an Alice operator $R$ with $\opn R\le2$,
$\|RU_A\Psi\|\le\|R\Psi\|+2\delta$ by \cref{lem:transfer-parties}(b) with the parties exchanged. Since
$C_{a+1}-\lambda^{a+1}V_A=U_A^{-1}(C_a-\lambda^aV_A)U_A+\lambda^a(U_A^*V_AU_A-\lambda V_A)$, this gives $b_1\le\delta$ and $b_{a+1}\le b_a+3\delta$. With
$|\lambda^a-\zeta^a|\le a\Delta$ and the $V$ mirror,
\[
\|(C_a-\zeta^aV_B)\Psi\|\le(3a+1)\delta+a\Delta\qquad(a\ge0).
\]
The relabelled spectral projections of $C_a$ are $U_A^{-a}P^A_jU_A^a$, and those of $\zeta^aV_B$ are $P^B_{j-a}$, since $\zeta^a$ rotates the
circle by $pa/q$. So~\eqref{eq:phase-cut} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-transport}
\mathbb E_t\sum_j\|(U_A^{-a}P^A_jU_A^a-P^B_{j-a})\Psi\|^2\le2q\bigl[(3a+1)\delta+a\Delta\bigr],
\end{equation}
and \cref{lem:transfer-parties}(c) gives $\|(U_B^a-U_A^a)\Psi\|\le a\delta$. No same-party difference of powers is used.

\emph{The instruments.} Let $r$ be uniform in $\Z/q\Z$, independent of $t$. Call the $mN$ labels $j=r+bN+a\bmod q$, $0\le b<m$, $0\le a<N$,
good, and the other $q-mN$ the tail. Alice applies the local instrument whose success branch is
\[
W_A=\sum_{b,a}|b,a\rangle\otimes U_A^{-a}P^A_{r+bN+a},\qquad W_A^*W_A=\sum_{j\ \mathrm{good}}P^A_j ,
\]
and Bob the isometry $W_B=\sum_{b,a}|b,a\rangle\otimes U_B^aP^B_{r+bN+a}+\sum_{j\ \mathrm{tail}}|\mathrm{tail},j\rangle\otimes P^B_j$. Alice
announces $r$, $t$ and her success flag; Bob does not postselect. Averaged over $r$ every label is good with probability $mN/q$, so
the success probability on the original state is exactly $P=mN/q$, with no assumption on the spectrum.

\emph{The comparison.} Work in the Hilbert direct sum over $(r,t)$ with their uniform weights, the record of $(r,t)$ kept in the
purification; for finite-dimensional operators the projections are piecewise constant in $t$ with finitely many breakpoints, so
the average over $t$ is a finite weighted sum. Let $\omega=(W_A\otimes W_B\otimes I_E)\Psi$ and
\[
y=\sum_{b,a}|b,a\rangle_A|b,a\rangle_B\otimes P^B_{r+bN}\Psi=\Phi_N\otimes\chi,\qquad\chi=\sqrt N\sum_b|b,b\rangle\otimes P^B_{r+bN}\Psi,
\]
with $\Phi_N$ the maximally entangled state of the two $a$-registers. Both have squared norm $P$; for $y$, average
$N\sum_b\|P^B_{r+bN}\Psi\|^2$ over $r$. We make three replacements.
(i) Replace $\omega$ by the vector with diagonal labels and coefficients $U_A^{-a}P^A_jU_B^a\Psi$, $j=r+bN+a$. For each good $j$ the
changed and discarded Bob components have squared norm $2\|P^A_j(I-P^B_j)\Psi\|^2$, and the sum of this over all $j$ is
$\sum_j\|(P^A_j-P^B_j)\Psi\|^2$, so the averaged squared error is at most $2q\delta$.
(ii) Replace $U_B^a$ by $U_A^a$ in these coefficients. For fixed $a$ the projections $P^A_{r+bN+a}$ are orthogonal, so the squared
error is at most $\sum_{a<N}a^2\delta^2\le N^3\delta^2$.
(iii) Replace $U_A^{-a}P^A_{r+bN+a}U_A^a\Psi$ by $P^B_{r+bN}\Psi$. Averaged over $r$, for each $a$ every label occurs with probability
$m/q\le1/N$, so by~\eqref{eq:phase-transport} the squared error is at most
$\frac1N\sum_{a<N}2q[(3a+1)\delta+a\Delta]\le3qN\delta+qN\Delta$.
By Minkowski's inequality $\|\omega-y\|\le\eta$. The normalized successful output $\omega/\sqrt P$ is therefore within $\eta/\sqrt P$ of the
unit vector $\Phi_N\otimes\chi/\sqrt P$, and its two $a$-registers have singlet fraction at least $1-\eta^2/P$; the environment, the
original systems, the $b$-registers and the public record are all in the junk.

\emph{Rates.} If $\gamma=\eta^2/P<\frac12$, Alice's heralded filtering (\cref{sec:prelim-usable}) and~\eqref{eq:singlet-hashing} give
$\ED^{A\to B}(\rho_{AB})\ge P[(1-2\gamma)\log N-h(\gamma)]$, with all communication from Alice to Bob. The decoder depends on the
strategy and is not efficient.

\emph{Consequences.} For a convergent $p_n/q_n$ of $\theta=\arg\lambda/2\pi$, $|\theta-p_n/q_n|<1/(q_nq_{n+1})$ gives
$q_n^2|\lambda-e^{2\pi ip_n/q_n}|\le2\pi$. Take $N=\lfloor cq_n\rfloor$ with a small fixed $c$ and $\delta\le q_n^{-4}$: then $P\ge1-c$, the first two
terms of $\eta$ tend to zero and $qN\Delta\le2\pi c$, so $\gamma=O(c)$ for large $n$ and the rate is at least $0.99\log q_n-O(1)$ once $c$ is
small. With $\eps_n=C_G^{-2}q_n^{-8}$ every strategy of loss at most $\eps_n$ has $\delta\le q_n^{-4}$, which gives the logarithmic bound
along $\eps_n\to0$. If $q_{n+1}\le Kq_n^B$, given $\delta$ choose the largest $q_n\le\delta^{-1/4}$; then $q_n\ge K^{-1/B}\delta^{-1/(4B)}$, and the bound
holds at every small $\eps$. For algebraic non-torsion $\lambda$, Baker--W\"ustholz~\cite{BW93} gives $|\lambda^q-1|\ge aq^{-A}$ for $q\ge1$, and
$|\lambda^{q_n}-1|\le2\pi/q_{n+1}$ yields $q_{n+1}\le(2\pi/a)q_n^A$. For the gadget, the admitted set is semialgebraic over the real algebraic
numbers by Tarski--Seidenberg~\cite{Sei54}, so it has finitely many connected components, points or arcs; a nontrivial arc contains a
root of unity, so only finitely many isolated points remain, and these have real algebraic coordinates.
\end{proof}

These statements do not cover an uncontrolled central-operator phase or unrestricted noncommutative encodings, and they provide no
fixed rational game compiler; no game below the logarithmic floor is obtained.

\subsection{Finite-support variants give integer growth}\label{sec:finite-support}

\begin{proposition}[Finite supports]\label{prop:finite-support}
Let $H_3$ act transitively on a set of objects. Fix a $k$-element set $S\subseteq Y$ and suppose that the stabilizer $K$ of a base
object $o$ satisfies $\mathrm{Fix}_{H_3}(S)\le K\le\mathrm{Stab}_{H_3}(S)$, the pointwise and the setwise stabilizer of $S$. Then
$L=K/\mathrm{Fix}_{H_3}(S)$ is a subgroup of $S_k$, the orbit is $\mathrm{Inj}([k],Y)/L$, the object $ho$ has support $h(S)$, and the
number of objects supported in the first $N$ points is $|\mathrm{Inj}([k],[N])|/|L|$, of integer polynomial order $N^k$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix a bijection $\iota:[k]\to S$. $H_3$ contains all finitary permutations, so it acts transitively on injections $[k]\to Y$ by
$h\cdot f=h\circ f$, with stabilizer $\mathrm{Fix}(S)$ at $\iota$, and restriction to $S$ maps $\mathrm{Stab}(S)$ onto $\mathrm{Sym}(S)\cong S_k$
with kernel $\mathrm{Fix}(S)$. So $\mathrm{Fix}(S)$ is normal in $K$ and $L=K/\mathrm{Fix}(S)$ embeds in $S_k$, acting on injections by
precomposition. The map $hK\mapsto(h\circ\iota)L$ is well defined, since $h\kappa\circ\iota=h\circ\iota\circ\iota^{-1}\kappa|_S\iota$ for
$\kappa\in K$, and bijective; and $L$ acts freely on injections. The count follows.
\end{proof}

The two-sided hypothesis is needed: the lower bound $\mathrm{Fix}(S)\le K$ alone does not make $S$ a least support. A trivial
one-object action has $K=H_3$, which contains $\mathrm{Fix}(S)$ for every finite $S$, so it admits declared supports of every size,
while its orbit has one element.

Finite colours, orderings and finite quotient patterns within this architecture therefore do not create a fractional exact law;
\cref{thm:gamma3} escapes by replication, not by refuting the premise. This is standard finite-support reasoning, not a
classification of all finitely presented actions.

\subsection{Operator accuracy without trace visibility}\label{sec:trace-visibility}

\begin{proposition}[Invisible witnesses]\label{prop:invisible}
\emph{(a)} A $D$-dimensional unitary $W$ with $\rank(W-I)\le r$ has $\|W-I\|_{2,D}^2\le4r/D$. \emph{(b)} If $J$ lies in the normal closure
of a word $w$ in a fixed extension presentation, then in the tracial normalized setting with root relator error $e$,
$\|J-I\|_2\le C\|w-I\|_2+C'e$. \emph{(c)} Consider the relations $S^*S=I-P_N$, $SS^*=I-P_0$, $XS=SX^2$, $XP_0=\frac12P_0$, $XP_N=0$,
with $P_0,P_N$ projections and $X$ positive. For every $D$ they have matrix models with all relations exact except
$\|XP_N\|=2^{-2^{D-1}}$, and with $\|P_0\|=1$ but $\mathrm{tr}_D(P_0)=1/D$; and every exact model in a finite von Neumann algebra with a
faithful normal tracial state, in particular every exact finite-dimensional model, has $P_0=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) $\Tr(W-I)^*(W-I)\le4\rank(W-I)$. (b) A fixed derivation writes $J$ as a product of conjugates of $w^{\pm1}$ and relators; unitary
invariance of the normalized norm and telescoping give the bound. (c) The shift $Se_i=e_{i+1}$ ($i<D-1$), $Se_{D-1}=0$ on $\C^D$, with
$P_0$ and $P_N$ the projections onto $e_0$ and $e_{D-1}$ and $X=\mathrm{diag}(2^{-2^i})$, satisfies all but the last relation exactly. In an
exact model, let $P_0v=v\ne0$. Then $Xv=\frac12v$, and $XS=SX^2$ gives $XS^kv=2^{-2^k}S^kv$ for every $k$ by induction. Since $X$ is
self-adjoint, each $S^kv$ with $S^kv\ne0$ lies in an eigenspace with nonzero eigenvalue, hence is orthogonal to $\ker X\supseteq\mathrm{ran}\,P_N$,
so $\|S^{k+1}v\|^2=\langle S^kv,(I-P_N)S^kv\rangle=\|S^kv\|^2$. Thus $S^k$ is isometric on $\mathrm{ran}\,P_0$, the projections
$E_k=S^kP_0S^{*k}$ are equivalent to $P_0$, and they lie in distinct eigenspaces of $X$, hence are mutually orthogonal. A faithful normal
tracial state $\tau$ then gives $(n+1)\tau(P_0)=\tau(E_0+\dots+E_n)\le1$ for every $n$, so $\tau(P_0)=0$ and $P_0=0$. For finite matrices
alone positivity of $X$ is not needed: $\ker S=\ker S^*S=\mathrm{ran}\,P_N\subseteq\ker X$, so inductively every $S^kv$ is a nonzero
eigenvector of $X$ with eigenvalue $2^{-2^k}$; these are linearly independent, which is impossible in finite dimension.
\end{proof}

So exponentially accurate operator relations with a bounded-rank witness have normalized visibility $O(D^{-1/2})$, and
normal-closure compilation cannot promote vanishing trace visibility into $J=-I$ at vanishing error on the whole space.
Baumslag--Solitar near-collision models~\cite{SV18,Fri12} have exponentially accurate operator relations of exactly this
invisible kind. A central marker that is visible in operator norm can always be compressed to a negative sector without loss
(\cref{lem:compression}); for these models the obstruction then reappears on the compressed sector
(\cref{prop:bs-central,prop:copied-gadget}). The known weak dimension lower bound for $\mathrm{BS}(2,3)$ is not a
polylogarithmic upper bound, and no claim is made that these routes are impossible in general.
