\section*{How this work was done}\label{sec:methods}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{How this work was done}
%======================================================================

\paragraph{Roles.} This paper is the work of its two authors together with current frontier AI models from more than one
developer. The authors set the direction throughout: they chose the questions, fixed the scope and the standards of proof and
review, decided which lines to pursue and which to stop, and take full responsibility for every claim. The models did most of the
exploration, derivation, computation and drafting, in a sequence of short research sprints. Each sprint ran several independent
lines on one question, with an adversarial line attacking the others, and recorded its results in written proof files with their
status; a consolidated ledger of the surviving theorems, their exact hypotheses, dependencies and corrections was then the sole
source for this write-up. Statements that a sprint proposed and a later sprint or review refuted are recorded as such; several of
them appear in \cref{sec:obstructions}.

\paragraph{Validation.} The evidence for the results is of three kinds: complete written proofs and computations by the
scripts in \texttt{verify/}, which the reader can check, and independent blind reviews, summarized below. The reviews served to
find errors; they do not replace the proofs.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Proofs.} Every theorem is proved in the text or the appendices, or, for the results of our companion
paper~\cite{DM-game}, stated with a pointer to its proof there; each theorem is proved in exactly one of the two papers.
Conditional results name their missing inputs.
\item \emph{Blind reviews.} Four independent reviews, each by a model of a different developer from the one that derived the
results, read only the proof files and the literature, formed their verdicts before reading any internal assessment, worked
refutation first, and wrote their own checks. The first covered the universal compiler and the Boolean return; the second the
rational source family, its Boolean constraint games, the R\'enyi spectrum, the shared-dilation source, the operational extraction tools and
the rational and configuration-lamp rates; the third Theorem~A, its certificates, the undecidability and protocol theorems, the
point window, the native class laws and the exact point and configuration sources; the fourth the directional results, the subset
and replication sources, the obstructions of \cref{sec:phase,sec:finite-support,sec:trace-visibility} and the $I_{3322}$ contrast. None of these four
reviews found a false statement. They found gaps that are repaired here: the moment hierarchy needs the projection relations on both sides; the
unrestricted point-game upper bound is $\eps^{-1/4}$, below the native law, which is therefore labelled as a class law; the protocol
is coherent white-box, not device-independent; Slofstra's construction already has ternary equations; the block lemma of
\cref{prop:normal-perfect} is known; and several attributions were added. The reviews also left two statements conditional, and
they are labelled so. The tool results inherited from our earlier draft (the count, the transfer and return, the compiler on
virtual retracts, $\mathrm{CHSH}(n)$ and the Slofstra--Vidick results) were reviewed in the same way before; those that are now
proved in the companion paper are reviewed with it.
\item \emph{A second read.} The finished manuscript was then read as a whole by a model of another developer, not blind, since
it had seen the discovery history; a separate fresh-context reader of the manuscript alone contributed to it. It compared every
statement with the ledger, wrote out the two proofs that had been outlined (\cref{prop:raw-pairs,prop:phase}), which were then checked
step by step, checked the several-copy route to linear games (\cref{cor:linear-existence}), which was then rederived, and ran a
citation snowball. It found
statements that were false as written, all repaired here: an inference from the $I_{3322}$ contrast to the classes of Theorem~A,
the domain of the R\'enyi formula in Theorem~C, the dimension of invariant measures, a permutation convention in
\cref{thm:shared}, an equality case in the Jensen--Shannon bound of the count and a missing hypothesis in \cref{prop:finite-support}; and several
attributions were added.
\item \emph{Audits of the whole manuscript.} The complete manuscript was then audited twelve times, each time by a model of another
developer; the first ten audits also used the scripts and checked every reference against a primary record. The first nine audits
were blind; the tenth saw this account of the earlier reviews and the status table after it had found its points, and the last two
each saw part of this account by accident. The first audit found one false statement, repaired here: the extraction clause of
Theorem~A had lost the hypothesis that the game has no finite-dimensional perfect strategy. No later audit found a counterexample
to any theorem. Their findings, all repaired here, included gaps in proofs and in the checks, statements wider than what was
proved, and points of wording and citation; the substantive ones were a false orthogonality claim in the one-stage proof of
\cref{lem:cyclic-triangular}, now proved without it; the lift in the proof of~\cite[Proposition~6.2]{SV18} for several
non-involutive generators, now replaced by \cref{prop:extended} and~\cite[Lemma~11.13]{DM-game}; the block size in
\cref{prop:window-dilution}; an interface hypothesis in the proof of \cref{thm:lamps-ED}; and summaries wider than the theorems
in the abstract, the introduction and the opening of \cref{sec:lamps} (the factor three, the computable thresholds, the
coefficient domain, a law stated for every strategy). \Cref{app:audits} lists each audit and its findings.
\item \emph{Two proof checks of new results.} The floor results of \cref{sec:floor,app:floor} and the window obstruction of
\cref{app:window} were derived after the second audit and proof-checked by a model of another developer. It found all of them to
hold and asked for repairs, all made here: the certificate transfer is stated only where \cref{prop:interface} proves it; the value-one
chain for Abels' group and the constants from relators to the interface are written out; the separation of single-copy thresholds is
claimed only below infidelity about $\frac14$; the polynomials of \cref{prop:slow-recovery} are formally self-adjoint; and the dilution
of \cref{prop:window-dilution} uses a weight depending on $M$, so that it covers the scale of the window proof. An exact form of type~II
sign and dilation, which rested on two imported theorems that had not been checked, was then removed from \cref{prop:typeII}. The coefficient
results of \cref{thm:rational-ED} for irrational $\alpha$, \cref{lem:cyclic-triangular} and \cref{sec:coefficient-set,sec:arithmetic} were
derived during the third audit and proof-checked by a model of another developer, which found all of them to hold and asked for
repairs, all made here: the small-loss domain of \cref{thm:rational-ED}; the extension of \cref{prop:interface} to selector
polynomials; an explicit cross-party bound for cuts with $q$ arcs; and the wording of the transcendence example, with the original
Gelfond and Schneider papers cited. The sharper arithmetic bound of \cref{thm:arithmetic}, left-c.e.\ relative to $0''$ rather than
computable relative to $0'''$, was sketched by that check and is proved here.
\item \emph{Computation.} The scripts in \texttt{verify/}, run by \texttt{verify/run\_all.py}, attack the inequalities on random,
perturbed, singular and non-flat instances and record their output in \texttt{verify/logs/}: the qubit count, the lemmas of the
preliminaries, the return theorems, the Wasserstein-$1$ and transport estimates, the lamp-source models and the arithmetic of their
orbits, the random-cut and weighted-history lemmas with a nonunital negative control, the cyclic extraction of
\cref{lem:cyclic-triangular} (one stage exactly, with controls of order other than $q$; the one-stage inequality~\eqref{eq:cyc-stage}
with constant one on perturbed and random instances, with the non-orthogonal unrecorded vectors as a negative control; and several stages
on a mixed state with perturbed mirrors, where the error scales linearly with the residuals, with a negative control), the cut bound across the parties with
$q$ arcs, Fourier-lamp models with non-dyadic dilations, the depth arithmetic of \cref{thm:rational-ED} and the coefficient set of
\cref{cor:coefficient-set}, the balanced construction of \cref{prop:extended} on random inputs (with the unbalanced literal marker,
which admits no approximate sign relation, as a negative control) and the lift of~\cite[Lemma~11.13]{DM-game} for the Slofstra--Vidick
models, replayed independently of the companion's own check, the universal compiler on two sources with
its split identity checked exactly in the negative quotient, the linear games K4 and L4M8 rebuilt from their sources, and the
tree of faces behind \cref{thm:linear-filling}, whose free equality is checked on integer edge words before group labels are
substituted. The larger exported objects are archived in \texttt{verify/artifacts} with their SHA-256 hashes, and
\texttt{verify/run\_artifacts.py} replays their checks: the point-Pauli Boolean game, checked exactly against the $22$ relators of
\cref{sec:point-source}; the archived L4M8 game, checked exactly against its source presentation, every square, row and row
commutator, by applying the normal-form engine of \texttt{verify/check\_linear\_games.py} to the archived file (a check adapted from the
second audit, which shares that engine), and played completely with Fourier-lamp strategies at four sizes in floating point; the
two-copy game, checked exactly in the negative source algebra, every square, row and row commutator, and played on every context and
raw outcome at two sizes; the selector construction of \cref{thm:undecidable} on two small inputs, with the L4M8 game in place of Slofstra's
fallback and without the ternary reduction; and bounded exact controls of the literal subset-lamp presentation for $k=2$ and of
the replication presentation of \cref{sec:exact}. No inequality was violated. Finite checks support the proofs and do not replace
them.
\end{itemize}
No argument here has been formally verified; the proofs, the computations and the blind reviews are offered for checking as
they stand.

\paragraph{Priority.} For each headline result, bounded searches of arXiv and the general literature, and the reviews, compared the
statements with the closest work; the credits they found are given at the results. They found no exact preemption of the composed
statements, which is not an exhaustive priority verdict. The second read compared the white-box protocol with the two September 2026
preprints on universal entanglement distillation~\cite{TWMAH26,TMGL26} against their primary texts, and ran a backward and
forward citation snowball over the bibliography; the ancestry it found (compound-source distillation~\cite{BJ14} and the
relation-decomposition lemma of~\cite{MSZ23}) is credited at the results. Two further blind sweeps by models of another developer
compared the claims with the literature up to October 2026, including recent self-testing results~\cite{Bap25,VZ26,CZ26} and the
concurrent $I_{3322}$ papers~\cite{Pau26,Col26}, and every reference was then checked against its primary source; the comparisons
are stated at the results.
