\section*{How this work was done}\label{sec:methods}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{How this work was done}

The authors selected the questions and scope, directed the research and take responsibility for every claim. AI models from more than one developer assisted with exploration, derivation, computation and drafting. The models were set against each other in refutation-first checks and repeated whole-paper adversarial reviews, with exact computation. Reports of the checks are available from the authors.

\paragraph{Proof and computational scope.} Each result in \cref{tab:status} identifies its proof, imported input or conditional hypothesis. The companion-source map in \cref{app:companion} records the external results used. The verification scripts test the specified finite constructions and numerical estimates; they do not establish the asymptotic theorems. \Cref{app:audits} gives reproducibility commands, dependencies and computational limitations. No formal verification of this manuscript is claimed.

\paragraph{Finite computations and exported objects.}
The scripts in \texttt{verify/}, run by \texttt{verify/run\_all.py}, test the inequalities on random, perturbed, singular and non-flat instances. They record the qubit count, preliminary lemmas, return theorems, Wasserstein-$1$ and transport estimates, lamp-source models and orbit arithmetic, random-cut and weighted-history lemmas, cyclic extraction on mixed states with perturbed mirrors, non-orthogonal unrecorded-vector controls, cross-party cut bounds, Fourier lamps with non-dyadic dilations, depth and coefficient arithmetic, balanced lifts and their unbalanced-marker controls, the universal compiler's exact split identity, finite linear games and tree-of-faces identities. The source relators and their raw finite words are tested before group labels are substituted. These are checks of the enumerated instances, not general proofs.

The larger exported objects are archived in \texttt{verify/artifacts/} with SHA-256 hashes. The runner \texttt{verify/run\_artifacts.py} replays their checks: the point-Pauli Boolean game against all $22$ source relators; the archived L4M8 game against its presentation, squares, rows and row commutators using the normal-form engine of \texttt{check\_linear\_games.py}, with complete Fourier-lamp play at four sizes; the two-copy game in the negative source algebra, with every context and raw outcome played at two sizes; the selector construction on two small inputs using L4M8 in place of the fallback game; and bounded exact controls for the literal subset and replication sources. The archived-game checker shares that normal-form engine, so it is not an independent implementation of the engine.

\paragraph{Spectrum, floor and window checks.} The script \texttt{verify/check\_refinements.py} checks nineteen finite groups of assertions, including arithmetic arrays, flagged ensembles, physical leakage, floor diagnostics, Laurent freeness and the small-error pairwise budget. The current point-window exponent is recorded by \texttt{check\_tree\_filling.py} and the window engines. The script \texttt{verify/check\_window.py} runs two independently implemented free-word verifiers of one certificate set produced by the first engine. Each verifies thirty-seven core and six transfer certificates before and after literal auxiliary-relator expansion; the second also checks sixty holonomy examples. They are two verifiers of the same certificate set, not two independent certificate-generation procedures. Their recorded JSON contains the matrices, spectra, constants and certificate results. The runners execute one process at a time with one numerical-library thread. Rerunning intentionally refreshes the JSON and logs specified in the repository README.

The leakage proof uses Winter's conditional-entropy bound~\cite[Lemma~2]{Winter16}. The magnetic-cycle spectrum and equal-distribution minimization used for holonomy are credited to~\cite[Lemma~2.9]{CTT11} and~\cite[Proposition~4.6(b)]{BGKLM20}. Recovery, exact synchrony, hashing, convex roofs and entropy continuity retain their cited sources at use.

\paragraph{Literature and limits.} Bounded primary-literature searches compare the headline statements with the works credited at the results; they do not establish exhaustive priority. The white-box protocol is compared with universal distillation~\cite{TWMAH26,TMGL26}, compound-source distillation~\cite{BJ14} and relation decomposition~\cite{MSZ23}. Comparisons also include self-testing~\cite{Bap25,VZ26,CZ26}, the $I_{3322}$ literature~\cite{Pau26,Col26}, and the tools listed in \cref{sec:intro-related}. Written proofs remain the evidence for the asymptotic statements; finite computations and reviews support their checking.
