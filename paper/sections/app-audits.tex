\section{Review history}\label{app:audits}
%======================================================================

This appendix records the twelve audits of the whole manuscript that are summarized in the section
\hyperref[sec:methods]{How this work was done}; they followed the four blind reviews and the second read described there.

A model of another developer then audited the complete manuscript blind, refutation first, with
its scripts and with every reference checked against its primary source. It found one false statement: the extraction clause of
Theorem~A had lost the hypothesis that the game has no finite-dimensional perfect strategy. It also found an explicit decoder
stated without its small-loss domain (\cref{thm:rational-ED}), game-variable counts that included the central symbol $J$,
computations described beyond what \texttt{verify/} reproduced, and several points of wording. All were repaired. The linear
filling charge (\cref{thm:linear-filling}) was found after this audit.

A second blind audit, by a model of another developer, read
the revised manuscript and its scripts refutation first, replayed every check and inspected every reference against its primary
source. It found no counterexample to any theorem, including the linear filling charge, and four points that are repaired here:
the companion paper on which several results depend was not obtainable, which the split of the two papers and their joint release
resolve; the infinite-dimensional comparison gave a pure-vector theorem a mixed-state scope (\cref{sec:intro-known},
\cref{rem:A-scope}); the archived L4M8 game was checked exactly only through a rebuilt copy, and the exact check of the archived file
is now part of the replay; and the last open question compared two profiles more broadly than shown. It also suggested four earlier
references, now cited at their uses~\cite{BBPS96,Uhl76,CoSt20,PV10}, and a consolidation of the scope statements into
\cref{sec:status}.

A third blind audit, by a model of another developer, read the manuscript with the floor and window results,
replayed both runners and looked up every reference in a primary record. It found no counterexample to any theorem and four points,
repaired here: \cref{prop:window-dilution} allowed an arbitrarily large second block while bounding its entanglement by the minimal
size, and the block size is now fixed; the discussion of \cref{prob:floor} treated the sufficient criterion of \cref{prop:criterion} as
necessary; the proof of \cref{thm:lamps-ED} invoked \cref{prop:interface}, which needs a source marker equal to $-1$, where the
transfer identities of \cref{lem:transfer-parties} and the companion's relator certificates give what is used; and the threshold of
\cref{app:floor-threshold} was called positive without the promise of Theorem~A. It also suggested three related works, now cited at
their uses~\cite{MV14,vDH03,CW26}.

A fourth blind audit, by a model of another developer, read the manuscript with the coefficient
results, replayed the runners and the coefficient checks, and looked up every reference in a primary record. It found no counterexample
to any theorem and three points, repaired here: the one-stage proof of \cref{lem:cyclic-triangular} asserted that the unrecorded vectors
of different labels are orthogonal, which is false, and the estimate now uses only the sum of their squared norms and the orthogonal
recorded labels; the input convention for effectively algebraic games did not select a root of a polynomial; and the limit lemma was
used without a reference~\cite{Sho59}. It also suggested five related works, now cited at their uses~\cite{Gao15,KMW19,Cor13,AC24,BWB25}.

A fifth blind audit, by a model of another developer, read the revised manuscript, including the repaired proof of
\cref{lem:cyclic-triangular}, replayed the three runners and looked up every reference in a primary record. It found no
counterexample to any theorem and two prose summaries wider than their theorems, repaired here: the introduction stated the factor
three for all games, where \cref{thm:factor-three} proves it for agreement games and \cref{prop:sync-repair} gives only a
square-root repair in class~(S); and the discussion excluded type~I limits without the hypothesis that the game has no
finite-dimensional perfect strategy. It also asked for three clarifications, made here: the finite-dimensional convention of
\cref{sec:prelim} now names its exceptions, the divisibility before \cref{thm:rational-ED} is stated as $2^{r_i}\mid m_i$, and the
comparison with self-testing now includes self-tests without a finite-dimensional ideal~\cite{CZ26}. It suggested four related
works, now cited at their uses~\cite{PaSc26,PSZZ24,MaSc23,MNY22}.

A sixth blind audit, by a model of another developer, read the
revised manuscript, replayed the three runners and looked up every reference in a primary record. It found no counterexample to any
theorem and one summary wider than its theorems, repaired here: the abstract and the introduction stated computable thresholds and
the white-box protocol without the restriction of \cref{thm:effective,thm:protocol} to rational games in class~(S) and rational
binary games in class~(C). It also found three local points, repaired here: one step of the proof of \cref{prop:interface} cited
\cref{lem:transfer-parties}(b), which concerns unitary words, for a polynomial suffix, and now uses~\eqref{eq:opposite-poly}; the
overview of the conversions of errors in \cref{sec:prelim-usable} denied the further power losses that the proofs account for; and
the coherent sign rounding of \cref{app:protocol} gave a sign to complex eigenvalues rather than to the cosine of their phase. Of the
related works it suggested, those on moment hierarchies and $\mathrm{MIP}^*=\mathrm{RE}$ are now cited at their
uses~\cite{NPA08,PNA10,BuKl12,JNVWY20}.

A seventh blind audit, by a model of another developer, read the revised manuscript, replayed
the three runners and looked up every reference in a primary record. It found no counterexample to any theorem and one exact cost
stated too low, repaired here: the compiled native witness after \cref{thm:native} uses the amplified flat state, so its rates include
$\log12$; it also found a discrepancy between one book chapter and its publisher's record~\cite{Abe79}, now resolved.

An eighth blind
audit, by a model of another developer, read the revised manuscript, replayed the three runners and looked up every reference in a
primary record. It found no counterexample to any theorem and four local points, repaired here: the coefficient domain of the
abstract and Theorem~C admitted the power of two $1$; the rigidity sentence of \cref{prob:analytic}(iii) lacked its hypotheses and
references~\cite{Rud90,Joh92}; the chapter~\cite{Abe79} now follows the record its DOI links; and a summary of the floor in
\cref{sec:open} is narrowed to the games for which a demand law is proved.

A ninth blind audit, by a model of another developer, read the
revised manuscript, replayed the three runners and looked up every reference in a primary record. It found no counterexample to any
theorem and two points, repaired here. The proof of~\cite[Proposition~6.2]{SV18} lifts a representation with a balanced marked
involution only for one non-involutive generator, so the amplification $8$ that we had taken from it was not supported for the lamp
sources; \cref{prop:extended} now rests on a balanced construction proved here, with amplification $2^{l+2}$, and the lift for the
group of~\cite{SV18} itself, first written out in \cref{sec:transport-game}, is now proved in our companion
paper~\cite[Lemma~11.13]{DM-game}. And the opening of \cref{sec:lamps} stated as an equality for every strategy a law whose lower
bound alone holds for every strategy; the equality holds for the infimum demand. It also found two local points in the text, repaired
here: the adapter sentence of \cref{sec:normal-perfect} named a resource cost without a conversion contract, and the comparison with
Culf and Mastel after \cref{thm:undecidable} called their arities and alphabets smaller without saying which.

A tenth blind audit, by a
model of another developer, read the revised manuscript, replayed the three runners and looked up every reference in a primary
record; it checked the balanced construction, with code of its own, and the lift for the group of~\cite{SV18}. After it had found its
two points, it also saw, while checking, this account of the earlier reviews and the status table of \cref{app:status}, so its
reading was not blind throughout. It found no counterexample to any theorem and two points of wording, repaired here: the comparison
with self-testing in \cref{sec:intro-known} gave Zhao and Kar one characterization on the game algebra, where Zhao's uses the
universal measurement algebra and requires the unique trace to be finite-dimensional, and Kar's is stated for synchronous game
algebras; and the opening of \cref{sec:point} could be read as giving the point game a power-law exponent, where
\cref{thm:point-window} proves a window between two positive powers.

Two further audits, by models of another developer, then
read the whole manuscript in parallel, refutation first; each saw part of this account of the earlier reviews by accident, so
neither was blind throughout. Neither found a counterexample or an error in a main statement. They asked for two local repairs,
made here: the rounding function in the proof of \cref{lem:S3-round} is defined with its exception at $-1$, and class~(C)
discards variables that occur in no constraint, so that every variable has an incident constraint (\cref{def:law-classes}).
They also suggested the citations of~\cite{HHH98,DPP19,MR20,Bei21}, added in the introduction.
