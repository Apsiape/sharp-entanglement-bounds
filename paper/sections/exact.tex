%======================================================================
\section{Exact sources: the price of an address}\label{sec:exact}
%======================================================================

A finite game sees its source only through approximate relations. It is instructive to ask what the same groups cost when
Bob's relations are exact. We work under the exact-source contract (EX) of \cref{sec:prelim-usable}, with Alice's mirror error $e$.
The demand is then governed by one quantity: how many independent Pauli qubits can be \emph{addressed} by short words. Linear, polynomial and
exponential address growth give demands $\Theta(1/e)$, $\Theta_k(e^{-k})$ and $2^{\Theta(1/e)}$, in both physical directions.
Exact relations and approximate ones can differ by an exponential in this sense (\cref{sec:obstructions}).

\subsection{The address-growth principle}\label{sec:address}

\begin{theorem}[Address growth]\label{thm:address}
Suppose an exact negative source representation contains $m$ independent exact Pauli pairs $X_i,Z_i$ whose \emph{individual}
generators are represented by source words of length at most $r$, and that Alice's actual signed-generator mirrors have root
error at most $e$. Then
\[
\ED^{B\to A}(\rho_{AB})\ge m\bigl[1-2h(r^2e^2/4)\bigr],\qquad\ED^{A\to B}(\rho_{AB})\ge m\bigl[1-2h(r^2e^2/2)\bigr],
\]
in the monotone range of the entropy argument. Choosing $re$ a small constant gives $\Omega(m(c/e))$ in both directions, where
$m(r)$ is the number of addressable sites.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The $m$ exact Pauli pairs generate $M_{2^m}(\C)$, so Bob's space is $\C^{2^m}\otimes K$ with $X_i,Z_i$ the standard Paulis on the first
factor; this is a local unitary identification of the original space, not a compression. The reversed Alice word of each
individual generator is an actual unitary predictor with error at most $re$, by \cref{lem:transfer-parties}(a). Apply
\cref{thm:recovery}(c) with $p\le r^2e^2/4$, using $2p(1-p)\le2p$ in the forward direction.
\end{proof}

The full Pauli-string length need not equal the individual addressing length, and no joint Alice measurement is used:
individual predictors need not commute. Addressing supplies only a lower bound; a matching law needs an upper construction in
an exact representation, which the following subsections give. The uncertainty relation with memory~\cite{BCCRR10} and
hashing~\cite{DW05} are the standard inputs.

\subsection{The point source}\label{sec:exact-point}

Let $\Omega$ be the three rays of $H_3$ and $P_\Omega$ the group generated by a central involution $J$ and involutions $x_p,z_p$,
$p\in\Omega$, with $[x_p,z_p]=J$ and all generators at distinct sites commuting. A word supported on a finite set $S$ has normal
form $J^\epsilon\prod_{p\in S}x_p^{a_p}z_p^{b_p}$, so $P_\Omega$ is locally finite.

\begin{proposition}[Structure of the point source]\label{prop:point-structure}
The group presented by the $22$ relators of \cref{sec:point-source} is $P_\Omega\rtimes H_3$. It is an extension of $\Z^2$ by the locally
finite group $P_\Omega\rtimes\FSym(\Omega)$, hence elementary amenable. Every finite-dimensional representation sends $J$ to $I$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the presentation, $\langle sa,sb\rangle$ is the full stabilizer of the first point, so the transported pairs
$x_y=g_yxg_y^{-1}$, $z_y=g_yzg_y^{-1}$, with $g_y$ taking the first point to $y$, are well defined; the four pair prototypes and
the two-transitivity of $H_3$ give all cross-site commutations, and the onsite and central relations give $[x_y,z_y]=J$.
Conversely $P_\Omega\rtimes H_3$ satisfies the $22$ relators, and the natural maps are mutually inverse. Finitely many generators of
$P_\Omega\rtimes\FSym(\Omega)$ involve finitely many sites and a finite permutation group, so the group is locally finite, and the
quotient by it is $H_3/\FSym=\Z^2$. In a finite-dimensional representation the $J=-1$ eigenspace is invariant and carries
arbitrarily many independent Pauli pairs, so it is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact point source]\label{thm:exact-point}
Under (EX) for the point source, for every $N$ there are product local isometries extracting $\C^{2^N}\otimes\C^{2^N}$ registers within
vector error $4Ne$ of a maximally entangled state times a residual purification, so with Bell infidelity at most $16N^2e^2$, and
\[
\ED^{A\to B},\ \ED^{B\to A}\ \ge\ N(1-32N^2e^2)-h(16N^2e^2)\qquad(16N^2e^2<\tfrac12).
\]
Exact induced representations with finite-rank pure states of entropy $O(1/e)$ exist, so both directional infima are $\Theta(1/e)$.
For a normal density $D$ in an exact negative representation, $\mathcal H(D)=\sum_g\hs{[\pi(g),\sqrt D]}^2$, the sum over signed
generators, satisfies $\mathcal H(D)\,(S(D)+1)^2\ge c$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{An exact $N$-qubit algebra with short words.} Put $X_i=\pi(a^ixa^{-i})$ and $Z_i=\pi(a^iza^{-i})$, $0\le i<N$; by
\cref{prop:point-structure} they are independent Pauli pairs, and Bob's space is $\C^{2^N}\otimes K_N$. For $s,t\in\{0,1\}^N$ the
Pauli $P_{s,t}=X^sZ^t$ is represented by the sorted word $a^{i_1}u_{i_1}a^{i_2-i_1}u_{i_2}\cdots u_{i_k}a^{-i_k}$, $u_i=x^{s_i}z^{t_i}$,
of length at most $4N$; let $M_{s,t}$ be its reversed Alice mirror, with error at most $4Ne$.

\emph{An always-valid isometry and an exact teleportation identity.} Alice's map $W_A|v\rangle=d^{-1}\sum_{s,t}M_{s,t}|v\rangle\otimes|s\rangle|t\rangle$,
$d=2^N$, is an isometry whatever the $M_{s,t}$ are, and by orthogonality of the labels
$\|W_A\Psi-d^{-1}\sum_{s,t}P_{s,t}\Psi\otimes|s,t\rangle\|^2=d^{-2}\sum_{s,t}\|(M_{s,t}-P_{s,t})\Psi\|^2\le16N^2e^2$. On the labels apply
$K=\mathrm{CNOT}_{T\to S}(I\otimes H_T)$. For a basis input $|j\rangle$ of Bob's logical register $B_0$, the ideal vector
$d^{-1}\sum_{s,t}(-1)^{t\cdot j}|j+s\rangle_{B_0}|s\rangle|t\rangle$ becomes $|\Phi_d\rangle_{B_0S}\otimes|j\rangle_T$, so by linearity the ideal
vector is $\Phi_d$ on $B_0S$ times the original state with $B_0$ relabelled $T$. The actual output is within $4Ne$ of it, and
\eqref{eq:singlet-hashing} gives the rates, in either direction since the output is a high-fidelity Bell state.

\emph{An exact upper bound.} Let $\Gamma=P_\Omega\rtimes H_3$, $K=P_\Omega\rtimes\FSym(\Omega)$, $\Gamma/K=\Z^2$ with $a,b$ the coordinate
generators, and $\sigma$ the negative spin representation of $K$ on the incomplete tensor product of qubits stabilized by the
all-zero vector: $x_p,z_p$ act as Paulis, $J=-I$, and finite permutations permute qubits. With the section $t_q=a^{q_1}b^{q_2}$,
the induced representation $\pi(g)(|q\rangle\otimes v)=|q+\lambda(g)\rangle\otimes\sigma(t^{-1}_{q+\lambda(g)}gt_q)v$ is exact, by the cocycle
identity, with $\pi(J)=-I$. For clocks in the box $[-L,L]^2$ and adjacent clocks, every active cocycle has zero translation and
length at most $4L+5$, so it permutes the finite set $S_L=\{C_i,A_i,B_i:0\le i\le4L+8\}$ of $12L+27$ sites into itself, and the
active lamps sit in $S_L$. Let $D_0$ be maximally mixed on $S_L$ and vacuum elsewhere, $f_j=(L+1)^{-1/2}\cos(\pi j/(2(L+1)))$ for
$|j|\le L$, and $D_L=\sum_qf_{q_1}^2f_{q_2}^2|q\rangle\langle q|\otimes D_0$. Every active cocycle commutes with $D_0$, so
$\hs{[\pi(a),\sqrt{D_L}]}^2=\hs{[\pi(b),\sqrt{D_L}]}^2=\sum_j(f_{j+1}-f_j)^2=2-2\cos\frac\pi{2(L+1)}$ and the other generators commute
with $\sqrt{D_L}$. The canonical purification, with Alice's transposes, is an admissible pure input with mirror errors
$O(1/L)$ and entropy $S(D_L)\le12L+27+2\log(2L+1)$. Take $L\asymp1/e$.

\emph{The Dirichlet inequality.} For a normal density $D$, the canonical purification with transpose mirrors has every mirror
error at most $\mathcal H(D)^{1/2}$, and its one-way rate is $S(D)$; the extraction bound gives $S(D)+1\ge c\,\mathcal H(D)^{-1/2}$.
\end{proof}

The traces of the exact negative algebra can be described completely. Let $A=\bigotimes_{p\in\Omega}M_2$ be the negative group algebra of
$P_\Omega$, with its unique trace $\mathrm{tr}_A$, and $\mathcal B=A\rtimes H_3$; for a finite permutation $f$ let $V_f\in A$ be its
qubit-permutation unitary and $U_h$ the implementers in $\mathcal B$.

\begin{proposition}[Negative traces]\label{prop:point-traces}
The tracial states of $\mathcal B$ are exactly $\tau_\chi(aU_h)=0$ for $h\notin\FSym(\Omega)$ and $\tau_\chi(aU_f)=\mathrm{tr}_A(aV_f)\chi(f)$ for
$f\in\FSym(\Omega)$, where $\chi$ ranges over the normalized positive-definite class functions of $\FSym(\Omega)$, which Thoma
classified~\cite{Tho64}. In particular $\tau(s)=\chi(s)/2\in[-\frac12,\frac12]$ and $\tau(\gamma)=\chi(\gamma)/4\in[0,\frac14]$ for the
three-cycle $\gamma$, so $\|\gamma-I\|_{2,\tau}^2\ge\frac32$, with equality for the trivial character.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The unitaries $W_f=V_f^*U_f$ commute with $A$ and represent $\FSym$, so $A\rtimes\FSym\cong A\otimes C^*(\FSym)$. In a tracial GNS
representation the closure of $A$ is the hyperfinite $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor $R$, and averaging over finite tensor factors gives
$\tau(aW_f)=\mathrm{tr}_A(a)\chi(f)$ with $\chi(f)=\tau(W_f)$. An infinite-support permutation $h$ induces an outer automorphism of $R$
(a local Pauli at a moved point outside the support of a finite-site approximant commutes with the approximant, while its image
is at $L^2$-distance $\sqrt2$), and the conditional expectation onto $R$ intertwines, $E_R(U_h)a=\alpha_h(a)E_R(U_h)$; in a factor a
nonzero intertwiner would make $\alpha_h$ inner, so $E_R(U_h)=0$. Conversely, for any trace $\chi$ on $C^*(\FSym)$ the trace
$\mathrm{tr}_A\otimes\chi$ on $A\rtimes\FSym$ is invariant under $H_3$, which preserves cycle types, and its composite with the canonical
expectation is tracial. The values follow from $\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{SWAP})=\frac12$ and $\mathrm{tr}(V_\gamma)=\frac14$; positivity of
$\chi(T_n^2)$ for $T_n=(n-1)^{-1}\sum_{j=2}^n(1\ j)$ gives $\chi(\gamma)\ge0$.
\end{proof}

This is an application of Thoma's classification and the standard inner-action argument, not a new classification. It shows
in particular that the limiting negative traces are not unique, so no single flat native trace covers them; and since every
exact finite-dimensional representation kills $J$, ordinary matrix stability towards exact finite models is impossible here.

\subsection{The configuration source}\label{sec:exact-config}

Let $\RP$ be the $27$-word Pauli certificate in $p,q,t,d,x,z$ of~\cite[Definition~7.1]{DM-game}, with $J=xzxz$; the lower bound uses
only these words, which are not asserted to present the configuration group $\GP$, and the upper bound uses $\GP$ itself.

\begin{theorem}[Exact configuration source]\label{thm:exact-config}
Under (EX) for $\RP$, with $\pi(J)=-I$ and $0<e\le\frac1{24}$,
\[
\ED^{B\to A}(\rho_{AB})\ge\tfrac34\,2^{\lfloor1/(24e)\rfloor},\qquad\ED^{A\to B}(\rho_{AB})\ge\tfrac12\,2^{\lfloor1/(24e)\rfloor}.
\]
Exact induced representations of $\GP$ with finite-rank pure states give an upper bound $2^{C/e}$, so both directional infima
are $2^{\Theta(1/e)}$. If $J$ is only an exact central reflection with $\|(J-I)\Psi\|\ge\Delta$, $0<\Delta\le2$, then
$\ED^{A\to B}(\rho_{AB})\ge(\Delta^2/8)\,2^{\lfloor\Delta/(48e)\rfloor}$ whenever the integer scale is at least one; this is a lower bound, not a
matching joint visibility law.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Addresses of length $6N-3$.} On the first ray $p(i)=i+1$. For $u=\{i_1<\dots<i_k\}\subseteq[N]$ put
$S_u=p^{i_1-1}tp^{i_2-i_1}t\cdots tp^{1-i_k}$, of length at most $3N-2$. In the companion's notation $U_i$ is a finite-permutation word
carrying $1$ to $i$ and $\tau_i=U_itU_i^{-1}$; the four host words of $\RP$ present $H_3$~\cite[Appendix~A]{DM-game}, $U_i^{-1}p^{i-1}$ lies in the stabilizer of
the first point, which is $\langle sp,sq\rangle$~\cite[Lemma~7.4]{DM-game}, and the relators $[t,sp],[t,sq]$ of $\RP$ make $t$ commute with it.
So $p^{i-1}tp^{1-i}=\tau_i$ in every exact certificate representation, and multiplying in increasing $i$ gives $S_u=T_u$, the
companion's transporter~\cite[Definition~7.5]{DM-game}. By~\cite[Theorem~7.8 and Lemma~7.11]{DM-game}, read exactly, $X_u=\pi(S_uxS_u^{-1})$ and $Z_u=\pi(S_uzS_u^{-1})$ are
$M=2^N$ independent Pauli pairs, with individual words of length at most $6N-3$.

\emph{Lower bounds.} \Cref{thm:address} with $r=6N-3$ and $N=\lfloor1/(24e)\rfloor$: the argument $p_N=((6N-3)e)^2/4\le\frac1{64}$ gives the
reverse bound with $1-2h(\frac1{64})>\frac34$, and $((6N-3)e)^2/2\le\frac1{32}$ gives the forward bound with $1-2h(\frac1{32})>\frac12$. For
the visibility version, apply the full lift of \cref{thm:recovery}; Alice's transposed central projection has the same weight
$p_A=\Tr(RP_-)\ge\Delta^2/4$ as Bob's, she measures it and announces success, conditional mirror errors are at most $\sqrt2e/\sqrt{p_A}$,
and the per-bit argument with $N=\lfloor\sqrt{p_A}/(24e)\rfloor$ gives $\frac{p_A}22^{N}\ge\frac{\Delta^2}82^{\lfloor\Delta/(48e)\rfloor}$. This is an equality
of marginal sector weights, not of sector outcomes.

\emph{Upper bound.} By~\cite[Proposition~6.8 and (5)]{DM-game}, $\GP=E_V\rtimes A$ has $\GP/K=\Z^2$ with $K=E_V\rtimes(V\rtimes\mathrm{GL}_{\mathrm{fin}}(Y))$,
$\lambda(p)=(1,0)$, $\lambda(q)=(0,1)$. Induce the negative spin representation of $K$ on the incomplete tensor product of qubits indexed by
all configurations, with the section $p^{c_1}q^{c_2}$; it is exact with $\pi(J)=-I$. For clocks in $[-L,L]^2$ the clock lemma of the
companion~\cite[Lemma~9.3]{DM-game}, with $m=L+1$, $r=R=2L+5$ and trivial ray swap, puts every active cocycle in the subgroup preserving
$W_R=\F_2^{(Y_R)}$, the configurations of the $3R$ points of height at most $R$. With $D_0$ maximally mixed on the $|W_R|=2^{6L+15}$
qubits of $W_R$ and the cosine clock as before, the mirror errors are at most $\pi/(2(L+1))$ and
$S(D_L)\le2^{6L+15}+2\log(2L+1)$. Take $L=\lceil\pi/(2e)\rceil$.
\end{proof}

The minimum rank of such a density is doubly exponential: its double logarithm is $\Theta(1/e)$.

\subsection{Subset lamps: every positive integer exponent}\label{sec:exact-subset}

Fix $k\ge1$. Let $Y$ be the three rays of $H_3$, with $p,q$ acting as in~\cite[Definition~6.1]{DM-game}, let $X_k=\binom Yk$ be the set of
unordered $k$-subsets, and let $E_{X_k}$ be the central Pauli group with finite-support normal forms $(\epsilon,a,b)$ over $\F_2$ and
product $(\epsilon,a,b)(\epsilon',a',b')=(\epsilon+\epsilon'+\langle b,a'\rangle,a+a',b+b')$, with common centre $J=(1,0,0)$. Put
$G_k=E_{X_k}\rtimes H_3$.

\begin{proposition}[Presentation]\label{prop:subset-presentation}
$G_k$ is presented on $p,q,x,z$ by $6k+11$ relators: the four relators of Johnson's presentation of $H_3$ in $p,q$
\cite{Joh99},~\cite[Theorem~2.8]{Lee12} (in the form~\cite[(6)]{DM-game}, with $s=[p,q]$); $x^2$, $z^2$, $(xz)^4$; the commutators of $x$
and $z$ with $s_i=p^{i-1}sp^{1-i}$ ($1\le i<k$), $v_k=p^kqp^{-k}$ and $u_k=v_kq^{-1}p$, which generate the setwise stabilizer of $[k]$;
the $4k$ commutators $[a,h_rbh_r^{-1}]$, $a,b\in\{x,z\}$, $h_r=p^{k-r}$, $0\le r<k$; and $[J,p],[J,q]$ with $J=(xz)^2$. For $k=2$ the
literal list has $23$ freely reduced words, of maximum length $22$ and total length $252$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The words $s_i$ act as adjacent transpositions of $[k]$, and $u_k,v_k$ fix $[k]$ pointwise and act on its three-ray complement as
the standard generators of $H_3$; so they generate the setwise stabilizer $S_k\times H_3$. Ordered pairs of distinct $k$-subsets are
classified up to $H_3$ by the size $r$ of their intersection, because $H_3$ contains all finitary permutations, and
$([k],p^{k-r}[k])$ represents size $r$. Without the two central relators the presented group $\hat G$ maps onto
$W=(\bigoplus_{X_k}D_4)\rtimes H_3$; conversely the stabilizer relators make the transported pairs well defined, the prototypes make
distinct sites commute, and this gives a homomorphism $W\to\hat G$ inverse to the first on generators. Adding $[J,p],[J,q]$
identifies all onsite centres, so the normal closure is the even-centre subgroup, and the quotient is $G_k$; its normal form
shows $J\ne1$. The input from the literature is Johnson's presentation; this is the permutational-wreath mechanism of
Cornulier~\cite{Cor06}, written out.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Integer exact-source spectrum]\label{thm:subset}
Under (EX) for $G_k$, both directional infima are $\Theta_k(e^{-k})$. Explicitly, with $r_N=2D_k(N-k)+1$, $D_1=1$ and
$D_k=6k-7$ for $k\ge2$, both rates are at least $\binom Nk[1-2h(r_N^2e^2/2)]$ when the argument is at most $\frac12$ (the reverse
estimate may use $r_N^2e^2/4$); for $k=2$ and $e\le\frac1{160}$ this is at least $e^{-2}/51200$. The upper bound uses all $k$-subsets of a
common three-ray point region of height $R_L=4L+\max(k,2)$, flat there and vacuum outside, and a smooth two-dimensional clock,
with entropy at most $\binom{3R_L}k+2\log(2L+1)$ and root generator error at most $\pi/[2(L+1)]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Addresses.} Let $g_0=p$ and, for $m\ge1$, $g_m=u_m$, which fixes $[m]$ and moves the rest of the first ray up by one. For
$S=\{a_1<\dots<a_k\}\subseteq[N]$, $a_0=0$, the word $T_S=\prod_{i=1}^kg_{i-1}^{a_i-a_{i-1}-1}$, factors in increasing $i$, carries $[k]$ to
$S$ and has length at most $D_k(N-k)$; so each of the $\binom Nk$ transported pairs has individual words of length at most $r_N$. On
an exact negative representation these sites generate $M_{2^{\binom Nk}}$. \Cref{thm:address} gives the lower bounds; for $k=2$,
$N=\lfloor1/(40e)\rfloor$ gives $r_Ne\le\frac14$ and the stated constant.

\emph{Upper bound.} The kernel of $G_k\to H_3\to\Z^2$ is $E_{X_k}\rtimes\FSym(Y)$; represent it on the finite-excitation spin space
of the subset sites, with point permutations permuting subset sites (they may move infinitely many sites but fix the all-zero
vector), and induce with the section $p^{c_1}q^{c_2}$. For a signed $p$ or $q$ edge with an endpoint in $[-L,L]^2$ the cocycle has
point-word length at most $4L+2$ and fixes every point above that height, and the transported base lamp at an active clock has
height at most $2L+k$; so one common region of height $R_L$ contains all supports. A finitary point permutation may move
infinitely many subset sites outside $\binom{S_L}k$ but preserves this set and its complement, so the flat density on its
$\binom{3R_L}k$ qubits with vacuum outside commutes with every active cocycle. With the cosine clock, the $p,q$ errors are
$\sqrt{2-2\cos(\pi/(2(L+1)))}$ and the $x,z$ errors vanish; pure concentration attains the entropy in either direction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Joint visibility law]\label{thm:subset-visibility}
On the exact relations of $G_k$, let $J$ be a central reflection, not necessarily $-I$ on the whole input, with
$\|(J-I)\Psi\|\ge\Delta$, $0<\Delta\le2$, and actual generator mirrors at error $e$. Uniformly for $e\le c_k\Delta$,
\[
\inf\ED^{A\to B}=\Theta_k(\Delta^{k+2}e^{-k}),\qquad\inf\ED^{B\to A}=\Theta_k(\Delta^{k+2}e^{-k}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The negative weight $p=\Tr\rho_B\frac12(I-J)$ is at least $\Delta^2/4$. For $B\to A$ Bob filters the negative sector and announces; for
$A\to B$ Alice applies the full lift of \cref{thm:recovery} and filters her exact lifted central sector, whose marginal weight is
also $p$. The conditional mirror errors are at most $e/\sqrt p$, or $\sqrt2e/\sqrt p$ in the forward case, and
$p\binom Nk[1-2h(r_N^2e^2/(2p))]$ with $N\propto\sqrt p/e$ gives $c\Delta^{k+2}e^{-k}$. For the upper bound take the locally flagged mixed
input that, with probability $p=\Delta^2/4$, is a pure negative-sector witness of \cref{thm:subset} at error $e/\sqrt p$, and otherwise a
trivial positive-sector product state; the flag is available to both parties. Squared mirror errors are multiplied by $p$, the
visibility is exactly $2\sqrt p=\Delta$, and both directional rates equal $p$ times the negative entropy, with no $h(p)$ term, by
local flag reading and pure concentration.
\end{proof}

The lower bound uses ``weight at least'', not an equality; Alice's and Bob's flag outcomes are not assumed identical on an
arbitrary input; and the law concerns mixed-state $\ED$, not a matching law for the marginal entropy or rank, which carry $h(p)$
and no factor $p$, respectively.

\subsection{A compact exponential source by replication}\label{sec:exact-gamma}

\begin{theorem}[A five-generator exponential source]\label{thm:gamma3}
Let $G_1$ be the case $k=1$ of \cref{prop:subset-presentation}, the exact point source on $p,q,x,z$ with $17$ relators. The map
$\varphi(p)=p^3$, $\varphi(q)=q^3$, $\varphi(x)=\prod_{i=0}^2p^ixp^{-i}$, $\varphi(z)=\prod_{i=0}^2p^izp^{-i}$, $\varphi(J)=J$ is an injective
endomorphism, and its ascending HNN extension $\Gamma_3=\langle G_1,t\mid tgt^{-1}=\varphi(g)\rangle$ has five generators and $21$ freely
reduced relators, of maximum length $22$ and total length $200$. Radix words address $N$ independent sites in individual length
at most $8\lceil\log_3N\rceil+1$, and under (EX), for $e\le\frac1{64}$, both directional rates are at least $\frac12\,3^{\lfloor1/(64e)\rfloor}$. Exact
induced representations over $\Z[1/3]^2\rtimes\Z$ with finite-rank pure states give both directional infima $\exp(\Theta(1/e))$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Replication.} Identify $Y\times[3]$ with $Y$ by $I(1,j,r)=(1,3(j-1)+r)$ and $I(i,j,r)=(i,3(j-1)+4-r)$ for $i=2,3$; the reversed copy
order on the donor rays is essential. Then $\varphi_H(g):I(y,r)\mapsto I(gy,r)$ is an injective endomorphism of $H_3$ with
$\varphi_H(p)=p^3$, $\varphi_H(q)=q^3$, including the crossing endpoints. On lamps $\varphi$ replicates each site on its block; the onsite
commutator becomes $J^3=J$ because $3$ is odd, and the normal forms show injectivity. By the normal-form theorem for ascending HNN
extensions $G_1$ embeds in $\Gamma_3$, and $J$ stays central and nontrivial. An even replication would kill the onsite commutator.

\emph{Addresses and the lower bound.} With $W_0$ empty and $W_n=p^rtW_at^{-1}$ for $n=3a+r$, $0\le r\le2$, $W_n$ represents $p^n$ and
$|W_n|\le4\lceil\log_3(n+1)\rceil$; the first $N$ point lamps have words $W_{j-1}xW_{j-1}^{-1}$, $W_{j-1}zW_{j-1}^{-1}$ of length at most
$r_N=8\lceil\log_3N\rceil+1$. With $d=\lfloor1/(64e)\rfloor$ and $N=3^d$, $r_Ne\le\frac9{64}$, and \cref{thm:address} gives at least $\frac12N$ in both
directions.

\emph{Upper bound.} The kernel $K$ of $\Gamma_3\to\Z[1/3]^2\rtimes\Z$ is the direct limit of $K_0=E_Y\rtimes\FSym(Y)$ under $\varphi$, locally finite. At
stage $L$ put $p_L=t^{-L}pt^L$, $q_L=t^{-L}qt^L$, and for $R$ let $K_{L,R}\cong E_{3R}\rtimes\mathrm{Sym}_{3R}$, of order $2^{6R+1}(3R)!$, be the
subgroup generated by the lamps and finitary permutations of the three rays up to height $R$. Take $M=(L+1)3^{2L}$, $R=8M+8$, the
section $s(a,b,j)=t^jp_L^aq_L^b$ on $|a|,|b|\le M$, $|j|\le L$, extended consistently to the neighbours. Every edge with an endpoint in the
support has its cocycle in $K_{L,R}$: $t^{\pm1}$ and $p^{\pm1}$ edges have trivial cocycle, $q^{\pm1}$ edges a finitary word fixing points
above height $8M$, and $x,z$ edges blocks of original lamps of height at most $3M$; inward and outward boundary edges are included.
Induce the negative-$J$ part of the regular representation of $K_{L,R}$, with $D_0$ uniform on it, and put cosine amplitudes
$f_M(a)f_M(b)f_L(j)$ on the clocks. The $t$ error is at most $\pi/[2(L+1)]$, the $p,q$ errors at most $3^{2L}\pi/[2(M+1)]\le\pi/[2(L+1)]$ by
telescoping shifts, and the entropy at most $6R+\log((3R)!)+2\log(2M+1)+\log(2L+1)\le e^{CL}$. Take $L=\lceil\pi/(2e)\rceil$.
\end{proof}

Exponential order was already obtained by \cref{thm:exact-config}; this is a smaller alternative mechanism, compressing addresses by
replication instead of configurations, not a new growth exponent. Exact address length and approximate relation filling are
different resources: the scaling relator alone, $tpt^{-1}=p^3$ with $t=1$ and $p=e^{i\pi/(3^k-1)}$, has base defect $O(3^{-k})$ and
iterated defect $|t^kpt^{-k}-p^{3^k}|=2$. The inputs to \cref{prop:subset-presentation,thm:subset,thm:gamma3} from the literature are
Johnson's presentation of $H_3$, Cornulier's criterion and the standard HNN and induction machinery; the operational inputs are
\cref{thm:recovery,thm:address}.
