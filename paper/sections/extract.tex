%======================================================================
\section{Operational extraction on the original state}\label{sec:extract}
%======================================================================

The counting theorem bounds the entropy of Bob's reduced state, which for a mixed strategy is not a bound on usable entanglement
(\cref{sec:status}). This section supplies the operational tools. Each takes approximate relations among actual operators of the two players, measured on a
purification $\Psi_{ABE}$ of the original mixed state, and produces local isometries whose output either is close to a
maximally entangled state or has few Bell errors, so that hashing applies. Nothing acts on $E$, no pure-state ensemble
decomposition is used, and the state is never replaced. The mechanisms (regular dilations, coherent Pauli extraction,
pretty-good recovery, hashing) are established; what is new is the accounting that keeps a growing register on the
original state with errors summed in squares~\cite{GH17,NV17,McK16,DW05}.

\subsection{The actual source interface of a compiled game}\label{sec:interface}

A source enters a game through a compiler whose lower map sends each source generator $s$ to a fixed polynomial $F_s$ in
the game's variables (\cref{sec:compile}). On a strategy Bob evaluates $F_s$ on his variable reflections. Alice evaluates
the \emph{opposite} polynomial $\theta(F_s)$, obtained by reversing every word and substituting, for each variable $j$,
her coarse-grained reflection for $j$ in one fixed incident context $i(j)$. The map $\theta$ is a complex-linear
$*$-preserving antihomomorphism of word polynomials, and \cref{lem:transfer-parties}(a) gives, for every polynomial
$P=\sum_wc_ww$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:opposite-poly}
\|(P(B)-\theta(P)(A))\Psi\|\le\delta\sum_w|c_w||w|,\qquad\delta=\max_j\|(A_j-B_j)\Psi\|.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Actual source interface]\label{prop:interface}
Let $G$ be a finite Boolean constraint-variable game, every incidence tested with probability at least $\pi_{\min}>0$,
and let $s\mapsto F_s$ be a lower map from a presentation $\langle S\mid R\rangle$ with marked central involution $J$ into
the game algebra $\mathcal A(G)$ of \cref{ex:presented}, given by fixed polynomials such that $F$ is a unital
$*$-homomorphism of the negative quotient. There is a constant $C_G$ such that every finite-dimensional mixed strategy
of loss $\eps$, with purification $\Psi$ after local projective dilation, has Bob unitaries $U_s$ and Alice unitaries
$U_s^A$ with, for $e=C_G\sqrt\eps$,
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),itemsep=1pt]
\item $\|(U_s^A-U_s)\Psi\|\le e$ and $\|(U_s^{A*}-U_s^*)\Psi\|\le e$ for every $s$;
\item $\|(r(U)-I)\Psi\|\le e$ for every relator $r$, and $\|(r^{\mathrm{rev}}(U^A)-I)\Psi\|\le e$ for the reversed word
evaluated on Alice;
\item $\|(U_J+I)\Psi\|\le e$;
\item $\hs{[U_s,\rho_B^{1/2}]}\le e$ and $\hs{[U_s^A,\rho_A^{1/2}]}\le e$.
\end{enumerate}
If the $F_s$ are words in the variables, one may take $U_s=F_s(B)$ and $U_s^A=\theta(F_s)(A)$ without rounding.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Relabel every illegal Alice answer to one fixed legal answer of its context, which cannot increase the loss, and dilate
all measurements to projective ones with local pure ancillas, which changes neither the correlations nor the directional
rates. For a context $i$ Alice's coarse-grained reflections $A_{ij}$ then commute exactly and every forbidden ordered
product of their spectral projections vanishes. A tested incidence is lost with probability at most $\eps/\pi_{\min}$,
so $\|(A_{ij}-B_j)\Psi\|\le\delta:=2\sqrt{\eps/\pi_{\min}}$. Every game relator $Q$ (a context commutator or a forbidden
ordered product) is a word of bounded length in the variables of one context $i$; transferring it to that context's own
Alice reflections by \cref{lem:transfer-parties}(a), where it is zero or the identity exactly, gives
$\|Q(B)\Psi\|=O(\delta)$.

Every polynomial in the ideal of the game relations has a finite certificate $\sum_la_lQ_lb_l$. For a term $aQb$, move
$b(B)$ to Alice by~\eqref{eq:opposite-poly}, commute it past $Q(B)$, and use $\|\theta(b)(A)\|\le\sum|c_w|$:
$\|a(B)Q(B)b(B)\Psi\|=O(\delta)$. Hence every element of the ideal has Bob residual $O(\delta)$ on $\Psi$, with a
constant depending only on the fixed certificate, and by~\eqref{eq:opposite-poly} its opposite polynomial has Alice
residual $O(\delta)$. In particular this holds for $F_s^*F_s-1$, $F_sF_s^*-1$, for $r(F)-1$ with $r\in R$, and for
$F_J+1$. Since $\theta$ reverses products, $\theta(F_s^*F_s)=\theta(F_s)\theta(F_s)^*$ and the two unitarity defects
exchange roles on Alice; both are controlled.

Let $U_s$ be a unitary polar part of $F_s(B)$ and $U_s^A$ one of $\theta(F_s)(A)$. The scalar inequality
$|1-t|\le|1-t^2|$, $t\ge0$, gives $\|(U-F)\Psi\|\le\|(F^*F-I)\Psi\|$ and $\|(U^*-F^*)\Psi\|\le\|(FF^*-I)\Psi\|$ on any
vector (the argument of \cref{lem:polar}, which uses only functional calculus of $|F|$). So $U_s$ and $U_s^A$ are within
$O(\delta)$ of $F_s(B)$ and $\theta(F_s)(A)$, together with their adjoints, and (a) follows from~\eqref{eq:opposite-poly}.
For (b) and (c), replace the letters of $r(U)$ one at a time, from the right, by the polynomials $F_s(B)$ or $F_s(B)^*$. At
each step the already replaced suffix is a polynomial $S(B)$ of fixed coefficient norm and degree, not necessarily unitary on
the strategy. Move it to Alice by~\eqref{eq:opposite-poly}, at cost $O(\delta)$ because the replacement error to its left is
bounded. Then $\theta(S)(A)$, of norm at most the coefficient norm of $S$, commutes with that replacement error, which is
$O(\delta)$ on $\Psi$. So each step costs $O(\delta)$, and $r(F)-1$ has residual $O(\delta)$. The reversed Alice words are
handled in the same way, with the parties exchanged. Part (d) is
\cref{lem:transfer-parties}(d). All constants depend only on $G$, the presentation and the chosen certificates.
\end{proof}

The same proof applies to linear system games, whose game relations are the parity rows and context commutators, and to
the corner maps of \cref{prop:corner}. The orientation matters: Alice realizes the opposite algebra. If Bob has
$TY=\zeta YT$, Alice has $T_AY_A=\zeta^{-1}Y_AT_A$ in the corresponding reversed words, and the invariant tensor of a
generator is $U_s^{A*}\otimes U_s$, not $U_s^A\otimes U_s$.

\paragraph{Selector polynomials.} The proof never uses that $F_s$ is the image of a generator, only that its unitarity defects lie in
the game ideal. Let $T$ be a fixed polynomial in the source generators such that $T^*T-1$ and $TT^*-1$ lie in the defining ideal of
the negative quotient, and let $T_F$ be the polynomial in the game variables obtained by substituting $F_s$ for each generator $s$.
Since $F$ is a homomorphism of the negative quotient, the two unitarity defects of $T_F$ lie in the game ideal. The certificate and
polar argument above then gives, verbatim: Bob and Alice residuals $O(\delta)$ for the unitarity defects of $T_F$ and of $\theta(T_F)$;
the mirror $\|(T_F(B)-\theta(T_F)(A))\Psi\|=O(\delta)$, by~\eqref{eq:opposite-poly}; unitary polar parts $U_T$ of $T_F(B)$ and $U_T^A$
of $\theta(T_F)(A)$, within $O(\delta)$ of them together with their adjoints, so that $U_T^A$ is an actual mirror of $U_T$, and
$U_T^{A*}$ of $U_T^*$, with error $O(e)$; square-root-state commutators $O(e)$, by \cref{lem:transfer-parties}(d); and residual
$O(e)$ for every word in $U_T,U_T^*$ and the $U_s,U_s^*$ whose image in the polynomials lies in the game ideal, replacing letters from
the right as in~(b), with the reversed words on Alice. In particular, if $Ty=\zeta yT$ in the negative quotient for a generator $y$,
then $\|(U_TU_yU_T^*-\zeta U_y)\Psi\|=O(e)$, and on Alice $\|(U_T^AU_y^AU_T^{A*}-\zeta^{-1}U_y^A)\Psi\|=O(e)$; and if $T$ commutes with a
generator $g$ in the negative quotient, then $\|[U_T,U_g]\Psi\|$ and $\|[U_T^A,U_g^A]\Psi\|$ are $O(e)$. The constants depend only on
$G$, the presentation, $T$ and the chosen certificates. This applies to the corner maps and linear system games as well, and it is
used for the rotation selectors of \cref{lem:rotation-selector} in \cref{thm:rational-ED}.

\subsection{Triangular extraction}\label{sec:triangular}

\begin{theorem}[Triangular extraction]\label{thm:triangular}
Let $\Psi$ be a unit vector in $H_A\otimes H_B\otimes H_E$ and $M\ge1$. On each party let $Z_j,T_j$, $0\le j<M$, be
reflections such that, for some $\delta\ge0$,
\begin{gather*}
\|(Z_j^A-Z_j^B)\Psi\|\le\delta,\qquad\|(T_j^A-T_j^B)\Psi\|\le\delta,\\
\|[Z_j^A,Z_s^A]\Psi\|\le\delta,\quad\|[T_j^A,Z_s^A]\Psi\|\le\delta\quad(s<j),\qquad\|\{T_j^A,Z_j^A\}\Psi\|\le\delta .
\end{gather*}
No relation between different $T_j$ is assumed. There are product local isometries $W_A,W_B$ and a unit vector $\chi$,
including $E$, with
\[
\|(W_A\otimes W_B\otimes I_E)\Psi-\Phi_{2^M}\otimes\chi\|\le\min\{2,20M^3\delta\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Histories.} Put $P^{a}_j=\frac12(I+(-1)^aZ_j)$ on each party. For $h=(a_0,\dots,a_{j-1})$ let
$K_h^A=P^{A,a_{j-1}}_{j-1}\cdots P^{A,a_0}_0$ (chronological order) and $Q_h^B=P^{B,a_0}_0\cdots P^{B,a_{j-1}}_{j-1}$ (the
reversed order). Summing the innermost equal pair of projections repeatedly gives
$\sum_hK_h^*K_h=\sum_hK_hK_h^*=I$, and the same for $Q$, with no commutation. Put
$\Delta_j=(\sum_h\|(K_h^A-Q_h^B)\Psi\|^2)^{1/2}$ and $z_s=\|(Z^A_s-Z^B_s)\Psi\|$. From
$P_s^AK_h^A-Q_h^BP_s^B=P^A_s(K^A_h-Q^B_h)+Q^B_h(P^A_s-P^B_s)$, summing the new outcome with $\sum_a(P^a_s)^2=I$ in the first
term and the old histories with $\sum_hQ_h^*Q_h=I$ in the second, Minkowski's inequality gives
$\Delta_{s+1}\le\Delta_s+z_s/\sqrt2$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tri-hist}
\Delta_j\le\sum_{s<j}z_s/\sqrt2\le j\delta/\sqrt2 .
\end{equation}
For an operator $F$ on Alice, $FK^A_h\Psi=Q^B_hF\Psi+F(K^A_h-Q^B_h)\Psi$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tri-suffix}
\Bigl(\sum_h\|FK^A_h\Psi\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\le\|F\Psi\|+\opn F\Delta_j ,
\end{equation}
and for a finite family $F_a$ the same holds with $(\sum_a\|F_a\Psi\|^2)^{1/2}+(\sum_a\opn{F_a}^2)^{1/2}\Delta_j$. There is
no factor of the number of histories.

\emph{Commutators along histories.} For a reflection $G$ on Alice put $C_{G,j}=(\sum_h\|[G,K_h^A]\Psi\|^2)^{1/2}$.
Telescoping $[G,P_{j-1}\cdots P_0]$, summing the later outcomes by completeness and applying~\eqref{eq:tri-suffix} to
the pair $F_a=[G,P^a_s]=\frac{(-1)^a}2[G,Z_s]$, of operator norm at most one,
\[
C_{G,j}\le\sum_{s<j}\bigl(\|[G,Z^A_s]\Psi\|/\sqrt2+\sqrt2\Delta_s\bigr)\le j\delta/\sqrt2+j(j-1)\delta/2\le(j+1)^2\delta-\delta
\]
for $G=Z_j$ or $T_j$. The mirror defect $G_A-G_B$ is bipartite and is not substituted into~\eqref{eq:tri-suffix};
instead $(G_A-G_B)K_h^A\Psi=K_h^A(G_A-G_B)\Psi+[G_A,K_h^A]\Psi$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tri-mirror}
\Bigl(\sum_h\|(G_A-G_B)K_h^A\Psi\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\le(j+1)^2\delta\qquad(G=Z_j,T_j).
\end{equation}
The conjugacy defect $C_j=T_jZ_jT_j+Z_j=T_j\{T_j,Z_j\}$ on Alice has norm at most two and $\|C_j\Psi\|\le\delta$, so
by~\eqref{eq:tri-suffix} its aggregate over histories is at most $\delta+2\Delta_j\le(2j+1)\delta$.

\emph{One stage.} For a target bit $a$ let $W_j^{a}=\sum_{b=0,1}|b\rangle\otimes T_j^{a\oplus b}P_j^b$ on each party; it is
an isometry because $T_j$ is unitary. Consider
\[
\mathcal A_j\eta=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\sum_a|a,a,a\rangle\otimes(W_j^{A,a}\otimes W_j^{B,a})\eta,\qquad
\mathcal I_j\eta=\sum_a|a,a,a\rangle\otimes\Phi_2\otimes P_j^{A,a}\eta .
\]
The first is the Stinespring form of a shared uniform random target, one copy of which is kept by the environment,
followed by local isometries; the second is an exact isometry used only in the analysis. For any vector $\eta$ put
$t=\|(T^A_j-T^B_j)\eta\|$, $z=\|(Z^A_j-Z^B_j)\eta\|$ and $c=\|C_j\eta\|$. The branch of $\mathcal A_j\eta$ with flags
$(a,b,c')$ is $2^{-1/2}T_A^{a\oplus b}P^b_AT_B^{a\oplus c'}P^{c'}_B\eta$; transferring the Bob factor to Alice by
\cref{lem:transfer-parties}(a) costs $t+z/2$ and gives $2^{-1/2}T^{a\oplus b}P^bP^{c'}T^{a\oplus c'}\eta$ on Alice. If
$b\ne c'$ this is exactly zero, as $P^bP^{c'}=0$; if $b=c'=a$ it is $P^a\eta$; and if $b=c'\ne a$ it is
$TP^{1-a}T\eta=P^a\eta\mp\frac12C_j\eta$. The ideal branch is $2^{-1/2}1_{b=c'}P^a_A\eta$. Over the eight orthogonal branches,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tri-stage}
\|(\mathcal A_j-\mathcal I_j)\eta\|\le2t+z+c .
\end{equation}

\emph{Telescoping.} Compose the stages $j=0,\dots,M-1$ and compare $\mathcal A_{M-1}\cdots\mathcal A_0$ with
$\mathcal I_{M-1}\cdots\mathcal I_0$ by telescoping, with actual maps on the left (isometries) and ideal prefixes on the
right. The ideal prefix after $j$ stages is a direct sum over histories $h$ of $\Phi_2^{\otimes j}\otimes K^A_h\Psi$, so
by~\eqref{eq:tri-stage}, \eqref{eq:tri-mirror} and the conjugacy aggregate, the $j$-th term is at most
$3(j+1)^2\delta+(2j+1)\delta\le5(j+1)^2\delta$. Summing, the actual composite is within $5M^3\delta$ of
$\Phi_{2^M}\otimes\chi$, $\chi=\sum_a|a,a,a\rangle\otimes K^A_a\Psi$, a unit vector by completeness.

\emph{A deterministic target.} For each target string $a$ let $v_a$ be the output of the local isometries with that target,
a unit vector. The squared error just bounded is $2^{-M}\sum_a\|v_a-\Phi_{2^M}\otimes2^{M/2}K^A_a\Psi\|^2$, so some $a$ has
$\|v_a-\Phi_{2^M}\otimes w\|\le5M^3\delta$ with $w=2^{M/2}K^A_a\Psi$; if this is below one, replacing $w$ by $w/\|w\|$
at most doubles it. The trivial bound $2$ covers the remaining case.
\end{proof}

The triangular matrix-unit identities hold exactly in the source algebra but only approximately on the actual state, and
the reversed orientation of the histories on the two parties is essential; with the same orientation on both sides the
estimate~\eqref{eq:tri-hist} fails.

The same argument works with cyclic labels of bounded size and unitary controls of arbitrary order. Errors indexed by labels are
measured by their \emph{root aggregates}, the square roots of the sums of their squares over the labels.

\begin{lemma}[Cyclic triangular extraction]\label{lem:cyclic-triangular}
Let $\Psi$ be a unit vector in $H_A\otimes H_B\otimes H_E$, $Q\ge2$ and $M\ge1$. For $0\le j<M$ let $2\le q_j\le Q$, and on each party
$P\in\{A,B\}$ let $\{P^P_{j,a}:a\in\Z_{q_j}\}$ be a projective partition and $U^P_j$ a unitary. Suppose that, for some $\delta\ge0$, all
of the following root aggregates on $\Psi$ are at most $\delta$, for every $j$, every $t<j$ and every integer $h$ with $|h|\le Q$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=1pt]
\item the mirrors $(\sum_a\|(P^A_{j,a}-P^B_{j,a})\Psi\|^2)^{1/2}$ and $\|((U^A_j)^h-(U^B_j)^h)\Psi\|$;
\item on Alice, $(\sum_{a,b}\|[P^A_{j,a},P^A_{t,b}]\Psi\|^2)^{1/2}$ and $(\sum_b\|[(U^A_j)^h,P^A_{t,b}]\Psi\|^2)^{1/2}$;
\item on Alice, $(\sum_a\|((U^A_j)^hP^A_{j,a}(U^A_j)^{-h}-P^A_{j,a+h})\Psi\|^2)^{1/2}$, the labels taken modulo $q_j$.
\end{enumerate}
No relation between controls of different stages and no relation $(U^P_j)^{q_j}=I$ is assumed. There are a constant $C_Q$, depending
only on $Q$, product local isometries $W_A,W_B$ and a unit vector $\chi$, including $E$, with
\[
\|(W_A\otimes W_B\otimes I_E)\Psi-\Phi_{q_0q_1\cdots q_{M-1}}\otimes\chi\|\le\min\{2,C_QM^3\delta\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We follow the proof of \cref{thm:triangular} and indicate the changes; $C_Q$ denotes constants depending only on $Q$.

\emph{Histories.} For a history $h=(a_0,\dots,a_{j-1})$ put $K^A_h=P^A_{j-1,a_{j-1}}\cdots P^A_{0,a_0}$ and
$Q^B_h=P^B_{0,a_0}\cdots P^B_{j-1,a_{j-1}}$. Summing the innermost partition repeatedly gives $\sum_hK_h^*K_h=\sum_hK_hK_h^*=I$, and
the same for $Q$, with no commutation. With $\Delta_j=(\sum_h\|(K^A_h-Q^B_h)\Psi\|^2)^{1/2}$, the identity $P^A_{j,a}K^A_h-Q^B_hP^B_{j,a}=P^A_{j,a}(K^A_h-Q^B_h)+
Q^B_h(P^A_{j,a}-P^B_{j,a})$, completeness in each term and Minkowski's inequality give $\Delta_{j+1}\le\Delta_j+\delta$, so
$\Delta_j\le j\delta$. As in~\eqref{eq:tri-suffix}, for a finite family $F_b$ of Alice operators,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyc-suffix}
\Bigl(\sum_{h,b}\|F_bK^A_h\Psi\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\le\Bigl(\sum_b\|F_b\Psi\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}+\Bigl(\sum_b\opn{F_b}^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\Delta_j .
\end{equation}

\emph{Commutators and mirrors along histories.} Let $G$ be $(U^A_j)^n$ with $|n|\le Q$, or the family $(P^A_{j,a})_a$. Telescoping
$[G,K^A_h]$ over the earlier stages $s<j$, summing the later outcomes by completeness, and applying~\eqref{eq:cyc-suffix} to the family
$[G,P^A_{s,b}]$, whose operator norms are at most two and whose number is at most $Q^2$, gives by~(ii)
\[
\Bigl(\sum_h\|[G,K^A_h]\Psi\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\le\sum_{s<j}(\delta+2Q\Delta_s)\le C_Q(j+1)^2\delta ,
\]
summed over $a$ as well when $G$ is the family. Since the Bob operator $G_B$ commutes with $K^A_h$,
$(G_A-G_B)K^A_h\Psi=K^A_h(G_A-G_B)\Psi+[G_A,K^A_h]\Psi$, and completeness and~(i) give the history aggregate
$C_Q(j+1)^2\delta$ for the mirrors of $P_{j,\cdot}$ and of $(U_j)^n$. The covariance defects of~(iii) have operator norm at most two,
so by~\eqref{eq:cyc-suffix} their history aggregate is at most $C_Q(j+1)\delta$.

\emph{One stage.} Drop the index $j$. For a target label $t\in\Z_q$ put
\[
W_A^t=\sum_{b\in\Z_q}|b\rangle\otimes(U^A)^{t-b}P^A_b,\qquad W_B^t=\sum_{c\in\Z_q}|c\rangle\otimes(U^B)^{c-t}P^B_c ,
\]
with exponents taken in $\{-q+1,\dots,q-1\}$; each is an exact isometry, since the recorded label makes the cross terms orthogonal.
Consider
\[
\mathcal A\eta=q^{-1/2}\sum_t|t,t,t\rangle\otimes(W_A^t\otimes W_B^t)\eta,\qquad\mathcal I\eta=\sum_t|t,t,t\rangle\otimes\Phi_q\otimes P^A_t\eta ;
\]
the first is the Stinespring form of a shared uniform random target, one copy of which is kept by the environment, followed by local
isometries, and the second is an exact isometry used only in the analysis. The branch of $\mathcal A\eta$ with flags $(t,b,c)$ is
$q^{-1/2}(U^A)^{t-b}P^A_b(U^B)^{c-t}P^B_c\eta$, and the ideal branch is $q^{-1/2}1_{b=c}P^A_t\eta$. Transferring the Bob word to Alice in
reverse order,
\[
(U^B)^{c-t}P^B_c\eta=P^A_c(U^A)^{c-t}\eta+(U^B)^{c-t}(P^B_c-P^A_c)\eta+P^A_c\bigl((U^B)^{c-t}-(U^A)^{c-t}\bigr)\eta ,
\]
splits each branch, after the Alice factor $q^{-1/2}(U^A)^{t-b}P^A_b$, into three parts. The first is $q^{-1/2}(U^A)^{t-b}P^A_bP^A_c(U^A)^{c-t}\eta$.
It vanishes exactly for $b\ne c$, and for $b=c$ it is $q^{-1/2}(U^A)^{t-b}P^A_b(U^A)^{b-t}\eta$, which differs from the ideal branch by $q^{-1/2}$
times a covariance defect of~(iii) with exponent $n=t-b$ and label $b$. The second, the projection-mirror part, is
$q^{-1/2}(U^A)^{t-b}P^A_bv_{tc}$ with $v_{tc}=(U^B)^{c-t}(P^B_c-P^A_c)\eta$. The unrecorded vectors $(U^A)^{t-b}P^A_bv$ need not be orthogonal
as $b$ varies (for $q=2$, $t=0$, $U^A=X$, $P^A_b=|b\rangle\langle b|$ and $v=|{+}\rangle$ both are $|0\rangle/\sqrt2$), but unitarity and
completeness give
\[
\sum_b\|(U^A)^{t-b}P^A_bv\|^2=\sum_b\|P^A_bv\|^2=\|v\|^2
\]
for every vector $v$. So the projection-mirror parts have squared norms summing over $(t,b,c)$ to $\sum_c\|(P^A_c-P^B_c)\eta\|^2$. The third,
the unitary-mirror part, vanishes unless $b=c$, and the pairs $(t,c)$ with $c-t=n$ number $q-|n|\le q$, so these parts have squared norms
summing to at most $\sum_{|n|<q}\|((U^A)^n-(U^B)^n)\eta\|^2$. In the same way the covariance parts have squared norms summing to at most
$q^{-1}\sum_{|n|<q}\sum_b\|((U^A)^nP^A_b(U^A)^{-n}-P^A_{b+n})\eta\|^2$. The output branches carry orthogonal recorded labels $(t,b,c)$, so
$\|(\mathcal A-\mathcal I)\eta\|^2$ is the sum of the squared branch errors, and Minkowski's inequality for the projection-mirror, unitary-mirror
and covariance contributions gives
\begin{multline}\label{eq:cyc-stage}
\|(\mathcal A-\mathcal I)\eta\|\le C_Q\Bigl[\Bigl(\sum_c\|(P^A_c-P^B_c)\eta\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}+\sum_{|n|<q}\|((U^A)^n-(U^B)^n)\eta\|\\
+\sum_{|n|<q}\Bigl(\sum_b\|((U^A)^nP^A_b(U^A)^{-n}-P^A_{b+n})\eta\|^2\Bigr)^{1/2}\Bigr],
\end{multline}
here with $C_Q=1$.

\emph{Telescoping and a deterministic target.} The ideal prefix after $j$ stages is a direct sum over histories $h$ of
$\Phi_{q_0\cdots q_{j-1}}\otimes K^A_h\Psi$, with orthogonal flags, so the squared error of stage $j$ on it is the sum over $h$ of the
squared errors~\eqref{eq:cyc-stage} at $\eta=K^A_h\Psi$. Minkowski's inequality over the at most $4Q$ terms of~\eqref{eq:cyc-stage}, with
the history aggregates, bounds the $j$-th term of the telescoping sum by $C_Q(j+1)^2\delta$, so the actual composite is within $C_QM^3\delta$ of
$\Phi_{q_0\cdots q_{M-1}}\otimes\chi$, $\chi=\sum_h|h,h,h\rangle\otimes K^A_h\Psi$, a unit vector by completeness; here $|h,h,h\rangle$ records the
target string in three copies. For each target string $t$ let $v_t$ be the output of the local isometries with that target. The squared
error just bounded is $(q_0\cdots q_{M-1})^{-1}\sum_t\|v_t-\Phi\otimes(q_0\cdots q_{M-1})^{1/2}K^A_t\Psi\|^2$, so some $t$ has
$\|v_t-\Phi\otimes w\|\le C_QM^3\delta$ with $w=(q_0\cdots q_{M-1})^{1/2}K^A_t\Psi$; if this is below one, normalizing $w$ at most doubles
it, and the trivial bound $2$ covers the remaining case.
\end{proof}

For $q_j=2$ and reflections $U_j$ the hypotheses agree with those of \cref{thm:triangular} up to constant factors, with $Z_j=P_{j,0}-P_{j,1}$:
the covariance~(iii) has aggregate $\|\{U_j,Z_j\}\Psi\|/\sqrt2$. The lemma is the finite-alphabet form of that theorem, not a new
self-testing or hashing mechanism.

\subsection{Signed regular extraction}\label{sec:signed-regular}

Let $Q_1,\dots,Q_{2N}$ be the labels $X_1,\dots,X_N,Z_1,\dots,Z_N$, and let $s_{jk}=-1$ if $\{Q_j,Q_k\}$ is a pair
$\{X_i,Z_i\}$ and $s_{jk}=+1$ otherwise. On the label register $\ell^2(\{0,1\}^{2N})$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:label-paulis}
R_k|v\rangle=r_k(v)|v+e_k\rangle,\qquad r_k(v)=\prod_{j>k}s_{jk}^{v_j}.
\end{equation}
These are real reflections with $R_jR_k=s_{jk}R_kR_j$ exactly. Writing $v=(a,b)$, $R_{X_i}=S_{a_i}F_{b_i}$ and
$R_{Z_i}=S_{b_i}$, with $S$ a bit flip and $F$ its sign; for one pair the real unitary $|z\rangle|t\rangle\mapsto
|t+z\rangle H|z\rangle$ conjugates $(R_X,R_Z)$ to $(X\otimes I,Z\otimes I)$. So the label algebra is exactly
$M_2^{\otimes N}$ with multiplicity $2^N$, by a fixed real identification.

\begin{theorem}[Signed regular extraction]\label{thm:signed-regular}
Let $\Psi$ purify $\rho_{AB}$, and on each party $P\in\{A,B\}$ let $Q^P_1,\dots,Q^P_{2N}$ be reflections. Put
\[
\eta_{jk,P}=\|(Q^P_jQ^P_k-s_{jk}Q^P_kQ^P_j)\Psi\|,\quad\mu_j=\|(Q^A_j-Q^B_j)\Psi\|,\quad c_{j,P}=\hs{[Q^P_j,\rho_P^{1/2}]},
\]
and $D_{k,P}=4\sum_{j>k}[(\eta_{jk,P}+c_{j,P})^2+c_{j,P}^2]$. There are exact local isometries $V_A,V_B$, with
$N$ output qubits per party after the fixed identification, whose $k$-th Bell stabilizer fails with probability
\[
p_k\le\bigl(\sqrt{D_{k,A}}+\sqrt{D_{k,B}}+\mu_k\bigr)^2/4 .
\]
Consequently $\ED^{A\to B}(\rho_{AB})\ge N-\sum_{k=1}^{2N}h(p_k)$, where upper bounds may be substituted for the $p_k$ in
the monotone range $p_k\le\frac12$. If every signed pair error, state commutator and mirror error is at most $\kappa$,
then $p_k\le25N\kappa^2$, and
\[
\ED^{A\to B}(\rho_{AB})\ge N\bigl[1-2h(25N\kappa^2)\bigr]\qquad\text{when }25N\kappa^2\le\tfrac12 .
\]
No independence of the errors is assumed, and these are bounds for the original mixed state.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $v\in\{0,1\}^{2N}$ put $f_P(v)=Q_1^{v_1}\cdots Q_{2N}^{v_{2N}}$ and $V_Pu=2^{-N}\sum_vf_P(v)u\otimes|v\rangle$, an exact
isometry since each $f_P(v)$ is unitary. With $\xi=\rho_P^{1/2}$ and $d_{k,P}=\hs{(R_kV_P-V_PQ_k)\xi}$, the coefficient
of $|v\rangle$ is $2^{-N}(r_k(v)f(v+e_k)-f(v)Q_k)\xi$, and writing $f(v)=LQ_k^{v_k}T$ with the suffix
$T=Q_{k+1}^{v_{k+1}}\cdots Q_{2N}^{v_{2N}}$, left unitary cancellation gives
\[
d_{k,P}^2=\mathbb E_v\hs{(r_k(v)T^*Q_kT-Q_k)\xi}^2 .
\]
Put $u=Q_k\xi$. Then $(r_kT^*Q_kT-Q_k)\xi=(r_kT^*uT-u)+r_kT^*Q_kT(\xi-T^*\xi T)$. The signed conjugations
$H\mapsto s_{jk}Q_jHQ_j$ are self-adjoint unitaries of the unweighted Hilbert--Schmidt space, so
\cref{lem:twirl}, with signs $s_{jk}$, gives $\mathbb E_v\hs{r_kT^*uT-u}^2\le\sum_{j>k}\hs{Q_ju-s_{jk}uQ_j}^2$ and
$\mathbb E_v\hs{T^*\xi T-\xi}^2\le\sum_{j>k}c_{j,P}^2$. Since $Q_jQ_k\xi-s_{jk}Q_k\xi Q_j=(Q_jQ_k-s_{jk}Q_kQ_j)\xi+s_{jk}Q_k
[Q_j,\xi]$ and $\hs{(\cdot)\xi}=\|(\cdot)\Psi\|$ by \cref{lem:standard-form},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sr-intertwine}
d_{k,P}^2\le2\sum_{j>k}(\eta_{jk,P}+c_{j,P})^2+2\sum_{j>k}c_{j,P}^2=\tfrac12D_{k,P}.
\end{equation}
For $\omega=(V_A\otimes V_B\otimes I_E)\Psi$, opposite-party maps commute, so
$\|(R^A_k-R^B_k)\omega\|\le d_{k,A}+d_{k,B}+\mu_k$, and for opposite-party reflections the squared difference norm is
four times the failure probability of their product. This proves the bound on $p_k$, a fortiori with $D_{k,P}$ in place
of $D_{k,P}/2$. After the fixed real identification and discarding the local junk (the original systems and the label
multiplicity), apply~\eqref{eq:bell-hashing}. In the uniform case use the sharper intertwiner estimate~\eqref{eq:sr-intertwine},
$d_{k,P}^2\le2T_{k,P}$ with $T_{k,P}=\sum_{j>k}[(\eta_{jk,P}+c_{j,P})^2+c_{j,P}^2]$, not the looser $D_{k,P}=4T_{k,P}$ of the statement:
there are fewer than $2N$ indices $j>k$, each with $(\eta_{jk,P}+c_{j,P})^2\le(2\kappa)^2$ and $c_{j,P}^2\le\kappa^2$, so
\[
d_{k,P}^2\le2(2N)\bigl[(2\kappa)^2+\kappa^2\bigr]=20N\kappa^2,\qquad p_k\le\frac{(2\sqrt{20N}+1)^2\kappa^2}4\le25N\kappa^2,
\]
since $(2\sqrt{20N}+1)^2\le100N$ for $N\ge1$. The constant $25$ is not obtained from $D_{k,P}$, which is already loosened by a factor two.
\end{proof}

The square-root-state commutators cannot be omitted; for reflections they are supplied by actual mirrors through
\cref{lem:transfer-parties}(d), $c_{j,P}\le\sqrt2\mu_j$. A whole-register fidelity bound would add the $2N$ failure
probabilities and lose a factor $N$; hashing uses only their entropy. For the qualitative law of \cref{sec:law} a
fixed-size version suffices, which needs no state commutators.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-size signed regular extraction]\label{lem:fixed-size}
Let $m=2r$ and $C_r=m(1+2m)$. If reflections $Q^P_1,\dots,Q^P_m$ on each party satisfy $\eta_{jk,P}\le\kappa$ and
$\mu_j\le\kappa$, then the isometries of \cref{thm:signed-regular} give Bell-stabilizer failures at most
$p=(2C_r+1)^2\kappa^2/4$, singlet infidelity at most $2rp$, and a unit vector $\chi$ with
$\|(V_A\otimes V_B\otimes I)\Psi-\Phi_{2^r}\otimes\chi\|\le\sqrt{4rp}$. If $p\le\frac12$, then
$\ED^{A\to B}(\rho_{AB})\ge r[1-2h(p)]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Move $Q_k$ through the suffix $T$ letter by letter from the right. A swap with $Q_j$ leaves the pair defect
$Q_jQ_k-s_{jk}Q_kQ_j$, of norm at most two, followed by a suffix of at most $m$ reflections; by
\cref{lem:transfer-parties}(b) its contribution on $\Psi$ is at most $\kappa+2m\kappa$. So each label block of
$(R_kV_P-V_PQ_k)\Psi$ has norm at most $C_r\kappa$ times its weight, and $\|(R_kV_P-V_PQ_k)\Psi\|\le C_r\kappa$. Hence
$\|(R^A_k-R^B_k)\omega\|\le(2C_r+1)\kappa$. The $2r$ Bell stabilizers commute and their common $+1$ space is
$\Phi_{2^r}$, so the infidelity is at most $2rp$, and the normalized projection of $\omega$ onto the Bell factor is within
$\sqrt{2\cdot2rp}$ of $\omega$. The last claim is~\eqref{eq:bell-hashing}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Aggregate and nonabelian bounds}\label{sec:aggregate}

\begin{theorem}[Aggregate signed extraction]\label{thm:aggregate}
In the setting of \cref{thm:signed-regular} put $E_B=\sum_{k<j}\eta_{jk,B}^2$ and $M_2=\sum_j\mu_j^2$. The average
Bell-stabilizer failure of the extracted $N$ pairs is at most
\[
b_N=9E_B/N+132M_2,
\]
and if $b_N\le\frac12$ then $\ED^{A\to B}(\rho_{AB})\ge N[1-2h(b_N)]$. A negative lower bound may be replaced by zero.
This is an entropy-density statement, not a full-register fidelity theorem at the same scale.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $m=2N$, $E_P=\sum_{k<j}\eta_{jk,P}^2$ and $C_P=\sum_j(j-1)c_{j,P}^2$. From the definition of $D_{k,P}$ and
$(a+b)^2\le2a^2+2b^2$, $\sum_kD_{k,P}\le8E_P+12C_P$, and so $\sum_kp_k\le6(E_A+E_B)+9(C_A+C_B)+\frac34M_2$. By
\cref{lem:transfer-parties}(d), $c_{j,P}\le\sqrt2\mu_j$, hence $C_A+C_B\le4(m-1)M_2$; transferring the two-letter products
to the other party, $\eta_{jk,A}\le\eta_{jk,B}+2(\mu_j+\mu_k)$, hence $E_A\le2E_B+16(m-1)M_2$. So $\sum_kp_k\le18E_B+132mM_2$.
Divide by $m$, use concavity of $h$, and apply~\eqref{eq:bell-hashing}.
\end{proof}

Now let $G$ be a finite group and, on each party, let $u^P_1,\dots,u^P_N$ be \emph{exact} unitary representations of $G$,
not assumed to commute for different $i$, oriented so that actual consistency reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:group-mirror}
\|(u^A_i(g)-u^B_i(g^{-1}))\Psi\|\le\mu\qquad(g\in G),
\end{equation}
which is the right orientation for nonabelian $G$, Alice representing the opposite algebra. On $\ell^2(G)$ use the right
regular representation and a unitary Fourier decomposition $\ell^2(G)=\bigoplus_\lambda V_\lambda\otimes\C^{d_\lambda}$.
For $d\ge2$ let $F_d(t)=h(\min(t,1-d^{-2}))+\min(t,1-d^{-2})\log(d^2-1)$, the largest entropy of a $d^2$-valued Bell error
of failure probability at most $t$, which saturates at $2\log d$; put $F_1=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-group extraction]\label{thm:group-extract}
Suppose, on both parties, $\hs{[u^P_i(h),u^P_j(g)]\rho_P^{1/2}}\le\kappa$ for $i\ne j$, $\hs{[u^P_j(g),\rho_P^{1/2}]}\le\kappa$,
and $\mu\le\kappa$. There are exact local regular isometries and a one-way protocol in which Alice measures and announces
the central types $\alpha_i$ of her label factors, such that, with $a_{i\lambda}$ the probability of type $\lambda$ at
coordinate $i$ and $q_{i\lambda}\le a_{i\lambda}$ the unnormalized failure of the invariant projector there,
\[
\ED^{A\to B}(\rho_{AB})\ \ge\ \sum_{i,\lambda}a_{i\lambda}\bigl[\log d_\lambda-F_{d_\lambda}(q_{i\lambda}/a_{i\lambda})\bigr],
\qquad\sum_\lambda q_{i\lambda}\le50N\kappa^2 .
\]
Zero-weight summands are zero, and Alice may discard any coordinate-type branch whose summand is negative. If only Bob
cross-commutators are bounded, by $\eta$, then $\kappa=\eta+4\mu$ is admissible.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $x\in G^N$ put $f_P(x)=u^P_1(x_1)\cdots u^P_N(x_N)$ and $W_Pv=|G|^{-N/2}\sum_xf_P(x)v\otimes|x\rangle$, an exact isometry.
With $R_i(h)|x\rangle=|x_1,\dots,x_ih^{-1},\dots,x_N\rangle$, the coefficient of $|x\rangle$ in $R_i(h)W_P$ is $f_P(xh_i)$,
and for the suffix $T=u_{i+1}(x_{i+1})\cdots u_N(x_N)$ left cancellation gives
$\delta_{i,P}(h)^2:=\hs{(R_i(h)W_P-W_Pu_i(h))\xi}^2=\mathbb E_T\hs{(T^*u_i(h)T-u_i(h))\xi}^2$. The maps
$A_j(H)=\mathbb E_gu_j(g)^*Hu_j(g)$ are orthogonal projections on the Hilbert--Schmidt space, and
$\|(I-A_j)z\|^2=\frac12\mathbb E_g\hs{[u_j(g),z]}^2$. The projection-product inequality (\cref{lem:projection-product})
gives $\mathbb E_T\hs{T^*zT-z}^2\le2\sum_{j>i}\mathbb E_g\hs{[u_j(g),z]}^2$. Applied to $z=u_i(h)\xi$ and to $\xi$, as in
\cref{thm:signed-regular}, this gives $\delta_{i,P}(h)^2\le20N\kappa^2$. For $\omega=(W_A\otimes W_B\otimes I)\Psi$,
$\|(R^A_i(g)-R^B_i(g^{-1}))\omega\|\le(2\sqrt{20N}+1)\kappa$. The diagonal invariant projector
$\Pi_i=\mathbb E_gR^A_i(g)\otimes R^B_i(g)$ has failure
$q_i=\langle\omega,(I-\Pi_i)\omega\rangle=\frac12\mathbb E_g\|(R^A_i(g)-R^B_i(g^{-1}))\omega\|^2\le50N\kappa^2$.

Let $P^A_{i\lambda}$ be Alice's central type projections, $a_{i\lambda}=\langle\omega,P^A_{i\lambda}\omega\rangle$ and
$q_{i\lambda}=\langle\omega,P^A_{i\lambda}(I-\Pi_i)\omega\rangle$; they commute, so $0\le q_{i\lambda}\le a_{i\lambda}$ and
$\sum_\lambda q_{i\lambda}=q_i$. By Schur's lemma, on the matching blocks $(\lambda,\bar\lambda)$
\[
\Pi_i=\bigoplus_\lambda|\Phi_{d_\lambda}\rangle\langle\Phi_{d_\lambda}|\otimes I_{\text{multiplicities}},
\]
and $\Pi_i$ vanishes on the other pairs of types; Bob's matching type is the contragredient $\bar\lambda$. The protocol:
both parties measure their central types; Alice announces her string $\alpha$; Alice keeps the payload $V_{\alpha_i}$ of
each coordinate and discards her multiplicities and the original system; Bob keeps and aligns his payload where
$\beta_i=\bar\alpha_i$ and otherwise replaces it by a dummy of dimension $d_{\alpha_i}$; Alice then samples generalized
Weyl twirls on every coordinate and announces them; finally hashing~\eqref{eq:hashing}. All messages go from Alice to Bob,
and there is no acceptance test on the whole string. Pulling the Bell-failure effect of coordinate $i$ back through these
steps gives an effect at most $P^A_{i\lambda}(I-\Pi_i)$ on the type-$\lambda$ branch, so the conditional failure
$t_{i\lambda}$ satisfies $a_{i\lambda}t_{i\lambda}\le q_{i\lambda}$. Conditional on $\alpha$ the twirled payload is diagonal in
the tensor product of the generalized Bell bases, possibly with correlated error labels, and Bob's marginal is maximally mixed,
of dimension $\prod_id_{\alpha_i}$; including the identical classical
registers $\alpha$ on both sides does not change the coherent information, so it is at least
$\sum_i\log d_{\alpha_i}-\sum_iH(\text{Bell error}_i\mid\alpha)$. Jensen's inequality with the concave envelope $F_d$ and
$a_{i\lambda}t_{i\lambda}\le q_{i\lambda}$ proves the displayed bound; discarding a branch is a decision of Alice that
depends only on her local label. For the last claim, transferring a length-two product to the opposite party costs
$2\mu$, so Alice's cross-commutators are at most $\eta+4\mu$, and $\sqrt2\mu\le\kappa$ by \cref{lem:transfer-parties}(d).
\end{proof}

One-dimensional sectors contribute nothing even when they carry nontrivial characters; the resource is the weight on the
noncommutative blocks. Exact irrep flags and their maximally entangled factors are established~\cite{MLWHS24,HM02}; the
contribution here is the per-coordinate mismatch accounting on an arbitrary original state.

\begin{corollary}[Three-cycle visibility]\label{cor:S3}
For $G=S_3$, suppose the hypotheses of \cref{thm:group-extract} hold with Bob cross-commutator bound $\eta$ and mirror
bound $\mu$, put $\kappa=\eta+4\mu$, and suppose a three-cycle $c$ has $\|(u^A_i(c)-I)\Psi\|\ge\Delta$ for every $i$. If
$N\kappa^2\le\Delta^2/38400$ then $\ED^{A\to B}(\rho_{AB})\ge\Delta^2N/16$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The irreps of $S_3$ have dimensions $1,1,2$, and $2I-R(c)-R(c)^*=3P_{\mathrm{std}}$ in the regular representation. Hence
$a_i:=a_{i,\mathrm{std}}=\frac13\|(R^A_i(c)-I)\omega\|^2\ge\frac13(\Delta-\sqrt{20N}\kappa)_+^2\ge\Delta^2/12$, and
$q_i/a_i\le50N\kappa^2\cdot12/\Delta^2\le\frac1{64}$. For $t\le\frac12$ the function $a\mapsto a[1-F_2(q/a)]$ increases in
$a$ (its derivative is $1+\log(1-t)\ge0$ at $t=q/a$) and decreases in $q$, so each summand of \cref{thm:group-extract} is at
least $\frac{\Delta^2}{12}[1-h(\frac1{64})-\frac{\log3}{64}]\ge\frac34\cdot\frac{\Delta^2}{12}$.
\end{proof}

The exactness of each individual copy is supplied, from approximate data, by a local rounding.

\begin{lemma}[Local $S_3$ rounding]\label{lem:S3-round}
Let $S_i^P,T_i^P$ be reflections on each party with $\|(S_i^A-S_i^B)\Psi\|,\|(T_i^A-T_i^B)\Psi\|\le\mu_0$,
$\|((S_i^PT_i^P)^3-I)\Psi\|\le e$, and $\|[Q_i^B,Q_j^B]\Psi\|\le\eta_0$ for $i\ne j$ and $Q\in\{S,T\}$. There are exact local
$S_3$ representations $u_i^P$ with $u_i^P(s)=S_i^P$ satisfying the hypotheses of \cref{cor:S3} with
$\kappa=9\eta_0+144\mu_0+36e$; if Bob's raw three-cycle has $\|(S_i^BT_i^B-I)\Psi\|\ge\Delta_0$, Alice's rounded three-cycle
has displacement at least $\Delta=\Delta_0-2\mu_0-e$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $C=S_iT_i$ on either party, so $SCS=C^*$. Let $r_3$ send each point of the unit circle other than $-1$ to a nearest
cube root of unity, choosing $r_3(e^{\pm i\pi/3})=1$ at those ties, and set $r_3(-1)=1$. Then $r_3(\bar z)=\overline{r_3(z)}$,
and $|r_3(z)-z|\le|z^3-1|$ on the whole circle (at $z=-1$ both sides equal $2$). Put $C_0=r_3(C)$, $u(s)=S$ and $u(t)=SC_0$. Then $C_0^3=I$ and $SC_0S=r_3(C^*)=C_0^*$, so $u(s),u(t)$ are
reflections with $(u(s)u(t))^3=I$: an exact $S_3$ representation. Functional calculus gives $\|(C_0-C)\Psi\|\le e$ and
$\|(u(t)-T)\Psi\|\le e$. Every element of $S_3$ is a word of length at most three in $s,t$, and for involutive generators
the reversed word represents the inverse, so the mirrors~\eqref{eq:group-mirror} hold with $\mu=3\mu_0+6e$. Replacing the
letters of a word of length $L\le6$ by rounded ones, with raw suffixes moved to the other party, costs at most
$Le+L(L-1)\mu_0\le6e+30\mu_0$; two normal forms from different copies commute after at most nine raw swaps with suffixes
of length at most four, each costing $\eta_0+8\mu_0$. So Bob's cross-commutators are at most $9\eta_0+132\mu_0+12e$, and
$\kappa=\eta+4\mu$ gives the stated value. Finally Bob's raw $S^BT^B$ transfers to Alice's $T^AS^A=(S^AT^A)^*$, which has
the same displacement as $S^AT^A$, at cost $2\mu_0$, and rounding costs $e$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Alice-local recovery and the forward direction}\label{sec:recovery}

\begin{theorem}[Alice-local recovery]\label{thm:recovery}
Let $\rho_{AB}$ be a normal state with purification $\Psi=\sum_k\sqrt{\lambda_k}|a_k\rangle_A|u_k\rangle_{BE}$. Let Alice apply
the \emph{linear} isometry $V_A|a_k\rangle=|\bar u_k\rangle_{\bar B\bar E}$ into fresh registers, where the bar denotes
complex conjugation of coordinates in fixed product bases, followed if desired by discarding $\bar E$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),itemsep=1pt]
\item $(V_A\otimes I)\Psi=|\sqrt R\rangle$ with $R=\rho_{BE}$, in the vectorization $\bar B\bar E:BE$. Before the discard both
directional rates are unchanged; afterwards neither increases. Bob's marginal and operators are untouched.
\item If $U$ is a unitary on Bob with an actual unitary Alice mirror of error $\delta\le\sqrt2$, then the exact transpose
$U^T$ on $\bar B$ mirrors $U$ on the output with error at most
\[
\phi(\delta)=\sqrt{2\delta^2-\delta^4/2}\ \le\ \sqrt2\,\delta .
\]
In binary form, a disagreement probability $p\le\frac12$ becomes at most $2p(1-p)$.
\item If Bob has an exact tensor factor of $M$ qubits with Paulis $X_i,Z_i$, and Alice has actual unitary predictors with
disagreement probabilities $p_{X_i},p_{Z_i}\le\frac12$, then
\[
\ED^{A\to B}(\rho_{AB})\ \ge\ M-\sum_i\bigl[h(2p_{X_i}(1-p_{X_i}))+h(2p_{Z_i}(1-p_{Z_i}))\bigr],
\]
and $\ED^{B\to A}(\rho_{AB})\ge M-\sum_i[h(p_{X_i})+h(p_{Z_i})]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) The conjugate vectors $\bar u_k$ are orthonormal, so $V_A$ is an isometry on the support, extended by a local
orthogonal register; it is linear, not an antiunitary operation on the input. On $\Psi$ it gives
$\sum_k\sqrt{\lambda_k}|\bar u_k\rangle|u_k\rangle=|\sqrt R\rangle$. Local isometries preserve, and local channels do not
increase, the directional rates; nothing acts on $B$ or $E$.

(b) Let $V$ be Alice's mirror. The vectors $V\Psi$ and $U\Psi$ purify $R$ and $S=URU^*$ on $BE$, and
$\Re\langle V\Psi,U\Psi\rangle=1-\delta^2/2$, so by Uhlmann's transition-probability theorem~\cite{Uhl76} the root fidelity satisfies
$F(R,S)=\trn{\sqrt R\sqrt S}\ge1-\delta^2/2$. The affinity $A(R,S)=\Tr\sqrt R\sqrt S$ satisfies $F^2\le A$: for a unitary
$W$, $\Tr\sqrt R\sqrt SW=\Tr(R^{1/4}S^{1/4})(S^{1/4}WR^{1/4})$, whose first factor has squared Hilbert--Schmidt norm $A$ and
second at most $\Tr\sqrt RW^*\sqrt SW\le1$; maximize over $W$~\cite[(44)]{IRS17}. On $|\sqrt R\rangle$,
$\|(U^T\otimes I-I\otimes U)|\sqrt R\rangle\|^2=\hs{[U,\sqrt R]}^2=2-2A(R,S)\le2-2(1-\delta^2/2)^2=2\delta^2-\delta^4/2$. For
reflections the disagreement probability is a quarter of the squared mirror error, which gives the binary form. Normal
infinite systems follow by trace-class approximation.

(c) Bob first discards the rest of his system into the environment; then $\bar B$ is exactly $M$ qubits on which Alice
holds exact $X_i^T,Z_i^T$, predicted by Bob's $X_i,Z_i$ with disagreements $p'\le2p(1-p)$ by (b). Fano's inequality for
each bit and subadditivity give $H(X^M_{\bar B}\mid B)+H(Z^M_{\bar B}\mid B)\le\sum_i[h(p'_{X_i})+h(p'_{Z_i})]$, and the
uncertainty relation with quantum memory~\cite{BCCRR10} gives $H(X^M\mid B)+H(Z^M\mid B)\ge M+S(\bar B\mid B)$. Hence
$-S(\bar B\mid B)$ is at least the displayed bound, and hashing with sender $\bar B$, held by Alice, makes it achievable
from Alice to Bob. For the reverse statement no lift is needed: Alice measures the binary POVM with observable
$\frac12(M+M^*)$ of her predictor $M$, whose disagreement with Bob's reflection $Q$ is exactly
$\frac14\|(M-Q)\Psi\|^2$; the same Fano, subadditivity and uncertainty argument on Bob's exact register gives
$-S(B_0\mid A)$, achievable from Bob to Alice. If Alice's system is infinite, apply the argument after finite-rank
erasure truncations on Alice, which change the finitely many prediction probabilities arbitrarily little.
\end{proof}

The map $V_A$, followed by the discard of $\bar E$, is a Petz recovery map~\cite{Petz86}, the reversal operation of Barnum and
Knill~\cite{BK02}, in the pretty-good recovery form of~\cite[App.~A.1]{BCW14}, with sides and conventions made explicit; its finite
Choi matrix is $(\rho_A^{-T/2}\otimes I)\overline{\rho_{AB}}(\rho_A^{-T/2}\otimes I)$ on the support, and the binary bound
$p'\le2p(1-p)$ also follows from~\cite[(12)]{Ren17}. We claim no new recovery map. One-shot bounds for one-way distillation with a
Petz decoder, through binary hypothesis testing, are given by Can and Wilde~\cite{CW26}; they concern a given state, not the
near-winners of a fixed game. Its use here is that the
\emph{original} state and Bob's operators are kept, which gives the physical forward direction. Applied to all
generators, Alice's lifted operators $D_g=\overline{U_g}=(U_g^*)^T$ form a homomorphism, so the whole exact source, with
$J=-I$, is transported to Alice; this is used in \cref{sec:direction,sec:exact}.

\subsection{Exact synchrony gives accessible maximally entangled factors}\label{sec:exact-sync}

\begin{theorem}[Exact synchrony]\label{thm:exact-sync}
Let $G$ be a finite synchronous game, $A_{xa},B_{xa}$ finite-dimensional projective measurements and $\rho_{AB}$ a mixed
state with $\Tr\rho(A_{xa}\otimes I-I\otimes B_{xa})^2=0$ for every $x,a$. Restricted to the marginal supports, decompose the
two measurement algebras into matching irreducible blocks of dimensions $d_\lambda$ with multiplicity spaces. Then every
vector in the support of $\rho$ lies in $\bigoplus_\lambda\Phi_{d_\lambda}\otimes(M^A_\lambda\otimes M^B_\lambda)$, measuring the
local central projections gives both parties the same label $\lambda$, and the conditional state is exactly
$\Phi_{d_\lambda}\otimes\tau_\lambda$, even with coherence between different $\lambda$ in $\rho$. If $G$ has a pure Schmidt-rank
lower bound $c\ell^{-\alpha}$ at small loss $\ell$, then for every fixed $0<\eta<1$ an exactly synchronous mixed strategy of
loss $\eps$ yields, with success at least $1-\eta$, an exact maximally entangled state of rank at least $c_\eta\eps^{-\alpha}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Exact synchrony means $(A_{xa}\otimes I)v=(I\otimes B_{xa})v$ for every $v$ in the support of $\rho$; the supports of the
marginals are invariant under the local measurements. Vectorizing $v$ gives an operator $D$ with
$A_{xa}D=DB_{xa}^T$, an intertwiner of the Alice representation with the transpose of Bob's. By Schur's lemma the space
of such intertwiners is $\bigoplus_\lambda|\Phi_{d_\lambda}\rangle\otimes(M^A_\lambda\otimes M^B_\lambda)$. The central projections lie
in the local measurement algebras, and measuring them gives the same $\lambda$ on both sides with probability $p_\lambda$,
leaving $\Phi_{d_\lambda}\otimes\tau_\lambda$. All game measurements act on $V_\lambda$ only, so the strategy with $\Phi_{d_\lambda}$ has
loss $\ell_\lambda$ with $\sum_\lambda p_\lambda\ell_\lambda\le\eps$. The sectors with $\ell_\lambda\le\eps/\eta$ carry weight at least
$1-\eta$ and have $d_\lambda\ge c\eta^\alpha\eps^{-\alpha}$; on them Nielsen's majorization theorem~\cite{Nie99} converts
$\Phi_{d_\lambda}$ deterministically to one common $\Phi_{M}$. Measuring $\lambda$, rejecting the other sectors and discarding
multiplicities is a local protocol.
\end{proof}

This is standard finite-dimensional structure~\cite{PSSTW16}; we record it because the label is physically accessible,
unlike the label of a convex-roof decomposition. Approximate synchrony does not suffice for an exact output
(\cref{thm:no-exact}), and native constraint-variable games are not intrinsically synchronous: mirroring one
representative bit per variable does not give the hypothesis on all row measurements.
