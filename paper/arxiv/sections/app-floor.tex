\section{The floor: candidate classes and method obstructions}\label{app:floor}
%======================================================================

This appendix proves the statements about the logarithmic floor that \cref{prob:floor} summarizes. The first four subsections concern candidate sources. The remaining subsections study method obstructions, continuous amplification, polynomial augmentation and original-state kernel leakage. Throughout,
operator norms are written $\opn\cdot$, and $\|\cdot\|_{2,k}$ is the normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm on $\C^k$.

\subsection{Centralizing a Baumslag--Solitar commutator kills it}\label{app:floor-bs}

The Baumslag--Solitar relation $vu^2v^{-1}=u^3$ has exponentially accurate unitary models of small dimension, whose commutator
witness $W=[u,vuv^{-1}]$ is visible in operator norm (\cref{ex:thin-bs}). Making that witness itself the central marker kills it.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bs-central}
Let $A,B$ be unitaries, $W=ABA^*B^*$, $\delta=\opn{B^2-A^3}$, $\gamma_A=\opn{WA-AW}$ and $\gamma_B=\opn{WB-BW}$. Then
$\opn{W-I}\le4\delta+3\gamma_A+\gamma_B$. If $B=VAV^*$ for a unitary $V$ with $\gamma_V=\opn{WV-VW}$, then
$\opn{W-I}\le4\delta+4\gamma_A+2\gamma_V$. Consequently, if a unitary $J$ has $\opn{J-W}$, $\opn{JA-AJ}$, $\opn{JV-VJ}$ and $\delta$ all at
most $\tau$, then $\opn{J-I}\le23\tau$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The identities
\[
AB^2A^*B^{-2}=W\,(BWB^*),\qquad A^3BA^{-3}B^{-1}=(A^2WA^{-2})(AWA^{-1})W
\]
hold in every group. In the first, replacing $B^2$
by $A^3$ twice turns the left side into $I$ at cost $2\delta$, and replacing $BWB^*$ by $W$ costs $\gamma_B$; so
$\opn{W^2-I}\le2\delta+\gamma_B$. In the second, replacing $A^3$ by $B^2$ twice costs $2\delta$, and the conjugates of $W$ by $A^2$ and $A$
differ from $W$ by at most $2\gamma_A$ and $\gamma_A$; so $\opn{W^3-I}\le2\delta+3\gamma_A$. Since $W=W^3(W^2)^*$,
$\opn{W-I}\le\opn{W^3-I}+\opn{W^2-I}$. If $B=VAV^*$, then
$WB-BW=(WV-VW)AV^*+V(WA-AW)V^*+VA(WV^*-V^*W)$, so $\gamma_B\le\gamma_A+2\gamma_V$. For the last claim, $\gamma_A\le\opn{JA-AJ}+2\opn{J-W}
\le3\tau$ and likewise $\gamma_V\le3\tau$, so $\opn{W-I}\le4\tau+12\tau+6\tau$ and $\opn{J-I}\le23\tau$.
\end{proof}

So no extension that identifies the central marker with the commutator, up to a vanishing error, keeps the marker separated from
$I$; the relations $W^2=1$ and $W^3=1$ that centrality forces are coprime.

\subsection{Clocked witnesses and copied normal-closure gadgets}\label{app:floor-clock}

A subtler extension keeps the old Baumslag--Solitar matrices and puts the marker into the normal closure of their commutator. The
following two statements show that this fails at constant error whenever the old witness lives on one cell of a clock.

\begin{lemma}[Clock projections]\label{lem:clock-proj}
Let a unitary $V$ cyclically permute orthogonal projections $E_0,\dots,E_{n-1}$ with $\sum_jE_j=I$, that is $VE_jV^*=E_{j-1}$ with
indices modulo $n$, of arbitrary ranks. Let $P$ be a nonzero projection of finite rank $k$, $p_j=\Tr(PE_j)/k$ and
$\gamma=\opn{PV-VP}$. Then $\max_jp_j\le1/n+\gamma$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Measuring $\{E_j\}$ in the state $VPV^*/k$ gives the probabilities $\Tr(VPV^*E_j)/k=\Tr(PE_{j+1})/k=p_{j+1}$, a cyclic shift of $(p_j)$.
Measurement contracts the trace norm, so $\sum_j|p_{j+1}-p_j|\le\trn{P-VPV^*}/k\le2\opn{P-VPV^*}=2\gamma$, using
$\rank(P-VPV^*)\le2k$. A cyclic sequence has total variation at least twice its range, so $\max_jp_j-\min_jp_j\le\gamma$, and
$\min_jp_j\le1/n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Copied normal-closure gadgets]\label{prop:copied-gadget}
Let $U,V$ be unitaries on $\C^d$ such that $V$ cyclically permutes orthogonal projections $E_0,\dots,E_{n-1}$ summing to $I$, and
$W=[U,VUV^*]$ satisfies $(W-I)^*(W-I)\le4E_0$. Let $\mathcal P=\langle S\mid R\rangle$ be a finite presentation containing generators
$u,v,J$ and the relators $J^2$ and $[J,s]$ for $s\in S$, with largest relator length $L$, and suppose that $J$ is freely equal to a
product of $a\ge1$ conjugates of $w^{\pm1}$, $w=[u,vuv^{-1}]$, and $b$ conjugates of relators. Every unitary assignment with
$u\mapsto U\otimes I_q$, $v\mapsto V\otimes I_q$ for some $q\ge1$, operator-norm relator errors at most $\tau$ and $\opn{U_J-I}>\tau$
satisfies
\[
2\ \le\ 2a\sqrt{1/n+3\tau/2}+(24a+b(1+3L))\tau .
\]
In particular, if $n\ge8a^2$, then $\tau\ge\min\{1/(24a+b(1+3L)),\,1/(12a^2)\}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:compression} gives unitaries $Q_s$ with $\opn{Q_s-U_s}\le3\tau$, all commuting with a nonzero projection $P$ of rank $k$,
with $Q_J=-I$ on the range of $P$ and relator errors at most $C\tau$, $C=1+3L$. Its proof also gives
$\opn{[P,U_v]}=\frac12\opn{[H,U_v]}\le\frac32\tau$. Apply \cref{lem:clock-proj} to $V\otimes I_q$ and the projections $E_j\otimes I_q$:
$\Tr(P(E_0\otimes I_q))/k\le1/n+\frac32\tau$. Since $w(U)=W\otimes I_q$ and $(W-I)^*(W-I)\le4E_0$,
\[
\|(w(U)-I)P\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2/k=\Tr\bigl(P((W-I)^*(W-I)\otimes I_q)P\bigr)/k\le4(1/n+\tfrac32\tau).
\]
The word $w$ has eight letters, so $\opn{w(Q)-w(U)}\le24\tau$, and in the normalized norm of the range of $P$,
$\|w(Q)|_P-I\|_{2,k}\le2\sqrt{1/n+3\tau/2}+24\tau$. On the other hand the normalized norm is unitarily invariant, so evaluating the
fixed derivation of $J$ on the restricted unitaries gives
$2=\|Q_J|_P-I\|_{2,k}\le a\|w(Q)|_P-I\|_{2,k}+bC\tau$. The two bounds give the inequality. If $n\ge8a^2$ and $(24a+bC)\tau\le1$, it
forces $1/n+\frac32\tau\ge1/(4a^2)$, hence $\tau\ge1/(12a^2)$.
\end{proof}

The bound holds for every number $q$ of copies and survives perturbations of the copied generators that tend to zero in operator
norm. It does not cover representation-changing amplifications, a new Baumslag--Solitar tuple, or extensions in which $J$ is not in
the normal closure of the old witness.

\begin{example}[Thin Baumslag--Solitar models]\label{ex:thin-bs}
Let $n\ge3$ be odd, $q=3^n-2^n$, $h=(q-1)/3$ and $a_j=3^j2^{n-1-j}$ for $0\le j<n$. On $\C^{2n}$ with basis $e_{r,j}$, $r\in\{1,2\}$,
$j\in\Z/n\Z$, put $Ue_{r,j}=\exp(2\pi ic_ra_j/q)e_{r,j}$ with $c_1=1$ and $(c_2-c_1)a_0\equiv h\pmod q$, and $V=RV_0$, where
$V_0e_{r,j}=e_{r,j-1}$ and $R$ is the real Hadamard matrix on $E_0=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1,0},e_{2,0}\}$ and the identity elsewhere. Since
$2a_{j+1}=3a_j$ and $2a_0=2^n\equiv3^n=3a_{n-1}\pmod q$, $V_0U^2V_0^*=U^3$ exactly, so $VU^2V^*=RU^3R^*$. On $E_0$ the two eigenvalues of
$U^3$ differ by the factor $\exp(2\pi i\cdot3h/q)=\exp(-2\pi i/q)$, so $\opn{VU^2V^*-U^3}=\sqrt2\sin(\pi/q)$: the error is exponentially
small in the dimension $2n$. The projections $E_j=\mathrm{span}\{e_{1,j},e_{2,j}\}$ are permuted cyclically by $V$. The unitary
$VUV^*=RV_0UV_0^*R^*$ preserves $E_0$ and is diagonal off $E_0$, so $W=[U,VUV^*]$ is the identity off $E_0$ and
$(W-I)^*(W-I)\le4E_0$; and $W\ne I$, because on $E_0$ the eigenvalues of $U$ differ by $\exp(2\pi ih/q)\ne1$ and $VUV^*$ has the nonzero
off-diagonal entry $\frac12(\nu_1-\nu_2)$, where $\nu_2/\nu_1=\exp(2\pi i(c_2-c_1)a_1/q)=-\exp(-\pi i/q)$. By
\cref{prop:copied-gadget}, no fixed extension in which the central marker lies in the normal closure of $w$ turns copies of these
models into negative models at vanishing error; and by \cref{prop:bs-central}, neither does identifying the marker with $W$.
\end{example}

\subsection{Locally stable groups}\label{app:floor-stable}

Let $\Gamma$ be a group with a finite generating set $S$. It is \emph{locally Schatten-$p$ stable}, $1\le p<\infty$, if for every
$a>0$ there are $\delta(a)>0$ and a finite set $R_a$ of words in $S$ trivial in $\Gamma$ such that every unitary assignment
$U:S\to\mathrm U(D)$, in any dimension $D$, with $\max_{r\in R_a}\|r(U)-I\|_p<\delta(a)$ in the unnormalized Schatten norm, satisfies
$\max_s\|U_s-\pi(s)\|_p<a$ for some homomorphism $\pi:\Gamma\to\mathrm U(D)$. For $p=2$ this is Frobenius
stability~\cite{DGLT20,BLSW23}: if every asymptotic homomorphism of $\Gamma$ is close to a homomorphism, the local form follows by the
standard compactness argument, choosing $R_a$ among longer and longer finite lists of relations.

\begin{theorem}[Stable sources]\label{thm:stable}
Let $\Gamma$ be locally Schatten-$p$ stable, with a central involution $J\in S$ that every finite-dimensional unitary representation
sends to $I$. Let $R$ be a finite set of words trivial in $\Gamma$ containing $J^2$, $[J,s]$ for $s\in S$, and $R_1$, with largest
length $L$. Every unitary assignment on $\C^D$ with $\max_{r\in R}\opn{r(U)-I}\le\eta$ and $\opn{U_J-I}>\eta$ has
\[
D\ \ge\ \Bigl(\frac{\delta(1)}{(1+3L)\eta}\Bigr)^p,\qquad\log D\ge p\log(1/\eta)-O(1).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:compression} gives unitaries $V_s$ on $\C^k$, $1\le k\le D$, with $V_J=-I_k$ and $\opn{r(V)-I}\le(1+3L)\eta$ for $r\in R$, hence
$\|r(V)-I\|_p\le k^{1/p}(1+3L)\eta$. If this were below $\delta(1)$ for all $r\in R_1$, there would be a representation $\pi$ of
$\Gamma$ on $\C^k$ with $\|V_J-\pi(J)\|_p<1$; but $\pi(J)=I$ and $\|-I_k-I_k\|_p=2k^{1/p}\ge2$. So $k^{1/p}(1+3L)\eta\ge\delta(1)$.
\end{proof}

So no locally Schatten-stable group satisfies the hypothesis $\log D_n=o(\log(1/\eta_n))$ of \cref{prop:criterion}. The theorem does
not say that all approximate models are close to exact ones; it prices separated models polynomially.

\begin{example}[The higher-rank Deligne extension]\label{ex:deligne}
Let $E$ be the preimage of $\mathrm{Sp}(8,\Z)$ in the universal cover of $\mathrm{Sp}(8,\R)$, let $z$ generate the infinite cyclic
kernel of $E\to\mathrm{Sp}(8,\Z)$, and put $Q=E/\langle z^4\rangle$ and $J=z^2\langle z^4\rangle$, a central involution with $J\ne1$. Deligne's
theorem~\cite{Del78}, stated there for $\mathrm{Sp}(2n,\Z)$ with $n\ge2$, says that every finite-index subgroup of $E$ contains $z^2$;
see also~\cite{BCRR18}. A
finite-dimensional unitary representation of the finitely generated group $Q$ has a finitely generated linear image, which is
residually finite by Mal'cev's theorem~\cite{Mal40}; if it did not kill $J$, some finite quotient of $Q$, hence of $E$, would not kill
$z^2$. So every finite-dimensional unitary representation sends $J$ to $I$. The group $E$ has property~$[T_2]$, since
$\mathrm{Sp}(8,\R)$ is simple of real rank four~\cite[Theorem~2.10]{BLSW23}; groups with vanishing second cohomology in every unitary
representation are Frobenius stable, by the theorem of De Chiffre, Glebsky, Lubotzky and Thom~\cite{DGLT20} in the form
of~\cite[Theorem~1.1(F)]{BLSW23}; and $\langle z^4\rangle$ lies in the intersection of the finite-index
subgroups of $E$, so the quotient $Q$ is Frobenius stable~\cite[Lemma~2.12]{BLSW23}. By \cref{thm:stable} with $p=2$, every
operator-norm model of $Q$ with $J$ separated has $D\ge c_Q\eta^{-2}$. These are imported results; their statements have been checked
against the primary sources, not their proofs.
\end{example}

\subsection{The dyadic pattern and Abels' group}\label{app:floor-dyadic}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:dyadic-models}
Let $\langle S\mid R\rangle$ contain generators $w,x,y,J$ and the relators $J^2$, $[J,s]$ for $s\in S$, $wyw^{-1}y^{-1}J^{-1}$ and
$xyx^{-1}y^{-2}$, with largest relator length $L$. Every unitary assignment on $\C^D$ with operator-norm relator errors at most
$\eta$ and $\opn{U_J-I}>\eta$ has $D\ge1/(2(1+3L)\eta)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{lem:compression} gives $V_s$ on $\C^k$, $k\le D$, with $V_J=-I$ and relator errors at most $C\eta$, $C=1+3L$. The first relator
gives $\opn{V_wV_yV_w^*V_y^*+I}\le C\eta$, hence $\opn{V_wV_yV_w^*+V_y}\le C\eta$; the second gives $\opn{V_xV_yV_x^*-V_y^2}\le C\eta$.
\Cref{prop:dyadic} gives $k\ge1/(2C\eta)$.
\end{proof}

The Abels quotient $B$ of \cref{sec:floor-abels} has these words, so its separated operator-norm models need $\log D\ge\log(1/\eta)-O(1)$
and it cannot meet the criterion of \cref{prop:criterion}; this is consistent with \cref{cor:abels}, which proves the floor for its
game. The same holds for every source in which the marker is in the normal closure of a dyadic sign-and-dilation pattern by a
fixed derivation, since a fixed derivation changes the errors only by a constant factor.

\subsection{Sign and dilation in the limits of Theorem~A}\label{app:floor-typeII}

The class theorem needs the sign and the dilation on the original operators, with controlled cost. In the limits of the proof of
Theorem~A for a game without a finite-dimensional perfect strategy they are always present, the sign exactly and the dilation to
every accuracy.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:typeII}
Let $\mathcal M$ be a finite von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal tracial state and no type~I central summand, for instance a
limit $\mathcal M$ of the proof of \cref{thm:A} for a game without a finite-dimensional perfect strategy. For every $n\ge1$,
$\mathcal M$ contains unitaries $X,Y$ and a reflection $T$ with
$TYT=-Y$ and $\opn{XYX^*-Y^2}=2\sin(\pi/2^n)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\mathcal M$ contains a unital copy of $M_d(\C)$ for every $d$~\cite[Lemma~1.4]{CETW22}. In $M_d$, $d=2^n$, put $Y=\mathrm{diag}(z_j)$
with $z_j=e^{2\pi ij/d}$, let $T$ exchange $e_j$ and $e_{j+d/2}$, and let $X$ be the permutation unitary with $Xe_{\varpi(j)}=e_j$,
$\varpi(j)=2(j\bmod d/2)+\lfloor 2j/d\rfloor$. Then $TYT=-Y$ since $z_{j+d/2}=-z_j$, and $XYX^*Y^{-2}$ is $I$ on the first half of the
coordinates and $e^{2\pi i/d}I$ on the second, as in~\cite[Example~11.11]{DM-game}.
\end{proof}

So the limit algebra cannot distinguish the class of \cref{thm:floor-class}. Bounded polynomial approximation recovers these
operators from the tested generators to any fixed accuracy, for
each perfect trace, but gives no bound on the degree and coefficients of the polynomials as the accuracy decreases, and no uniform
neighbourhood of the trace. The class theorem has these data because its operators are actual local operators on the original
state, available before any limit is taken. The next proposition shows how large the missing bound can be.

\subsection{Slow recovery from four projections}\label{app:floor-slow}

For a word polynomial $F=\sum_wc_ww$ in finitely many letters put $B(F)=\sum_w|c_w||w|$. This is the quantity in the actual-mirror
transport estimate~\eqref{eq:opposite-poly}: if every tested generator has mirror error $\delta$, then $F$ has mirror error at most
$B(F)\delta$. Let $\mathcal A=\bigotimes_{j\ge1}M_2$ be the dyadic UHF algebra with its product trace, $Z_j,X_j$ the Pauli reflections at site
$j$, $\alpha_j=2^{-2^j}$, $a=\sum_j\alpha_jZ_j$, $b=\sum_j\alpha_jX_j$, $U=e^{ia}$ and $V=e^{ib}$. In $\widetilde{\mathcal A}=M_2(\mathcal A)$ let
\[
S_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad S_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad S_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&U\\U^*&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
S_3=\begin{pmatrix}0&V\\V^*&0\end{pmatrix},\quad P_\ell=\tfrac12(1+S_\ell).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Slow recovery]\label{prop:slow-recovery}
The four projections $P_\ell$ generate $\widetilde{\mathcal A}$, which has no nonzero finite-dimensional representation and whose closure in its
faithful trace is the hyperfinite $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor. For $r\ge3$ let $F_1,\dots,F_{2r}$ be word polynomials in four letters that
are formally self-adjoint, $F_j=F_j^*$, with $\opn{F_j(P)}\le2$ and, in the normalized trace norm $\|\cdot\|_2$,
\[
\|F_j(P)^2-1\|_2\le\kappa_r,\qquad\|F_j(P)F_k(P)-s_{jk}F_k(P)F_j(P)\|_2\le\kappa_r,\qquad\kappa_r=10^{-6}r^{-5/2},
\]
where $s_{jk}$ are the signs of the standard $r$-qubit Pauli array of \cref{thm:signed-regular}. Then
$\max_jB(F_j)>5\cdot10^{-7}\,r^{-5/2}\,2^{2^{r-1}}$. A polynomial whose value at $P$ is self-adjoint can be replaced by $\frac12(F+F^*)$,
which has the same value and no larger $B$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Generation.} Since $\opn a,\opn b<1$, the continuous logarithm recovers $a,b$ from $U,V$. The $Z_j$ commute and
$\alpha_j>\sum_{k>j}\alpha_k$, so the map $\sigma\mapsto\sum_j\alpha_j\sigma_j$ is a homeomorphism of $\{\pm1\}^{\N}$ onto the spectrum of $a$, and
every $Z_j$ is a continuous function of $a$. Next $[Z_j,[Z_j,b]]=4\alpha_jX_j$, so $X_j\in C^*(a,b)$, and the $Z_j,X_j$ generate $\mathcal A$. In
$\widetilde{\mathcal A}$, $S_0$ and $S_1$ give the matrix units of $M_2$, and the corners of $S_2,S_3$ give $U,V$. A nonzero
finite-dimensional representation would restrict to unital representations of the unital subalgebras $M_{2^k}$ for all $k$ in one fixed
dimension, which is impossible. The trace is the product trace, whose closure is the hyperfinite $\mathrm{II}_1$ factor.

\emph{Truncation.} Let $a_n,b_n,U_n,V_n$ and $P^{(n)}_\ell$ be obtained by stopping the sums at site $n$; they lie in
$M_2\otimes M_{2^n}=M_{D_n}$, $D_n=2^{n+1}$, a unital subalgebra on which the trace is the normalized matrix trace. With
$\tau_n=\sum_{j>n}\alpha_j\le2\cdot2^{-2^{n+1}}$, $\opn{e^{ia}-e^{ia_n}}\le\opn{a-a_n}\le\tau_n$, so $\opn{P_\ell-P^{(n)}_\ell}\le\tau_n$; telescoping
each word, whose letters are contractions, gives $\opn{F(P)-F(P^{(n)})}\le B(F)\tau_n$.

\emph{Contradiction.} Take $n=r-2$, so $D_n=2^{r-1}$, and suppose $t=\max_jB(F_j)\tau_n\le\kappa_r$. The matrices $H_j=F_j(P^{(n)})$ are
self-adjoint, because $F_j$ is formally self-adjoint and the $P^{(n)}_\ell$ are self-adjoint, and $\opn{H_j}\le3$. Replacing $F_j(P)$ by
$H_j$ changes the square by at most $5t$ and each ordered product by at most $5t$, so $\|H_j^2-1\|_2\le6\kappa_r$ and
$\|H_jH_k-s_{jk}H_kH_j\|_2\le11\kappa_r$. Let $Q_j=\sgn(H_j)$, with $\sgn0=1$; the scalar inequality $|\sgn x-x|\le|x^2-1|$ gives
$\|Q_j-H_j\|_2\le6\kappa_r$, and $\|Q_jQ_k-H_jH_k\|_2\le\|(Q_j-H_j)Q_k\|_2+\|H_j(Q_k-H_k)\|_2\le24\kappa_r$, so the reflections $Q_j$ have
signed pair defects at most $59\kappa_r$. Let Bob use the $Q_j$ and Alice their transposes on $\Phi_{D_n}$: the mirrors are exact and
both parties have these defects. \Cref{lem:fixed-size}, with $m=2r$, $C_r=2r(1+4r)\le10r^2$ and $p=(2C_r+1)^2(59\kappa_r)^2/4$, gives an
output $\Phi_{2^r}$ with singlet infidelity at most $2rp\le\frac{441}2\cdot59^2\cdot10^{-12}<10^{-6}$. But product local isometries applied
to $\Phi_{D_n}$ give singlet fraction at most $D_n/2^r=\frac12$ with $\Phi_{2^r}$, by the Schmidt-rank bound of \cref{prop:separation}.
So $\max_jB(F_j)>\kappa_r/\tau_n\ge5\cdot10^{-7}r^{-5/2}2^{2^{r-1}}$.
\end{proof}

The accuracy $\kappa_r$ is inverse-polynomial in $r$, of the order of the fixed-fidelity tolerance in the proof of \cref{thm:A}. So no proof
that uses only finitely many tested projections, absence of finite-dimensional representations and a type~II tracial closure can bound
the transport budget of its Pauli polynomials by $e^{Cr}$: here the budget is doubly exponential, at one faithful trace, in a nuclear and
hyperfinite example. This is not a counterexample to the floor: no finite game presenting this tuple, and no retained-state upper
strategy, is constructed, and other decoders might avoid these polynomials.

\subsection{Single-copy thresholds}\label{app:floor-threshold}

For a finite-dimensional state $\rho_{AB}$ let $f_r(\rho_{AB})$ be the largest overlap $\langle\Phi_{2^r}|\sigma|\Phi_{2^r}\rangle$ of a state $\sigma$
obtained from $\rho_{AB}$ by product local isometries followed by discarding junk. For a game $G$ in class~(S) or~(C), with quantum
value one and no finite-dimensional perfect strategy, and $0<\gamma<\frac12$, let $\Theta_G(r;\gamma)$ be the supremum of the losses
$\eps\le1$ such that every finite-dimensional strategy of loss at most $\eps$ has $f_r\ge1-\gamma$; Theorem~A says that this threshold
is positive.

\begin{proposition}[Exponential thresholds suffice]\label{prop:threshold}
If $\Theta_G(r;\gamma)\ge e^{-C_Gr}$ for all $r\ge1$, then $d^\to_G(\eps)\ge\frac{1-2\gamma}{C_G}\ln(1/\eps)-O_{G,\gamma}(1)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Given $\eps$, let $r=\lfloor\ln(1/\eps)/C_G\rfloor-1$. If $r\ge1$, then $\eps<e^{-C_Gr}\le\Theta_G(r;\gamma)$, so every strategy of loss at
most $\eps$ has $f_r\ge1-\gamma$,
and~\eqref{eq:singlet-hashing} with $D=2^r$ gives $\ED^{A\to B}\ge(1-2\gamma)r-h(\gamma)$; local isometries and discarding do not increase the
directional rates.
\end{proof}

The converse cannot be derived from the definitions: a large distillable entanglement does not give a good single-copy extraction at
a target logarithmic in it.

\begin{proposition}[A separation]\label{prop:separation}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),itemsep=1pt]
\item If a unit vector has Schmidt rank at most $s$, then every product local isometry followed by discarding junk gives overlap
at most $s/K$ with $\Phi_K$.
\item For $n=2^t$ let $\psi_n=\frac12|00\rangle\otimes\Phi_n+\frac{\sqrt3}2|11\rangle\otimes\Phi_{2^n}$, with orthogonal local flags. Then
$\ED^{A\to B}(\psi_n)=h(\frac14)+\frac t4+\frac{3n}4$; for every fixed $r$ and $t\ge r$, $f_r(\psi_n)=1$; but at $r_n=2t$,
\[
f_{r_n}(\psi_n)\ \le\ \Bigl(\frac1{2\sqrt n}+\frac{\sqrt3}2\Bigr)^2\ \longrightarrow\ \frac34 .
\]
\end{enumerate}
So for every fixed $\gamma<\frac14$ and large $n$, single-copy extraction of $r_n=2\log n$ pairs at fidelity $1-\gamma$ fails, although
the distillable entanglement is of order $n=2^{r_n/2}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) Product local isometries do not increase the Schmidt rank. Discarding junk systems $A_j,B_j$ from a vector
$\sum_{k\le s}\sqrt{\lambda_k}\,a_k\otimes b_k$ leaves the mixture of the vectors $\sum_k\sqrt{\lambda_k}\langle\alpha|a_k\rangle\otimes\langle\beta|b_k\rangle$ over
basis vectors $\alpha,\beta$ of the junk, each of Schmidt rank at most $s$. For a vector $v=\sum_{k\le s}c_kx_k\otimes y_k$ with orthonormal
$(x_k),(y_k)$, $|\langle\Phi_K,v\rangle|\le K^{-1/2}\sum_k|c_k|\le\sqrt{s/K}\,\|v\|$ by Cauchy--Schwarz; summing over the mixture gives $s/K$.

(b) $\psi_n$ is pure with Schmidt coefficients $\frac1{4n}$ ($n$ times) and $\frac3{4\cdot2^n}$ ($2^n$ times), so its directional rates are its
entanglement entropy~\cite{BBPS96}, as displayed. For $t\ge r$ both $n$ and $2^n$ are multiples of $2^r$, and controlled local isometries,
reading the flags, factor $\Phi_n$ and $\Phi_{2^n}$ as $\Phi_{2^r}$ times a maximally entangled junk exactly. At $r_n=2t$, $K=n^2$; for product
local isometries $V$ and the projection $\Pi$ onto $\Phi_K$ times the junk, $\|\Pi V\psi_n\|\le\frac12\|\Pi V(|00\rangle\Phi_n)\|+\frac{\sqrt3}2\le
\frac12\sqrt{n/K}+\frac{\sqrt3}2$ by (a), and $f_{r_n}$ is the supremum of $\|\Pi V\psi_n\|^2$.
\end{proof}

No game's loss is identified with these states, so the proposition is not a counterexample to any game-specific converse: it shows
that a converse needs information about the game. Extraction fails here only for target infidelities below about $\frac14$.
