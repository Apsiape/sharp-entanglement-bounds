\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%======================================================================

\subsection{The question}\label{sec:intro-question}

In a nonlocal game a referee asks two players questions and accepts if their answers satisfy a fixed predicate. The players
cannot communicate, but they may share an entangled state. Some games of quantum value one cannot be won exactly by any
finite-dimensional strategy, although finite strategies come arbitrarily close~\cite{Slo19}. Such nonattainment forces the
dimension of finite near-winning strategies to diverge as their loss $\eps$ tends to zero. A large dimension certifies only a
large space, which may hold a separable state, so we ask when nonattainment also forces the distillable entanglement of their
original mixed states to diverge, and at what rate.

``Entanglement'' can mean several different things here, and they behave differently. The local \emph{dimension} of a strategy,
or the Schmidt rank of a pure one, counts the size of the Hilbert space. The \emph{entanglement entropy} of a pure strategy counts
ebits. The \emph{entanglement of formation} of a mixed strategy is the least average entropy over pure decompositions. Dimension,
marginal entropy and entanglement of formation do not in general lower-bound the distillable entanglement of an arbitrary mixed
strategy: a large dimension or a large marginal entropy is compatible with a separable state, and a pure decomposition is a
mathematical expansion, not a label the players can read. A mixed state can even have positive entanglement of formation
and no distillable entanglement at all~\cite{HHH98}. For pure states, entropy does equal the asymptotic Bell-pair
rate~\cite{BBPS96}. We
seek guarantees on the actual mixed state without access to a label of one of its pure decompositions, so the resource we bound
is the \emph{distillable} entanglement of the actual shared state, the rate at which local operations and classical communication
turn copies of it into Bell pairs. We work with the one-way version, $\ED^{A\to B}$, and define the \emph{demand} of a game as
\[
d_G^\to(\eps)=\inf\bigl\{\ED^{A\to B}(\rho_{AB}):\ \text{some finite-dimensional strategy on }\rho_{AB}\text{ has loss}\le\eps\bigr\}.
\]
The infimum is over arbitrary mixed states and measurements, so a lower bound on $d^\to_G$ is a statement about every
near-winning strategy, with all its noise and all its correlations with an environment. Throughout, the \emph{original state}
is the shared state $\rho_{AB}$ that the players hold before they measure. Playing the game consumes it, and we make no claim
about entanglement that survives play. That question is addressed by Arnon-Friedman and Bancal, who certify,
device-independently, one-shot distillable entanglement in the state that remains after a test~\cite{AFB18}, and Bhavsar,
Wooltorton and Bae give a composable device-independent certification of states that a source prepares as a finite sequence of
independent, not necessarily identical, states~\cite{BWB25}. Our bounds concern the state before the game is played, and they hold
for every strategy of a fixed game rather than for a protocol that tests a source.

Winning with loss $\eps$ controls the players' operators to precision about $\sqrt\eps$, measured in their shared state. Through
this precision a game sees a group or an algebra, its \emph{source}, and the demand is a property of that source at precision
$\sqrt\eps$. A source group always comes with marked data: a central involution $J$, which must act as $-1$ on the state, or at
least stay visibly away from $1$ there. Without this requirement the trivial representation would satisfy every relation at no
cost. Our thesis is that \emph{the geometry of groups is the price of precision}: the absence of finite-dimensional
representations makes usable entanglement diverge, and quantitative invariants of the source (areas of fillings, spectra of
dilations, the growth of short addresses) set how fast.

\subsection{What was known}\label{sec:intro-known}

Man\v{c}inska and Vidick gave a two-player game, with answers of unbounded length, whose optimal value no finite amount of
entanglement attains~\cite{MV14}; the games below have finite answer sets. Slofstra showed that every finitely presented group
embeds in a solution group~\cite{Slo20}, and used embeddings of this kind to prove that the set of quantum correlations is not
closed and that deciding whether a linear system game has a finite-dimensional perfect strategy is undecidable~\cite{Slo19}.
Non-closure was later proved by other routes: via graphs~\cite{DPP19}, via correlation matrices
and factorizable channels that require infinite-dimensional ancillas~\cite{MR20}, and via embezzlement~\cite{Col20}; and Beigi
separated the quantum, spatial quantum and approximate quantum correlation sets~\cite{Bei21}. Slofstra and Vidick connected the entanglement of a linear system game to the hyperlinear profile of its
solution group, and built a fixed game whose least local dimension lies between $\eps^{-1/6}$ and $\eps^{-1/2}$, from a group whose
profile lies between $\eps^{-2/3}$ and $\eps^{-1}$~\cite[Theorem~1.1 and Proposition~3.2]{SV18}. Slofstra then built a finitely
presented group with at least subexponential hyperlinear profile, and from it a two-player game of commuting-operator value one in
which success probability $1-\eps$ needs dimension at least $\exp(c\,\eps^{-a})$ for some $a>0$; whether that game has quantum value
one is left open there~\cite[Theorem~1.2 and Corollary~1.4]{Slo18}. Ji, Leung and Vidick gave a three-player game of quantum value
one in which success probability $1-\eps$ needs local dimension $2^{\Omega(\eps^{-c})}$~\cite[Theorem~1]{JLV20}. Coladangelo gave a
two-player game, based on embezzlement, in which a strategy whose expected score is within $\eps$ of the optimum needs dimension
$2^{\Omega(\eps^{-1/8})}$~\cite[Theorems~1 and~5]{Col20}. His game is scored by a real-valued function, so his $\eps$ is a gap below an
optimal Bell score, not a loss from winning probability one; his exponent and the exponents below are therefore measured under
different contracts and are not directly comparable. Our companion paper gives an explicit linear system game of quantum value
one whose near-winners need entanglement entropy, and mixed strategies entanglement of formation, $\eps^{-1+o(1)}$, so that
$\log\Ent(G,\eps)=\eps^{-1+o(1)}$~\cite[Theorem~A]{DM-game}; it is built on the configuration lamps of our earlier
paper~\cite{DM-lamps}. Ostrev and Vidick showed that the $\mathrm{CHSH}(n)$ games need $\Omega(\eps^{-1/5})$ ebits at relative
precision $\eps$, that is, at a bias within a factor $1-\eps$ of the optimum~\cite{OV16}, and the companion paper improves this to
the optimal order $\Theta(\min(n,1/\eps))$ for entanglement entropy and formation~\cite[Theorem~E]{DM-game}. None of these bounds
the distillable entanglement of a mixed near-winner.

Near-perfect strategies can often be rounded to better ones, at the price of replacing the state. Paddock rounds near-perfect
strategies of constraint system and synchronous games to strategies on a new maximally entangled state, close to the original
measurements on its support~\cite[Corollaries~4.14 and~4.28]{Pad22}. Vidick approximates almost synchronous strategies by convex
combinations of projective strategies on maximally entangled states~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{Vid22}, and Marrakchi and de la Salle extend
this to infinite-dimensional strategies~\cite{MdlS23}; for projection games Culf rounds to approximately tracial
strategies~\cite{Cul26}. None of these extracts Bell pairs from the original state. Robust self-testing of many EPR
pairs~\cite{NV17,CRSV18,CS18,McK16} and constant-sized self-tests of unbounded dimension~\cite{Fu22,MPS21} are established, and,
under mild conditions on the ideal strategy, self-tests proved for pure states with projective measurements extend to arbitrary
mixed states and POVMs~\cite[Theorem~4.1]{Bap25}. For synchronous games, robust self-testing against projective maximally entangled
strategies lifts to all POVM strategies~\cite[Theorem~1.2]{VZ26}, and, for extreme synchronous correlations, to arbitrary
mixed-state strategies~\cite[Theorem~3.3]{CZ26}. For games with a robust game algebra, among them synchronous and binary constraint
system games, Zhao characterizes robust self-testing through uniqueness of an amenable tracial state on the universal measurement
algebra satisfying the game relations, with that unique trace finite-dimensional~\cite[Theorem~7.10]{Zha24}; Kar gives a
corresponding formulation for synchronous game algebras~\cite[Theorem~5.5]{Kar25}. The results just cited certify a prescribed ideal
strategy of a fixed finite dimension. Self-tests whose ideal has no finite-dimensional realization also exist, in the abstract-state
formulation of self-testing of Paddock, Slofstra, Zhao and Zhou~\cite[Section~7]{PSZZ24}: a correlation that is both a
commuting-operator and a quantum-approximate self-test~\cite[Section~6.1, Proposition~6.2]{CZ26}, and a commuting-operator self-test
for exact embezzlement of entanglement~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{PaSc26}. Each asserts that one exact correlation is realized by a unique
abstract state. Theorem~A below is a statement of another kind. Its games have no finite-dimensional perfect strategy, it specifies
no ideal strategy, and its conclusion is extraction of any prescribed number of Bell pairs, by one pair of local isometries, from
the original mixed state of every sufficiently good finite-dimensional strategy of the fixed game. Universal distillation from
unknown mixed states in a fixed finite dimension is also established~\cite{TWMAH26,TMGL26}, building on protocols for compound
sources~\cite{BJ14}.

In infinite dimensions the mechanism behind Theorem~A is exact. For hyperfinite factors in Haag duality that generate all
bounded operators, Keyl, Matsui, Schlingemann and Werner prove the infinite-one-copy-entanglement statement for every normal
joint state when the factors are not of type~I~\cite[Theorem~4.9]{KMSW06}. Van Luijk, Stottmeister, Werner and Wilming remove
hyperfiniteness for extraction from pure vector states by operations in the closure of LOCC~\cite[Theorem~54]{vLSWW25}; their
all-density-matrix Bell statement retains approximate finite dimensionality. Theorem~A uses the same dichotomy of types, but only
for a tracial limit of finite-dimensional strategies, and it proves its finite-strategy mixed-state assertion directly, by
extracting from those strategies themselves. Normal infinite-dimensional correlations that have no finite-dimensional
realization were constructed by Coladangelo and Stark~\cite{CoSt20}; the finite-block conclusion for the classes of Theorem~A
(\cref{prop:normal-perfect}) has a more restricted scope, perfect strategies of games in those classes.

Prior work includes operational distillation certification and quantitative entanglement bounds for specified self-tests. Our
question is different: for a fixed finite consistency-tested game, does the original mixed-state one-way distillable entanglement
of every near-winner diverge as its loss tends to zero, and can its growth be sharp?

\subsection{The main results}\label{sec:intro-results}

We state the results in plain terms and point to the precise theorems. Logarithms are to base two. Two classes of games carry the
law: \emph{synchronous games} in which every diagonal question pair is tested with positive probability, and
\emph{constraint-variable consistency games}, in which Alice answers a satisfying assignment to a constraint, Bob the value of one
of its variables, and every incidence is tested (\cref{def:law-classes}). Linear system games and Boolean constraint games belong to
the second class. Here \emph{Boolean} refers to the constraint variables: Bob answers one bit, but Alice answers an assignment to all
variables of her constraint. Single-bit answers on both sides would not do, since a game in which both players answer one bit and
which has a perfect finite-dimensional quantum strategy has a perfect classical one~\cite[Theorem~3]{CHTW04}.

\begin{theoremA}[Unreachable perfection forces usable entanglement; \cref{thm:A}]
Let $G$ be a finite game of quantum value one in either class. Then $G$ has no finite-dimensional perfect strategy if and only if
$d^\to_G(\eps)\to\infty$ as $\eps\to0$. More precisely, if $G$ has no finite-dimensional perfect strategy, then for every $r$
and $\gamma>0$ there is $\eps_0>0$ such that every
finite-dimensional mixed strategy of loss at most $\eps_0$ admits product local isometries producing $r$ Bell pairs with fidelity at
least $1-\gamma$, the environment, untouched, being part of the junk.
\end{theoremA}

The proof takes a tracial limit of Bob's moments, uses the absence of finite perfect strategies to exclude every finite type~I
summand of the limit, approximates Pauli matrices there by bounded polynomials, and evaluates the polynomials back on the original
strategies, where actual mirrors between the players' operators let them act on the original state. Three further results sharpen the
picture. For rational games in class~(S), and rational binary constraint-variable games in class~(C), a threshold $\eps_0$ for
fixed $r$ and rational $\gamma$ is computable under the promise of quantum value one and no finite-dimensional perfect strategy,
with finite rational certificates, once the polynomial moment relations are imposed on both sides (\cref{thm:effective}). Among
games promised to have quantum value one, whether the demand is bounded or divergent is undecidable, and the divergent branch is
not even semidecidable, already for games with contexts of arity four, sixteen answers for Alice and two for Bob
(\cref{thm:undecidable}). The reduction keeps the promise by construction: each instance is gated against a fixed game of
Slofstra that has value one and no finite-dimensional perfect strategy, so every instance has value one and only the existence of
a finite perfect strategy varies. For the same rational games under the same promise, if the players have independent copies
of the state and coherent white-box access to their own measurement circuits, as specified in \cref{thm:protocol}, one finite
protocol, independent of the hidden state and its dimension, extracts the pairs from one retained copy; this model is not
device-independent.

Theorem~A gives no rate. A logarithmic floor does hold on an explicit class: if every near-winner of a fixed game supplies, with
actual mirrors on its original state, unitaries satisfying a sign relation $WY=-YW$ and an even dilation $XYX^*=Y^m$, then
$d^\to_G(\eps)\ge\log(1/\eps)/(4\log m)-O(\log\log(1/\eps))$, and the Boolean constraint game compiled from Abels' finitely
presented solvable group is an instance (\cref{thm:floor-class,cor:abels}). Whether every game of Theorem~A has such a floor is
open; \cref{sec:floor} gives a sufficient criterion for a counterexample, and \cref{app:floor} the candidate classes that cannot meet
it (\cref{prob:floor}).

The demand has a direction, and one might fear that a game could demand usable entanglement only from Alice to Bob. It cannot,
for the large class of agreement games, in which acceptance means agreement of labels of the two answers.

\begin{theoremB}[Directions are interchangeable at a loss factor of three; \cref{thm:factor-three,thm:factor-three-sharp}]
For every finite agreement game, one channel applied by Alice alone, followed by a measurement of whole answers, turns a strategy
of loss $\eps$ into one of loss at most $3\eps$ whose two directional rates are equal and no larger than either original rate. So
$d^\to_G(\eps)\ge d^\leftarrow_G(3\eps)$ and conversely, uniformly in the game. The factor three is optimal for this mechanism: no
deterministic Alice-local channel that symmetrizes the state while keeping Bob's system, marginal and measurements fixed gives a
smaller factor, whatever legal decoding follows.
\end{theoremB}

The optimality concerns this mechanism, not every comparison of the two demand profiles (\cref{prob:direction}). For
$\eps\le\frac12$ the loss bound improves to $1-(1-\eps)^3$ (\cref{thm:factor-three}), and the guarantee of this channel and
decoder holds for a predicate exactly when the predicate has the agreement form (\cref{thm:factor-three-sharp}). The channel
keeps Bob's operators and
marginal, so every source budget measured on Bob is unchanged. Its output is the reflected
state of Dutta and Faulkner~\cite{DF19}, reached by a Petz recovery map~\cite{Petz86} in the pretty-good form of Barnum and
Knill~\cite{BK02,BCW14}, and the factor three is the polynomial inequality $h^3\ge3h-2$ on $[-1,1]$ applied to KMS-adjoint
correlation maps, related to the correlation-operator framework of Beigi's quantum maximal correlation~\cite{Bei13}.

Changing the finite presentation of the marked group changes the precision of every model by at most a constant factor and keeps
the state (\cref{prop:algebraic-map} and the paragraph after it). A constant factor changes $\alpha\log(1/e)$ only by $O(1)$ and
leaves every exponent unchanged, so all the laws below are independent of the presentation; a change of precision by a power
would change them, and none occurs. For the normalized trace, Chapman, Vidick and Yuen transfer stability moduli between
presentations with the same kind of constant rescaling~\cite[Lemma~2.6]{CVY26}; here the state is kept, and conjugations are paid
for through state commutators. For permutation and metric models, Cornulier's sofic profile is defined from the finite pieces of the
group itself~\cite{Cor13}, and the profiles of Arzhantseva and Cherix do not depend on the generating set up to the usual
equivalence~\cite{AC24}. The next theorem collects the fixed games whose demand we can compute; all are finite games fixed
before $\eps$ is chosen.

\begin{theoremC}[A spectrum of fixed games; \cref{thm:rational-games,thm:renyi,thm:rational-ED,cor:linear-existence,cor:coefficient-set,thm:lamps-ED,thm:SV,thm:SV-entropy,thm:point-window}]
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{A dense set of coefficients.} Let $\alpha=\frac12\sum_i\log_{m_i}q_i$ for finitely many (at least one) pairs of integers $m_i\ge2$ and
$q_i=2^{r_i}$ with $r_i\ge1$ and $q_i$ dividing $m_i$; these numbers form a dense subset of $(0,\infty)$ that contains every positive rational and some transcendental numbers, such
as $\frac12\log_62$. For each such $\alpha$ there are a fixed Boolean constraint game and a fixed linear system game with
$d^\to(\eps)=\alpha\log(1/\eps)+O(\log\log(1/\eps))$ for small $\eps$, minimum Schmidt rank of a pure strategy $\Theta(\eps^{-\alpha})$, and
minimum pure entropy and formation $\alpha\log(1/\eps)+O(1)$. For rational $\alpha$ the games can be chosen so that, over pure
strategies, the coefficient of $\log(1/\eps)$ in the minimum R\'enyi entropy $H_\beta$ is $\alpha$ for $0\le\beta\le1$ and
$\min\{\alpha,\beta/(\beta-1)\}$ for $1<\beta<\infty$ (\cref{thm:renyi}, which also treats $H_\infty$, smooth min-entropy and
one-shot concentration).
\item \emph{Almost inverse loss.} The configuration-lamp game of~\cite{DM-game} has $d^\to(\eps)=\eps^{-1+o(1)}$, with an explicit
lower bound $c\,\eps^{-1}2^{-4(24+\log\log(1/\eps))^2}$ and upper bound $O(\eps^{-1}\log(1/\eps))$.
\item \emph{The Slofstra--Vidick baseline.} Slofstra and Vidick bounded the least dimension of their game~\cite{SV18} between
$\eps^{-1/6}$ and $\eps^{-1/2}$; our companion paper shows that it is $\Theta(\eps^{-1/2})$ and that the source has profile
$\Theta(1/e)$ at every visibility~\cite[Theorem~F]{DM-game}. The game's least pure entropy and formation are
$\frac12\log(1/\eps)+O(1)$.
\item \emph{A polynomial window.} A fixed Boolean constraint game built from Pauli lamps on the points of Houghton's group $H_3$ has
$c\,\eps^{-\beta}\le d^\to(\eps)\le C\eps^{-1/4}$, where $\beta=(2\log_2\lambda+1)^{-1}=0.0734595360384268649\ldots$ and $\lambda$ is specified in \cref{prop:point-fill-sharp}.
\end{enumerate}
By Theorem~B each holds in both directions.
\end{theoremC}

In (i) the source is a finitely presented group $L_{q,m}$ with a dilation $xyx^{-1}=y^m$ and $q$ lamps cyclically permuted by $y$:
the spectral law of $y$ is nearly invariant under multiplication by $m$ and rotation by $1/q$. Every invariant law satisfies a
Frostman bound of exponent $s=\log_mq$, hence has dimension at least $s$. This fixes the minimum spectral scale relevant to the
profile, and matching approximate models attain the corresponding rank and entropy rates. The exponents of independent copies
add, without a joint spectral measure (\cref{thm:lamp,thm:additive}). The usable-entanglement
coefficient comes from a triangular extraction on the original state with cyclic labels (\cref{thm:triangular,lem:cyclic-triangular}).
The transcendence of $\frac12\log_62$ is the Gelfond--Schneider theorem. In the other direction, the logarithmic coefficient of a fixed
game of value one, when it exists, is left-c.e.\ relative to the second Turing jump $0''$ (\cref{thm:arithmetic}). Every positive $\Delta^0_2$ real occurs with a matched original-mixed-state usable law~\cite[Theorem~16.6 and Corollary~16.7]{DM-U3}; the full usable spectrum remains open (\cref{prob:dense}). The positive pure original-state entropy spectrum is exactly the positive $\Delta^0_2$ reals (\cref{thm:pure-spectrum}). In (ii) the source has $2^N$ Pauli
lamps whose pairwise commutators have area $2^{O(\log^2N)}$; the companion paper counts them in an arbitrary state, with errors
summed in squares, and an extraction that counts Bell-error entropy rather than fidelity gives the usable rate
(\cref{thm:count,thm:signed-regular}). In (iv)
Houghton's far commutators have area $O(R^{\log_2\lambda})$ (\cref{prop:point-fill-sharp}), by an explicit six-type refinement of the filling method of~\cite{DM-H3}; on the original state a filling is paid only
linearly in its area and length (\cref{thm:linear-filling}), and this gives the lower exponent, while an explicit finite model
that spreads one collective defect over a torus of clocks gives the upper one (\cref{prop:collective}). Whether the demand in
(iv) has a limiting exponent, and what it is, remain open. On a calibrated native class of strategies the point game has demand $\Theta(\eps^{-1/3})$, a conditional class
law (\cref{thm:native}; see \cref{sec:status}).

The least usable entanglement of an original state carrying $M$ pairwise approximate Pauli pairs at error $0\le\kappa\le1/100$ is of order $\min\{M,\kappa^{-2}\}$, and exactly $M$ at $\kappa=0$ (\cref{thm:optimal-pair-budget}). The restriction $\kappa\le1/100$ cannot be dropped: at $\kappa=2$ a product state satisfies every defect bound and has rate zero (end of Section~\ref{sec:optimal-pair-budget}). The spectrum refinements in \cref{sec:arithmetic} separate computable pure-state optimization, convex-roof formation and physical one-way leakage; \cref{app:floor} gives quantitative obstructions and sufficient conditions for the logarithmic floor. For the collective point model, the magnetic-cycle estimate fixes the best defect order obtainable by subdividing its fixed holonomy (\cref{prop:fixed-holonomy}), without settling the quarter lower bound.

What a game sees through approximate relations, an exact source sees through exact ones. Under exact relations the demand is
governed by a single growth function. These results belong to the exact-source contract (EX) of \cref{sec:prelim-usable}, not to
finite-game loss (\cref{sec:status}).

\begin{theoremD}[The price of an address; \cref{thm:address,thm:exact-point,thm:exact-config,thm:subset,thm:subset-visibility,thm:gamma3}]
If Bob satisfies the relations of a source exactly, in the sector $J=-I$ of a necessarily infinite-dimensional representation, and
Alice mirrors each generator with error $e$ on an arbitrary original mixed state, then the demand in both directions is at least
a fixed positive multiple of the number of independent Pauli qubits addressable by words of length $c/e$. For Pauli lamps on the
points of $H_3$, on the $k$-subsets of its points, and on its configurations, the demand is $\Theta(1/e)$, $\Theta_k(e^{-k})$ and
$2^{\Theta(1/e)}$, with matching finite-rank constructions; a five-generator replication source is also exponential. With a central
reflection of visibility $\Delta$ instead of $J=-I$, the subset sources have, uniformly for $e\le c_k\Delta$, the joint law
$\Theta_k(\Delta^{k+2}e^{-k})$.
\end{theoremD}

Exactness can be exponentially expensive. The approximate configuration models of~\cite[Proposition~9.4]{DM-game} cost only $O(e^{-2}\log(1/e))$
ebits, the exact ones $2^{\Theta(1/e)}$, so a correction that keeps the original state and its resource needs error
$\Omega(1/\log(1/e))$; the collective point models cost $O(e^{-1/2})$ ebits against $\Theta(1/e)$ for exact ones
(\cref{prop:correction}).

For near-optimal Bell violations, finite-dimensional nonattainment alone need not force divergent usable entanglement. The
$I_{3322}$ Bell functional is not attained in finite dimension, as Pauwels~\cite[Theorem~2]{Pau26}, Coladangelo~\cite[Theorem~11.1]{Col26}
and the first author~\cite[Theorem~1]{Dou26} showed independently; it is attained by an infinite-dimensional spatial
strategy~\cite[Theorem~11.1]{Col26}, \cite[Theorem~2]{Dou26}, and its least dimension at Bell gap $\varepsilon$ grows like
$\log(1/\varepsilon)$~\cite[Theorem~6]{Dou26}. The Schmidt coefficients of the attainer of~\cite{Dou26} have exponential
tails~\cite[Section~5.2]{Dou26}, and bounded usable entanglement is an elementary consequence of those tails. The analytic proofs of
attainment and of the tails are not formalized, so we state the consequence conditionally.

\begin{theoremE}[A conditional Bell-gap contrast; \cref{thm:i3322}]
Conditional on the spatial attainment theorem and the tail estimate of~\cite{Dou26}, strategies for the $I_{3322}$ functional
with Bell gap tending to zero need only bounded usable entanglement, in either direction.
\end{theoremE}

This changes both the contract and the class (\cref{sec:status}), so it does not decide whether Theorem~A extends beyond
classes~(S) and~(C). What the contrast does show is a difference in the kind of nonattainment. The infinite-dimensional
construction of the $I_{3322}$ optimum and the optimality conjecture are due to P\'al and V\'ertesi~\cite{PV10}; the cited 2026
papers concern its analytic resolution. In the
classes of Theorem~A a normal perfect strategy of any entropy already has a finite perfect block, by a theorem of Kim, Paulsen
and Schafhauser~\cite{KPS18} and its standard analogue for constraint games (\cref{prop:normal-perfect}), whereas, by
\cite[Theorem~2 and Section~5.2]{Dou26}, the $I_{3322}$ optimum is attained by a normal strategy of finite entropy.

\paragraph{The engine.} Behind these results is a set of tools that keep the state; the first two are proved in our companion
paper~\cite{DM-game} and are used here as stated there. In the hyperlinear-profile route of
Slofstra and Vidick, the passage from a general strategy to a normalized-trace model, through a spectral projection of the
reduced state, changes the precision from order $\sqrt\eps$ to order $\eps^{1/4}$~\cite[Theorem~5.1 and Lemmas~5.5,
5.6]{SV18}, and rounding to a maximally entangled strategy~\cite{Pad22} replaces the state. Our arguments keep the
state-weighted model through these steps. Original-state self-testing and mixed-state assumption lifting were discussed
above; what differs here is the nonattaining fixed-game regime and its resource laws. (For the memory of a repeatedly used
channel, the weighted one-round theorem of our earlier paper already works with a non-flat state~\cite[Theorem~10.1]{DM-lamps};
the tools below do this for both players of a game.)
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \emph{A qubit count in an arbitrary state} (\cref{thm:count}, from~\cite[Theorem~C]{DM-game}): pairwise relations among $2M$
approximate Pauli involutions, measured in the state and summed in squares, together with their commutators with the square root
of the state, bound its entropy below by about $M$.
\item \emph{Transfer and return with the state kept} (\cref{thm:transfer,thm:return}, from~\cite[Theorem~D]{DM-game}, and
\cref{thm:boolean-correspondence}): the cost of a linear or Boolean constraint game is a state-weighted profile of its algebra at
precision $\Theta(\sqrt\eps)$, in both directions, with the whole spectrum of the state preserved.
\item \emph{A retained-state compiler} (\cref{thm:universal}): every finite presentation with a central involution is compiled into
a Boolean constraint game of arity three, with a split algebraic retraction that keeps the whole state; corner maps
(\cref{prop:corner}) recover a group inside a game algebra without cutting out a sector.
\item \emph{Extraction on the original mixed state} (\cref{sec:extract}): triangular and signed regular extraction, aggregate and
finite-group versions with a center-aware one-way protocol, and Alice-local recovery for the forward direction.
\item \emph{A linear filling charge} (\cref{thm:linear-filling}): on the original state, a null word of length $n$ with a filling
of $A$ cells has error $O((A+n)e)$ at precision $e$, although the conjugators of its cells can be long.
\end{itemize}
The tools added here are the Boolean case of the correspondence, the compilers and the original-state extraction; they turn the
entropy and dimension bounds of the companion into bounds on usable entanglement.

\subsection{The ideas of the proofs}\label{sec:intro-ideas}

\paragraph{Precision lives in the state.} If Bob's reduced state is $\rho$ and $\xi=\rho^{1/2}$, a strategy of loss $\eps$ gives Bob
operators that satisfy the relations of the game up to $\sqrt\eps$ in the norm $\hs{Y\xi}=\Tr(Y^*Y\rho)^{1/2}$, and that nearly
commute with $\xi$, again to order $\sqrt\eps$: consistency between Alice and Bob forces it (\cref{lem:consistency}). Fixing a state
is not like fixing a trace: conjugation by a unitary moves the state, and the price is the commutator with $\xi$. Every estimate
in this paper is made in such a norm, and the only cost of changing the state is a commutator of this kind. Errors are summed in
squares wherever possible, because the counting and extraction arguments below charge each qubit only the sum of squares of its
pair errors.

\paragraph{Counting qubits without flattening.} Exact Pauli pairs $X_i,Z_i$ force dimension $2^M$. To see approximately the same in a
state, the companion paper's argument~\cite[Section~4]{DM-game} proceeds as follows: embed the space coherently into a register indexed by $\{0,1\}^M$ through the products $Z_1^{g_1}\cdots Z_M^{g_M}$; on the
register the shifts and signs are exact Pauli operators, and the embedding intertwines them with the $X_i,Z_i$ up to an error
that the two-sided twirl of \cref{lem:twirl} computes as a sum of squares of pair relations; pinching the embedded state then
gives about one bit per pair (\cref{thm:count}).

\paragraph{From a count to Bell pairs.} A count bounds the entropy of Bob's marginal, which a separable state can also have. To get
usable entanglement, both players apply the coherent embedding, each to their own operators; actual mirrors then make the two
exact label registers nearly agree, and every Bell stabilizer of the output fails with probability $O(M\kappa^2)$
(\cref{thm:signed-regular}). The decisive point is to use these failures through their \emph{entropy}: after a Pauli twirl the
output is Bell-diagonal, and hashing gives $M-\sum_kh(p_k)$ ebits however the errors are correlated. A fidelity bound for the
whole register would add the failures and lose a factor $M$.

\paragraph{Why unreachable perfection is enough.} In Theorem~A there is no rate and no explicit source. The tracial limit of Bob's
moments is a finite von Neumann algebra. When the game has no finite-dimensional perfect strategy, the limit has no type~I piece,
since such a piece would yield one; so the limit is of type~II and contains every matrix algebra. Pauli matrices there are
approximated, in the trace norm, by bounded polynomials in the game's measurements; evaluated on the original strategies, on both sides, and rounded locally, they are
approximate Pauli pairs with small mirrors, and the fixed-size extraction lemma (\cref{lem:fixed-size}) applies.

\paragraph{Why the directions agree.} Alice can map her half of a purification to a conjugate copy of Bob's side; on the output the
two parties hold mirror images, so the two one-way rates coincide, and they are no larger than before. To keep playing an
agreement game, Alice measures the image of her old measurements under the correlation map $S$; the new success is $\langle w,H^3w\rangle$ for the
self-adjoint contraction $H$ whose quadratic form is the old success, and $h^3\ge3h-2$ on $[-1,1]$ gives the factor three.

\paragraph{Rates from transport.} For a source with $xyx^{-1}=y^m$ and lamps rotated by $y$, the relations make the spectral law of $y$,
measured in the state, nearly invariant under multiplication by $m$ and under rotation by $1/q$. A nearly invariant law is close to an
invariant one, and every invariant law satisfies a Frostman bound of exponent $\log_mq$, hence has dimension at least $\log_mq$;
so a law with few atoms is far from all of them. Entropy, rather than
dimension, needs the commutator of $y$ with $\xi$ once more: it lets both halves of a purification read the same spread spectral
law (\cref{thm:entropy-transport}). For several copies we cannot use a joint spectral measure; instead we measure spectral arcs of
short words in reverse order of depth, so that the errors form geometric series (\cref{thm:additive}).

\paragraph{Rates from areas.} For lamps on configurations or points of Houghton's group, the qubits come from transported Pauli
pairs, and the cost of making two transported pairs commute is governed by the area of a filling. On a non-flat state each cell of
a filling is reached through a conjugator, and charging every cell its own conjugator costs area times length. Cutting the van
Kampen diagram into a tree of faces, and substituting the whole boundary of each subdiagram at once, shares the conjugators, so a
filling costs only linearly in its area and length (\cref{thm:linear-filling}). Quasi-polynomial areas give the
configuration-lamp rate, and polynomial areas the lower end of the point window.

\paragraph{Exact sources and collective models.} When the relations are exact, every independent qubit is free, and the cost is the
number of qubits addressable by short words. Approximate models are cheaper because they can merge addresses or, as in the
collective model of the point game, replace a sum of many small defect logarithms by the logarithm of their product, which is a
single bounded projection. The difference can be exponential (\cref{prop:correction}).

\subsection{Scope and status}\label{sec:status}

We state the scope once here, and the later sections refer to this subsection. Three distinctions run through the paper.
\emph{Original state versus replacement state}: every lower bound on usable entanglement is a bound on the distillable
entanglement of the original mixed state, with its purifying environment untouched; bounds on marginal entropy, pure Schmidt
rank, entanglement of formation or dimension are stated as such and are never used as bounds on $\ED$. \emph{Loss versus Bell
gap}: $\eps$ is a game loss from value one, except in \cref{sec:boundary}, where a gap below the Bell optimum of $I_{3322}$ is
used, which no affine rescaling turns into a game loss (\cref{sec:i3322}). \emph{Unrestricted versus class laws}: a demand is an
infimum over all finite-dimensional strategies; the native laws of \cref{sec:point-native} are laws on a restricted, calibrated
class of strategies, and the exact-source laws of \cref{sec:exact} are laws for a different contract, exact source relations and
approximate mirrors.

The companion paper~\cite{DM-game} proves the qubit count, transfer and return for linear system games, the $\mathrm{CHSH}(n)$ and Slofstra--Vidick rates, and the explicit configuration-lamp game with its entropy, formation and dimension bounds. We state the inputs we use with precise pointers in \cref{app:companion}. The realization and matched coefficient laws used in \cref{sec:arithmetic,prob:dense} are supplied by~\cite[Theorem~1.1, Theorem~16.6 and Corollary~16.7]{DM-U3}. Proofs identify the imported results at their uses; the remaining arguments are written in the text or appendices. The native class laws of \cref{thm:native,thm:native-enlarged}
are \emph{conditional} on estimates from our manuscript on immediate delivery~\cite{DM-ID} and on an aggregate estimate, which have
not been proved self-containedly. The specifications of the restricted linear compiler for several copies
(\cref{thm:rational-linear-k}: arity, amplification, algorithm and exports) are \emph{conditional} on a self-contained proof of that
compiler; the existence of linear system games with every positive rational exponent is not (\cref{cor:linear-existence}), and
neither are the Boolean constraint family and the one-copy linear family. The $I_{3322}$ statement
\cref{thm:i3322} is \emph{conditional} on the spatial attainment theorem and tail estimate of~\cite{Dou26}. \Cref{tab:status} identifies the proof locations and computational scope of the results; the section \hyperref[sec:methods]{How this work was done} describes the process and the accompanying finite checks.

Slofstra and Vidick's proof of~\cite[Proposition~6.2]{SV18} takes from~\cite[Proposition~4.8]{Slo19} a $2d$-dimensional lift in
which the marked involution is balanced. With two or more non-involutive generators, as in the group behind the Slofstra--Vidick game, the literal
lift is unbalanced, so that step does not apply as written. This affects only the dimension factor of the upper bound: their value
one, their lower bounds and the non-triviality of $J$, which they take from~\cite[Lemma~5.2]{Slo19}, do not use the lift. Our
linear games therefore use a balanced construction (\cref{prop:extended}), and our companion paper proves the lift for the
Slofstra--Vidick group~\cite[Lemma~11.13]{DM-game}, so~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{SV18} holds as stated (\cref{sec:transport-game}).

The results are compositions of established tools with new accounting, and we say so at each result: tracial and synchronous
correspondences~\cite{CM14,PSSTW16,KPS18}, Kaplansky density, Pauli and regular-representation extraction~\cite{GH17,NV17},
Petz and pretty-good recovery~\cite{Petz86,BK02,BCW14,IRS17,Ren17}, the uncertainty relation with memory~\cite{BCCRR10} and hashing~\cite{DW05,BDSW96}.
Bounded literature searches found no exact preemption of the composed headline statements; this is not an exhaustive priority
verdict, and we make no claim of being first.

We do not claim a classification of all games or an exact description of the full mixed-state usable-entanglement coefficient spectrum. The positive pure original-state spectrum is classified by \cref{thm:pure-spectrum}. We do not claim a device-independent protocol,
efficient decoders, a matching quarter exponent for the point game, or a logarithmic floor for every nonattaining game; these and
other open targets are stated in
\cref{sec:open}. Constants are deliberately poor and depend on the fixed game.

\subsection{Related work and credits}\label{sec:intro-related}

The Hilbert--Schmidt picture of a shared state, with the reduced state as a factor $\xi$, is the standard form of the state; Slofstra
and Vidick use it for general strategies~\cite{SV18}, and Marrakchi and de la Salle use it to round near-synchronous
strategies~\cite{MdlS23}. The coupling of two spectral measures in one state is Connes' joint distribution~\cite{Con76}. Defect bounds
in the state of a near-perfect strategy of a boolean constraint system game, and embeddings of group algebras into such games with
finite certificates, are due to Fanizza, Kroell, Mehta, Paddock, Rochette, Slofstra and Zhao~\cite{FKMPRSZ25}, whose
relation-decomposition bound is Lemma~3.9 of Mehta, Slofstra and Zhao~\cite{MSZ23}; Slofstra's finite gadgets
and embedding theorem~\cite{Slo19,Slo20} and Paddock's constraint-system machinery~\cite{Pad22} underlie our compiler, whose new part is
the split retraction. The finite-dimensional structure of perfect synchronous strategies is due to Paulsen, Severini, Stahlke,
Todorov and Winter and to Kim, Paulsen and Schafhauser~\cite{PSSTW16,KPS18}; approximate tracial rounding to Vidick, Marrakchi and
de la Salle, Paddock, and Culf~\cite{Vid22,MdlS23,Pad22,Cul26}; Pauli stability for synchronous strategies to de la Salle~\cite{dlS22}.
Thoma's classification of the characters of the infinite symmetric group~\cite{Tho64}, Cornulier's criterion for finitely presented
wreath products~\cite{Cor06}, and Johnson's presentation of Houghton's group~\cite{Joh99,Lee12} are used for the exact sources. The
functional-inequality framework behind the R\'enyi spectrum is that of~\cite{BCLS95,Rum10}, and one-shot concentration is due
to~\cite{Vid99,LP01,BD10,Nie99}. The undecidable core of \cref{thm:undecidable} is Slofstra's~\cite{Slo19}; the remark after that
theorem credits the related game disjunction of Man\v{c}inska and Schmidt~\cite{MaSc23} and compares the theorem with the
RE-completeness of entangled constraint satisfaction problems~\cite{CuMa25}.

The configuration lamps come from our arXiv paper~\cite{DM-lamps}. There, copies of $S_3$ sit on the configurations of the points of
Houghton's group; the paper bounds the sofic profile of the resulting group and the dimension of its unitary models at
normalized-trace precision $e$, which is at least $\exp(c\,e^{-2}(\log(1/e))^{-2\kappa})$ with $\kappa=6+\log107$ at fixed
visibility~\cite[Theorem~A]{DM-lamps}, and its weighted
one-round theorem bounds the entropy of a non-flat memory state through transported copies of $S_3$~\cite[Theorem~10.1]{DM-lamps}.
The companion paper~\cite{DM-game} puts Pauli pairs on the same configurations, builds the game and bounds the entropy, formation
and dimension of its near-winners. Neither paper bounds the distillable entanglement of a strategy for a game; that is what
\cref{thm:lamps-ED} adds.

\subsection{Organization}\label{sec:intro-org}

For the qualitative law, read \cref{sec:prelim-usable}, the fixed-size extraction lemma (\cref{lem:fixed-size}) and
\cref{sec:law}. The logarithmic laws need in addition the compiler and extraction tools of \cref{sec:compile,sec:extract}, the
lamp sources of \cref{sec:rates} and the coefficient family of \cref{sec:rational}. The polynomial demands are in
\cref{sec:lamps,sec:point}. \Cref{sec:exact} changes to the exact-source contract (EX) of \cref{sec:prelim-usable}.

\Cref{sec:prelim} fixes norms, the standard form, a calculus for words in a state, and the resources and contracts. Part~I, the
engine, consists of \cref{sec:count} (the qubit count of the companion), \cref{sec:transfer} (transfer and return
for linear and Boolean constraint games), \cref{sec:compile} (compilers) and \cref{sec:extract} (extraction). Part~II, the law, is
\cref{sec:law} (Theorem~A and the results around it) and \cref{sec:direction} (directions). Part~III, the spectrum, consists of
\cref{sec:transport} (the Slofstra--Vidick baseline), \cref{sec:rates} (lamp sources), \cref{sec:rational} (a dense set of
coefficients and the arithmetic of coefficients),
\cref{sec:lamps} (the configuration-lamp game), \cref{sec:point} (the point game) and \cref{sec:exact} (exact sources). Part~IV contains
the obstructions (\cref{sec:obstructions}), the conditional Bell-gap contrast (\cref{sec:boundary}), the logarithmic floor on a
class and a sufficient criterion for a counterexample (\cref{sec:floor}) and open problems (\cref{sec:open}), followed by a
discussion and an account of how the work was done. The appendices give the proof of the white-box protocol, a table of the status of
every result, reproducibility commands and computational scope, the results used from our companion papers, a tapered reservoir for the game of Ji, Leung and Vidick, the candidate
classes and method obstructions for the logarithmic floor, and, for the point window, the six-type far-commutator fillings, the fixed-holonomy subdivision estimate and an obstruction to raw cross-copy estimates.
