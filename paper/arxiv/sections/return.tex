\subsection{The return direction}\label{sec:return}

Conversely, a model that satisfies \ref{S1}--\ref{S3} at precision $e$ gives a strategy that loses with probability
$O(e^2)$, on a purification of the same state~\cite[Theorem~5.4]{DM-game}. For the flat state this is the classical direction
of the correspondence~\cite[Proposition~4.4(a)]{SV18}. The point is that the state, and with it its spectrum, is kept: the game
and the state-weighted profile then determine each other up to constant factors in the precision.

Let $\rho$ be a density matrix on $\C^d$ and $\xi=\rho^{1/2}$. The isometry $\Theta:\C^d\otimes\C^d\to M_d(\C)$ with
$\Theta(v\otimes w)=wv^T$ satisfies $\Theta((Q\otimes I)x)=\Theta(x)Q^T$ and $\Theta((I\otimes Y)x)=Y\Theta(x)$. The
\emph{canonical purification} of $\rho$ is $\psi_\rho=\Theta^{-1}(\xi)$. Bob's reduced state is $\rho$, Alice's is
$\rho^T$, and we write $\eta=\xi^T$ for its square root. So $\lVert(C\otimes I)\psi_\rho\rVert=\hs{\xi C^T}=\hs{C\eta}$ for
operators $C$ on Alice's side, and, for an operator $B$ on Bob's side,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical}
\lVert(B^T\otimes I-I\otimes B)\psi_\rho\rVert=\hs{\xi B-B\xi}=\hs{[B,\xi]} .
\end{equation}
Alice therefore imitates Bob's observable $B$ by measuring $B^T$, at a cost equal to its state commutator.

\begin{theorem}[Return; {\cite[Theorem~5.4]{DM-game}}]\label{thm:return}
Let $G=G(A,b)$ be a binary linear system game and $s=\max_i|V_i|$. Let $\varphi:F(x_1,\dots,x_n,J)\to\mathrm U(d)$ and a
density matrix $\rho$ on $\C^d$ satisfy \ref{S1}--\ref{S3} for the presentation of \cref{def:lsg} and $w=J$, at precision
$e$. Put $B_j=\mathrm r(\varphi(x_j))$. Consider the strategy on $\psi_\rho$ in which Bob, on question $j$, measures $B_j$,
and Alice, on question $i$ with $V_i=\{j_1<\dots<j_r\}$, measures the binary projective measurements
$\{\frac12(I\pm B_{j_1}^T)\},\dots,\{\frac12(I\pm B_{j_r}^T)\}$ in turn and answers the recorded signs, flipping the last one if their product is not $(-1)^{b_i}$. Every
question pair $(i,j)$ with $j\in V_i$ is lost with probability at most $C_se^2$, where
\[
\beta_l=3\Bigl(1+\frac{(l-1)(l-2)}2\Bigr)+\frac{13(l-1)}{\sqrt2},\qquad C_s=\frac14\Bigl[\beta_s^2+\Bigl(s^2+2+\sum_{l=1}^s\beta_l\Bigr)^2\Bigr].
\]
The strategy has local dimensions $d$, and Bob's reduced state is $\rho$.
\end{theorem}

The proof~\cite[Section~5.2]{DM-game} rounds the generators by \cref{lem:rounding}, bounds the commutators and the parity
of each equation in the state, and controls Alice's sequential records through the branch commutators $[K_a,\rho_A^{1/2}]$ of
her Kraus operators~\cite[Lemma~5.3]{DM-game}.

For $s=3$ the constant is $C_3\approx788.2$; it is far from optimal. Nothing is postselected: every run of Alice's
procedure produces a valid answer, and the shared state is $\psi_\rho$ itself.

\begin{corollary}[Two-sided correspondence; {\cite[Corollary~5.5]{DM-game}}]\label{cor:correspondence}
Let $G=G(A,b)$ be a binary linear system game with $\omega_q(G)=1$ and question distribution $\pi$, put
$C_G=2\max(s,4)/\sqrt{\pi_{\min}}$, and let $C_s$ be as in \cref{thm:return}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),itemsep=1pt]
\item For every $\eps>0$, $\hlp^\rho_\Gamma(J;C_G\sqrt\eps)\le\Ent(G,\eps)\le\hlp^\rho_\Gamma(J;\sqrt{\eps/C_s})$.
\item Let $h^S_\Gamma(e)$ be the infimum of $S(\rho)$ over all models satisfying \ref{S1}--\ref{S3} for $w=J$ at precision
$e$, in any dimension, and $q(\eps)$ the infimum of the entanglement entropy of pure-state strategies that win with
probability at least $1-\eps$. Then $h^S_\Gamma(C_G\sqrt\eps)\le q(\eps)\le h^S_\Gamma(\sqrt{\eps/C_s})$.
\item Let $e_\Gamma(d)$ be the infimum of the precisions $e$ with $\hlp^\rho_\Gamma(J;e)\le d$, and $\ell_G(d)$ the
infimum of the losing probabilities of strategies with $H_A=H_B=\C^d$. Then
$e_\Gamma(d)^2/C_G^2\le\ell_G(d)\le C_s\,e_\Gamma(d)^2$ for every $d$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

The state commutators in~\ref{S2} are needed for the return direction, not only for counting~\cite[Example~5.6]{DM-game}.
That flat approximate representations give strategies with quadratically small loss on a maximally entangled state is due to
Slofstra and Vidick~\cite[Proposition~4.4(a)]{SV18}, following~\cite[Proposition~3.4]{Slo19}, and Paddock develops the
correspondence between near-optimal strategies and approximate representations, including rounding to maximally entangled
strategies~\cite{Pad22}. Since both directions keep an arbitrary state, the dimension, the entanglement entropy, or any other
function of the spectrum that does not change when zero eigenvalues are added can be read off the state-weighted profile
(\cref{cor:spectrum}).
